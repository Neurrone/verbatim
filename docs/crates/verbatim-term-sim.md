# verbatim-term-sim

A simulated terminal for Verbatim's tests (`docs/design/terminal-model.md`, section 9): a VT emulator whose rows carry identities, answering the questions Verbatim's read program asks of a terminal's UI Automation provider as the console host or Windows Terminal answers them. Its answers are checked against the real terminals by the conformance expectations in its `conformance` folder. It is used by tests only, and it depends on no Windows bindings, so the platform-neutral crates' tests can use it; `cargo xtask ci`'s check of the platform-neutral crates does not cover it, since it is no product code.

## Public API

- `Terminal::new(flavor, width, height, history)` makes an empty terminal of a `Flavor` (`ConsoleHost` or `WindowsTerminal`), `width` cells by `height` rows, its history holding up to `history` rows above the screen. `Terminal::write(bytes)` writes to it as a program writes to its output.
- `Terminal::snapshot(&Query)` answers one read as the read program takes it (`verbatim_uia_rops::terminal_snapshot`), returning a `Snapshot`: the text from the read's start to the document's end, the screen's text, whether the start was used or refused, whether the read and the screen start at the document's start, the document's first row, the caret's row, its column, how many lines the caret moves to the document's end, and where the next read starts. A `Query` gives the start the last read named (`Range`), the screen's height as last found, and whether to read the whole text. A call the real provider would fail returns `Refused`.
- `Terminal::colours()` gives each screen row's stretches of one foreground and background as shown (`Run`), from the screen's top to the document's last row. `Terminal::text(range)` gives a range's text.
- `Terminal::grid()` exposes the state: the main and alternate `Buffer`s, their `Row`s with their `RowId`s, cells and styles, and the cursor. Row identities follow rows through scrolls, rotation and clears, which no real terminal exposes; the oracle's checks rest on them.
- `Terminal::output()` is the oracle's ground truth: the lines the stream has written as output so far (`OutputLine`: the text, the rows' identities, the write that finished the line, and the write during which one of its rows left the buffer, if one did), in the order they were finished. `Terminal::writes()` counts the writes.
- `oracle`: the invariants of the design's section 9 and what they are checked over. A reader under test implements `Reader`: it gives each read's `Query`, is handed the read's `Snapshot`, and answers with `Heard` (what it would say, as `Spoken` reports that each name the row their line starts on, and optionally where it thinks each row of its previous read went) or asks to read again at once (`Reply::Again`). `observe` reads a terminal once before anything is written, then writes a stream chunk by chunk and reads after each, recording each read's rows as they truly are (`Observation`, `Seen`); `check` (or `Run::violations`) lists every `Violation`. `Op` is one of the operations of section 3.2 as bytes, `stream` turns operations and read points into a corpus-shaped `Stream`, `every_stream` lists every stream of up to a given number of operations of an alphabet (`TINY_ALPHABET`), and `Size::SMALL` and `Size::TINY` are the random and exhaustive cases' terminals, with `WORDS` the random streams' words.
- `screen_rows(screen)` counts the rows of a screen's text as Verbatim counts them.
- `conformance`: the corpus (`streams()`, each a `Stream` of chunks), the expectations (`Expectation`, `Answer`, `expectation_path`), `simulate` and `replay`, which reads the simulated terminal after each chunk as the runner reads a real one and lists where it differs from an expectation, and `history(flavor)`, the history each terminal keeps by default.

## How it works

The grid (`grid.rs`) is a buffer of rows with fixed coordinates. The main buffer holds up to its capacity, the screen at its bottom; a line feed on the screen's last row moves the screen down the buffer until the buffer is full, then discards the first row so that every row's content moves up one coordinate, as the real buffers rotate. The alternate buffer is one screen tall. A scroll region whose top is the screen's top scrolls into the history as the whole screen does, the rows below it keeping their places and the new blank row coming in at the region's bottom; any other region scrolls among its own rows. Erasing the screen on the main buffer pushes its rows with text into the history and then erases every cell left on it, rows of colour without text included; erasing the history drops the rows above the screen. Erasing, inserted characters, the cells deleting characters shifts in at a row's end, rows scrolled in and the alternate screen when it is entered take the current colours, without bold, faint or reverse video; erasing never ends a wrapped line. The console host marks a row as wrapping as soon as its last column is written, and an explicit line feed, index or next line on it clears the mark, so a full row followed by anything but a line feed joins the next row in the text; a row inserted at a marked row takes the mark. Windows Terminal marks a row only when a character wraps onto the next.

The VT parser is the `vte` crate; `vt.rs` maps what it parses onto the grid: printing with deferred wrapping, which reverse index, inserting and deleting rows and cursor moves cancel; carriage return, line feed, backspace and tab; cursor moves, absolute and relative: moving up from at or below the scroll region's top stops at the top margin and moving down from at or above its bottom stops at the bottom margin, wherever the move starts, while the vertical position relative to the cursor (VPR) stops only at the screen's edge; saving and restoring the cursor, one slot for each screen, the alternate screen's kept between its visits; erasing in the line and the display; inserting and deleting rows and characters; scrolling; scroll regions, margins out of order ignored; the alternate screen (modes 47, 1047 and 1049); the whole screen in reverse video (mode 5, a visual bell's flash); the cursor's visibility (mode 25); and colours (the sixteen-colour palette as Campbell's, bold shown as the bright version of the default foreground or of a basic colour chosen with SGR 30 to 37, but not of a 256-colour index or true colour of the same value, faint shown by halving each channel of the foreground, 256 colours, true colour, and reverse video). Anything else is ignored: wide characters, which take two cells, autowrap turned off (mode 7), and the colon form of true colour are not modelled.

The ground truth (`truth.rs`) is recorded by the grid as the stream is written. A line is a row and the rows after it while each wraps onto the next. It is output when an explicit line feed (line feed, vertical tab, form feed, index or next line) on its last row moved the cursor off it on the main screen, it has text other than spaces, every change to its rows' characters until then appended (put characters where the row had only spaces to its end), and nothing changed it afterwards: no character of its rows, no row of it starting or stopping to wrap, and no row above it starting or stopping to wrap onto it, which scrolling a region beneath a wrapping row also does. So a line redrawn after a carriage return, an erased row written again, a redrawn region, and a footer are never output, and a line that changes later drops out of the list. Moving changes nothing: a line scrolled into the history or pushed there by erasing the screen is the same line. A row leaving the buffer (rotation out of a full history, erasing the history, deletion, or scrolling out of a region) is not a change; the line records the write it left in. A line whose first row continued a row that had already left is not output.

The oracle (`oracle.rs`) checks four invariants against that truth:

- Every output line some read found finished (on the main screen, at or after the write that finished it, before one of its rows left) is reported exactly once, as a line or in a summary, and in order (`Missed`, `Repeated`, `OutOfOrder`). Lines no read found finished are exempt: those that left a full history, or scrolled out of a region, between two reads. The order is the order reads found the lines, a line counting from the first read that found it finished or from the read that reported it if that came first, and lines of one read from the top, since a reader sees positions, not the order of writing. A line reported while it was still being written must add up, with what it gained later, to its text as first found finished (`Incomplete`). Resizing is not modelled, so the design's exemption for lines written in the same read as a resize does not arise.
- Everything reported is text the read shows: a line exactly the line at the row it names, growth and a redrawn row's speech part of that line, and every report naming the first row of a line (`Misquoted`, `NotALine`).
- For every row with text unchanged between two reads and on no other row of either, a reader that reports where rows went maps it to the row it really became (`Mismapped`).
- Padding is never reported: a report that matches the read only with the spaces after a line's end is `Padded`.

Reports of rows that are not output, such as a footer's first appearance or a redrawn row's change, are allowed by these invariants; what they say is decided speech, tested by unit tests of the reading core.

The provider (`provider.rs`) answers as the real providers do, each rule measured by the conformance expectations:

- Positions are a row's coordinate and a column; a position anywhere in the allocated buffer is accepted, unwritten rows reading as blank, and one past it is refused, so a range kept on the main screen reads the alternate screen while its row is within the alternate screen's height.
- The document ends at the end of the lower of the last row with text and the cursor's row. In the console host a row marked as wrapping counts as a row with text, so a whole row of spaces written without a line feed, or such a row erased, keeps the document from ending above it.
- The buffer's last cell reads in the colours the next character would be written in, whatever it holds: the alternate screen's last cell, and the main screen's once the history is full.
- Rows are padded to the width and joined by line breaks, none between a row and the row its line wraps onto.
- The console host's ranges end before a row's line break, so an expanded line, the screen and the document end without one. Windows Terminal's end after it, except on the buffer's last row, which has no row after it for the break: there the range ends at the width, but its text leaves out the row's last cell. That is the alternate screen's last row, and the main screen's once the history is full.
- Moving by lines stops at each row's start and at the document's end, so the last step forward goes from the last row's start to its end.
- Expanding a position past the document's last row gives that row.

## Conformance

`cargo xtask regenerate-terminal-conformance-expectations [--stream <name>]... [--flavor console-host|windows-terminal]...` (`xtask/src/conformance.rs`) writes each stream of `conformance/streams` into the real console host and the end-to-end suite's pinned Windows Terminal, 120 by 30. It reads each after every chunk with `verbatim_uia_rops::terminal_snapshot`, the caret classically, and every screen cell's colours, and writes `conformance/expected/<stream>.<flavor>.json`. It opens terminal windows, so it runs on demand on a desktop, never in continuous integration: when a stream is added, when the simulator's behaviour is changed, and when Windows or Windows Terminal updates. The changed expectation files are reviewed in their commit.

The terminal runs the same executable's `conformance-player`, which connects back over a local socket, writes each chunk it is sent to its console, and then sets the window's title to a token naming the chunk. A terminal changes its title only once it has parsed everything written before, and Windows Terminal parses it itself, where a cursor-position query could be answered by the pseudoconsole in front of it; so the runner waits for the token in the title before it reads. The console host is started through `cmd /c start`, since given any standard handles it serves them as a pseudoconsole instead of opening a window; the player opens `CONOUT$` itself for the same reason, and sets the console mode programs run with (processed output, wrapping at the end of a line, VT processing, and a line feed that does not return the carriage, which is why the simulator's line feed never does).

Colours are read cell by cell: over a stretch of two colours both providers answer 0 rather than "mixed", and their format unit does not stop at every change of colour. A read that falls back from the remote program to the classic one fails the run rather than being recorded.

The expectations depend on the terminals' settings on the machine that recorded them: the default history (9,001 rows in all in the console host, 9,001 above the screen in Windows Terminal), the Campbell colour scheme and the 120 by 30 size.

`cargo xtask record-terminal-streams [--recipe <name>]...` (`xtask/src/record.rs`) adds recorded programs to the corpus. Each recipe in `conformance/recipes` names a command and the input to type into it; the program runs in a pseudoconsole of 120 by 30, and what it writes is saved with when it came (`Stream::at_ms`), split into chunks where the output paused for 30 milliseconds or input was typed. The pseudoconsole's own requests to its terminal, programs' questions to the terminal (whose answers would arrive in the player's input) and title changes are left out. The recordings are bash with readline from Git for Windows, vim, less, Windows PowerShell with PSReadLine 2.0, Claude Code (an Ink program; nothing is sent), Pi in its full-screen and regular modes, and a script drawing progress the ways programs do.

## Tests

- `src/tests.rs`: the emulator's own behaviour, one test per operation: padding and the document's end, wrapping, rotation under fixed coordinates, row identity through a scroll, a kept range on the alternate screen, a kept start, reverse video, and Windows Terminal's line breaks.
- `src/tests.rs` also pins the ground truth: a finished line and one still being written, a wrapped line over its rows, a line rewritten after a carriage return, a row erased and written again, a line changed after its line feed, the write a line left a full history in, and a line on the alternate screen.
- `tests/oracle.rs`: the oracle against readers whose faults are known. A reader that speaks every row differing from the same place in the previous read is caught repeating and mismapping the rows a scroll moved, and missing the lines a flood pushed into the history; a reader that diffs the lines of whole texts by a longest common subsequence, reading from the kept start as the read program does, is caught taking an output line for the footer that showed its text, and missing the lines a flood rotated above its kept start. Each is asserted with its exact violations. A reader that reports from the ground truth passes those streams, 512 random streams of up to 40 operations with random read points on `Size::SMALL` in either terminal (`proptest`, with a fixed seed so every run checks the same streams), every stream of up to four operations of `TINY_ALPHABET` on `Size::TINY` in both terminals, and the conformance corpus.
- `tests/conformance.rs`: every stream of the corpus replayed and compared field by field with both terminals' expectations, failing with each differing field's first difference. The corpus holds hand-written streams, one per kind of operation; streams that pin rules found in review (a pending wrap cancelled, cursor moves inside margins and from outside them, the saved cursor across screens, erasing in a background colour, deleting characters and entering the alternate screen in one, erasing while bold, wrap marks through erasing and through index and next line, bold over each kind of colour, faint, Windows Terminal's last cell, the colours of the buffer's last cell, and rows of spaces at the document's end); and the recordings.

The read program's own behaviour, both paths, is tested in `mockapp` (`crates/mockapp/tests/terminal_snapshot.rs`).
