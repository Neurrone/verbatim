# verbatim-term-sim

A simulated terminal for Verbatim's tests (`docs/design/terminal-model.md`, "Testing"): a VT emulator whose rows carry identities, answering the questions Verbatim's read program asks of a terminal's UI Automation provider as the console host or Windows Terminal answers them. Its answers are checked against the real terminals by the conformance expectations in its `conformance` folder. It is used by tests only, and it depends on no Windows bindings, so the platform-neutral crates' tests can use it; `cargo xtask ci`'s check of the platform-neutral crates does not cover it, since it is no product code.

## Public API

- `Terminal::new(flavor, width, height, history)` makes an empty terminal of a `Flavor` (`ConsoleHost` or `WindowsTerminal`), `width` cells by `height` rows, its history holding up to `history` rows above the screen. `Terminal::write(bytes)` writes to it as a program writes to its output.
- `Terminal::snapshot(&Query)` answers one read as the read program takes it (`verbatim_uia_rops::terminal_snapshot`), returning a `Snapshot`: the text from the read's start to the document's end, the screen's text, whether the start was used or refused, whether the read and the screen start at the document's start, the document's first row, the caret's row, its column, how many lines the caret moves to the document's end, where the next read starts, and how many rows the read holds above its start. A `Query` gives the start the last read named (`Range`), the screen's height as last found, whether to read the whole text, and how many rows above the start to begin the read (`above`), the history rows whose place shows how far a full history rotated. A call the real provider would fail returns `Refused`.
- `Terminal::colours()` gives each screen row's stretches of one foreground and background as shown (`Run`), from the screen's top to the document's last row. `Terminal::program_colours()` gives the same as Verbatim's read program reads them (`verbatim-uia-rops`'s colour read): each row checked whole, a row of several colours in chunks of 16 cells, a chunk of several cell by cell, every check answered as the providers answer a colour over a stretch, from every cell but its last, and Windows Terminal's last row of the buffer, which gives moves by characters a cell fewer than the width, read cell by cell with its last chunk. `Terminal::text(range)` gives a range's text.
- `Terminal::grid()` exposes the state: the main and alternate `Buffer`s, their `Row`s with their `RowId`s, cells and styles, the cursor, whether the main buffer's first row continues a line whose start left it (`Buffer::continued`), and the rows the full main buffer rotated out with their text (`Buffer::rotated`), and how many rows it erased from its front with the history (`Buffer::erased`). Row identities follow rows through scrolls, rotation and clears, which no real terminal exposes; the oracle uses them only to know whether a held row left the buffer.
- `Terminal::writes()` counts the writes.
- `oracle`: the invariants of the design's "Testing" and what they are checked over. A reader under test implements `Reader`: it gives each read's `Query`, is handed the read's `Snapshot`, and answers with `Heard` (what it would say, as `Spoken` reports that each name the row their line starts on; the lines it withholds, each a `Withheld` naming the row and a `Reason`; and optionally where it thinks each row of its previous read went and which row its review position is on, counted from the read's first row and negative above it in the history; and the listed limits it states at that read, each a `Limit`) or asks to read again at once (`Reply::Again`), as a reader reads the whole document, at most `AGAIN_LIMIT` (one) time at one point; `Reader::quiet` tells it that the quiet period has passed before the last read. `observe` reads a terminal once before anything is written, then writes a stream chunk by chunk and reads after each, then tells the reader the quiet period has passed and reads once more, recording each read's rows and where they start, the main buffer's rows, where its screen starts, whether its first row continues a line whose start left it, the rows its full history rotated out since the read before, and how many rows it erased with the history since then, as they truly are (`Observation`, `Seen`); `check` (or `Run::violations`) lists every `Violation`. `Op` is one of the operations of the design's "The model" as bytes, `stream` turns operations and read points into a corpus-shaped `Stream`, `every_stream` lists every stream of up to a given number of operations of an alphabet (`TINY_ALPHABET`), and `Size::SMALL` and `Size::TINY` are the random and exhaustive cases' terminals, with `WORDS` the random streams' words, `STATUSES` the status rows their footers and headers draw (a spinner whose symbol alone changes and a timer that ticks), and `PIECES` the text they write split inside a character's encoding. Besides writing, moving and erasing, the operations include the ways programs stream text that `verbatim-stream-test` records: bytes that end or start inside a character, a row rewritten after erasing it, Ink's erasing of its region upward, a partly written word moved down to the next row, a header drawn above the output, a row inserted above a footer by deleting the screen's first row, the screen erased and painted again, and a row above the cursor restyled in bold. `random_op`, `random_steps` and `random_config` generate the random streams with `proptest`, so every crate's tests check the same ones: an operation of `Op` with its weight and arguments for `Size::SMALL`, the steps of a stream (1 to 39 operations, each followed by a read or not), and the configuration, every field written out, with a fixed seed, no failure files and nothing taken from the environment.
- `screen_rows(screen)` counts the rows of a screen's text as Verbatim counts them.
- `conformance`: the corpus (`streams()`, each a `Stream` of chunks, each `Chunk` the bytes a program wrote at once, kept in its file as text or, when it is not text on its own, as when it ends or starts inside a character's encoding, as `{"hex": ...}`; `inside_character` says after which chunks the stream's bytes end inside a character), the expectations (`Expectation`, `Answer`, `expectation_path`), `simulate` and `replay`, which reads the simulated terminal after each chunk as the runner reads a real one and lists where it differs from an expectation, and `history(flavor)`, the history each terminal keeps by default.

## How it works

The grid (`grid.rs`) is a buffer of rows with fixed coordinates. The main buffer holds up to its capacity, the screen at its bottom; a line feed on the screen's last row moves the screen down the buffer until the buffer is full, then discards the first row so that every row's content moves up one coordinate, as the real buffers rotate. The alternate buffer is one screen tall. A scroll region whose top is the screen's top scrolls into the history as the whole screen does, the rows below it keeping their places and the new blank row coming in at the region's bottom; any other region scrolls among its own rows, each row keeping its mark that it wraps, and the blank row that comes in at its edge taking the mark its place had, so a row filled to its end at a region's bottom leaves the row a wrap scrolls in marked too. Erasing the screen on the main buffer pushes its rows with text into the history and then erases every cell left on it, rows of colour without text included; erasing the history drops the rows above the screen. Erasing, inserted characters, the cells deleting characters shifts in at a row's end, rows scrolled in and the alternate screen when it is entered take the current colours, without bold, faint or reverse video; erasing never ends a wrapped line. The console host marks a row as wrapping as soon as its last column is written, and an explicit line feed, index or next line on it clears the mark, so a full row followed by anything but a line feed joins the next row in the text; a row inserted at a marked row takes the mark. Windows Terminal marks a row only when a character wraps onto the next.

The VT parser is the `vte` crate; `vt.rs` maps what it parses onto the grid: printing with deferred wrapping, which reverse index, inserting and deleting rows and cursor moves cancel; carriage return, line feed, backspace and tab; cursor moves, absolute and relative: moving up from at or below the scroll region's top stops at the top margin and moving down from at or above its bottom stops at the bottom margin, wherever the move starts, while the vertical position relative to the cursor (VPR) stops only at the screen's edge; saving and restoring the cursor, one slot for each screen, the alternate screen's kept between its visits; erasing in the line and the display; inserting and deleting rows and characters; scrolling; scroll regions, margins out of order ignored; the alternate screen (mode 1049; modes 47 and 1047 switch no screen in either terminal, which ignore them); the whole screen in reverse video (mode 5, a visual bell's flash); the cursor's visibility (mode 25); and colours (the sixteen-colour palette as Campbell's, bold shown as the bright version of the default foreground or of a basic colour chosen with SGR 30 to 37, but not of a 256-colour index or true colour of the same value, faint shown by halving each channel of the foreground, 256 colours, true colour, and reverse video). Anything else is ignored: wide characters, which take two cells, autowrap turned off (mode 7), and the colon form of true colour are not modelled.


The oracle (`oracle.rs`) does not decide what must be heard, which is the speech rules' business; it checks that every silence has a true reason, judging by text, as a reader can observe it, never by the hidden identity of the row a program wrote to (the design's "Testing"). A line of a read is a row of the main buffer that the row above does not wrap onto and the rows after it while each wraps onto the next, its text their text joined with the padding after its end left out; only lines with text count. The main screen of a read is the main buffer from its first row to the document's end, history included, whatever the reader's own read covered. A line is new when no earlier read showed a line with its text anywhere on the main screen or in what the read showed of the alternate screen, which a reader cannot always tell from the main one (the design's "Screens"), the rows of only spaces at its start left out (the console host joins a row filled with spaces to the line below). The last read a read is judged against, for growth, vanished lines and the premises, is the last read on the main screen, or the last on the alternate screen when no read showed the switch to it, since the main screen had no history; what that alternate read showed counts as heard. A line whose start left a full history is new only when its text ends no line shown before; a line whose start is above the read's first row is reported by naming the read's first row with the text of its part in the read, and is new or not by its whole text. A line's place is its first row's row counted from the screen's top.

A reason is accepted when any explanation consistent with what the reads showed makes it true (the design's principle 5). Rows of the history keep their text and move only by the rotation, while a program may write any text on any row of the screen and move the screen's rows anywhere, so an explanation is a rotation that keeps every row the last read showed above its screen where the read now shows it, with its text, and any pairing of the screen's rows. The smallest such rotation, found by matching the last read's history rows against the buffer, places a line at the last read: its coordinate now plus that rotation. The premises that speak of a line's row, and what was reported of a line that is not new, compare it with the line its row was in at the last read on the main screen, by ground truth of where rows went rather than a row's identity: a row the buffer held then is followed to where it was, its coordinate moved down by the rows the buffer discarded from its front since (rotated out of a full history or erased with the history), as a reader follows it by the coordinates the providers report and the rotation it measures, so it stays with its row as the history grows, rotates or is erased; a row written since is compared with the line at its place, since a reader sees only the screen there. A line is also followed through a move with its neighbours, judged by text alone, as a reader's alignment finds a block of rows moved by the same distance: a row with text moved when its text is on exactly one row of the main screen at both reads, at coordinates other than following by the history alone gives. A line whose first row moved is followed to where that row was; a line whose place by the history alone had no text at the last read, or text that moved, and whose nearest row with text above it, or below its last row, over rows blank in both reads, moved, is followed by the same distance when a line with text started there at the last read and the rows between were blank then too, as a footer's status row that changed as output above it pushed the footer down a row, or a row that changed while the rows of a scroll region moved up. A line rewritten where it was is never followed by its neighbours' move, so a header that stays while the scroll region below it moves stays put. Where a line changed from one text to another moves with it. A line of the last read on the main screen that the smallest rotation does not take out of the buffer, and that is no longer shown as often, has vanished: under some pairing it is the row a new line was written over. Each vanished line justifies one change (a redrawn row's speech, a spinner, an unfinished word or a volatile row), matched one to one with the most claims justified. The invariants:

- Every silence is accounted for (`Missed`, `FalseReason`). Every new line is, at that read, reported with its new text or withheld with a reason whose premise is true. A line says its text whole, or its part in the read. Growth says it when what was reported of the line it grew from, with the growth, is the whole line; a line withheld as a spinner or as having lost text counts as reported whole, and a line whose start left the buffer grows from the longest end of a line reported whole that begins it. A redrawn row's speech says it when it covers every character that differs from a vanished line's text, so a row that had no text, or one letter of a changed word, says nothing new. A summary counts it. A reason given for a line that is not new is not checked, and nothing is checked on the alternate screen. Lines written and erased between two reads, and lines that left a full history or a scroll region between two reads, are never shown to a read, so nothing accounts for them; resizing is not modelled, so the design's exemption for output written in the same read as a resize does not arise. A reader that states `Limit::BlankScreen` at a read whose screen, main or alternate, shows no text, a program's clear taken as one before its reprint, need not report that read's new lines there; the next read is judged against the read before the blank one, and the lines the blank screen hid are judged as new at the first later read whose screen shows text; stated where the screen shows text, it is `FalseLimit`.
- A line is reported once while its text stays on the screen (`Repeated`): a line or a summary's count repeats when as many lines with its text were reported, at reads that each showed at least that many, as the read shows, so a row changed back and spoken again is not a repeat; a line of the last read that the full history rotated out since, or that was erased with the history, no longer counts, so a line printed again after its text left the buffer may be reported. While the alternate screen is up, the main screen's lines are on the screen only as often as the alternate screen shows their text. Among the new lines of one read, those reported as lines or in summaries are reported top to bottom (`OutOfOrder`).
- Everything reported is text the read shows: a line exactly the line, or its part in the read, at the row it names, growth and a redrawn row's speech part of that line, and every report, every row a summary counts and every row withheld naming the first row of a line of the read or the read's first row (`Misquoted`, `NotALine`).
- For every row on the screen whose text is on exactly one row of each of two reads in a row, a reader that reports where rows went maps it to the row with that text (`Mismapped`). A row's text keeps the spaces at the end of a row that wraps onto a row with text, but not the padding after its line's last character (the design's "Rows").
- Padding is never reported: a report that matches the read only with the spaces after a line's end is `Padded`.
- A reader that reports a review position keeps it on its text: when the text of the row it was on is on exactly one row of that read and of the next, on the screen in both, it is on that row (`ReviewMoved`), and it is always a row of the document: of the read, or of the history above it down to the document's first row (`ReviewOutside`). The streams press no keys and nothing follows the caret.

The mapping and the review position are not judged across a switch of screens: the alternate screen opening or closing, or what a reader cannot tell from one (the design's "Screens"), the history going away so that the screen starts at the document's start, and its coming back after that.

Each reason stands for a silence of the design's rules, or for a case it lists under "Limits", and the oracle checks its premise and nothing more. Two are holds, which the same or a later read must resolve: by a report that says the held line's text (a line, a summary's count, growth that completes it, or a redrawn row's speech covering its change from the text its place had before the hold), or by the held text being gone from the main screen, written over, erased or deleted, or by the alternate screen coming up without it, or by a held line spoken as it was last read (`Spoken::Held`, which names no row, as a reader speaks a held line the rotation carried above its read), whose text ends the held text; with no hold to resolve, that speech must end a line a read showed, or it is `Unheld`. The row rotated out of a full history with its text, or a report merely naming the row, resolves nothing. The stream's end is the last read, which `observe` takes after telling the reader the quiet period has passed with nothing more written, so a hold still unresolved after it is false.

- `Volatile`, a hold: a volatile row is not spoken while it changes and is spoken once it settles ("Volatile rows"). Premise: the row changed at an earlier read and changes again under some consistent pairing: a vanished line's text is one that appeared at an earlier read on the main screen while a line vanished there, so that it could be that line's row changed; each vanished line justifies one claim.
- `UnfinishedWord`, a hold: a growing line's unfinished last word waits for the line to grow past it, the cursor to leave, or the quiet period ("Growing lines"). Premise: a vanished line's text begins this one's and is shorter.
- `HeldForKeys`, a hold: a row a key changed away from the caret waits while keys keep coming ("Held for keys"). Premise: a key was pressed. The streams press none, so it is never true yet.
- `Spinner`: a spinner's symbol replaced alone is silent ("Changed rows"). Premise: a vanished line's text differs from this one in exactly one character, neither the old nor the new one a letter or a digit, so a counter's tick is heard.
- `LostText`: a row that only lost text is silent, as a list entry that lost its marker or a wrapped line whose last row was erased ("Changed rows"). Premise: compared without spaces, the line covering its row at the last read on the main screen, placed by the smallest consistent rotation, or a vanished line, as when a line is split in two, contains this one's text and is longer. What was reported of each such longer line is what was said of this one, kept while a line with its text is shown: when the line gains text again, the text it gets back is not new, so a redrawn row's speech, or growth that completes the line from what was said of it, says the line's new text when it says what the line gained beyond what was said of it.
- `WrittenBack`, a listed limit: text erased and written back within the time a gone row lingers is silent wherever it comes back, as a wrapped line's last row printed again as a line of its own. Precondition: compared without spaces, the line's text is part of a line an earlier read showed on the main screen that vanished at a read since. Under the same limit, a redrawn row's speech of a new line says it when it covers the line's change from a line that vanished at an earlier read, as text written back with a change.

A reader states the listed limits it applies at a read (`Limit`), and the oracle accepts a violation through one only when the limit's precondition holds in the ground truth: `BlankScreen`, a read whose screen shows no row with text, which changes nothing, the next read judged against the read before it and a hold that only such reads follow left to the reader's timer; `RepeatedText`, a missed, repeated or misplaced line or a moved review position when the buffer moved since the last read and the last read's rows with text fit the main screen equally well at more than one place, or the line's text is not unique there; `LostRead`, a missed line above the screen when at least a screen's rows left the buffer since the last read; and `RefusedScreen`, a missed line above the screen of a new screen whose start was refused, when no row with text of the last read's screen and history block is still in the buffer with that text. When no read shows a switch to the alternate screen, a line changed there counts as a change of a row, since a reader cannot tell the screens apart.

What redrawn rows, keys, lists and colours say is decided speech, tested by unit tests of the reading core, not here.

The provider (`provider.rs`) answers as the real providers do, each rule measured by the conformance expectations:

- Positions are a row's coordinate and a column; a position anywhere in the allocated buffer is accepted, unwritten rows reading as blank, and one past it is refused, so a range kept on the main screen reads the alternate screen while its row is within the alternate screen's height.
- The document ends at the end of the lower of the last row with text and the cursor's row. In the console host a row marked as wrapping counts as a row with text, so a whole row of spaces written without a line feed, or such a row erased, keeps the document from ending above it.
- The buffer's last cell reads in the colours the next character would be written in, whatever it holds: the alternate screen's last cell, and the main screen's once the history is full.
- Rows are padded to the width and joined by line breaks, none between a row and the row its line wraps onto.
- The console host's ranges end before a row's line break, so an expanded line, the screen and the document end without one. Windows Terminal's end after it, except on the buffer's last row, which has no row after it for the break: there the range ends at the width, but its text leaves out the row's last cell. That is the alternate screen's last row, and the main screen's once the history is full.
- Moving by lines stops at each row's start and at the document's end, so the last step forward goes from the last row's start to its end.
- Expanding a position past the document's last row gives that row.
- A read asked to start rows above its start moves up by lines, which stop at each row's start and at the document's start, and says how many it moved.

## Conformance

`cargo xtask regenerate-terminal-conformance-expectations [--stream <name>]... [--flavor console-host|windows-terminal]...` (`xtask/src/conformance.rs`) writes each stream of `conformance/streams` into the real console host and the end-to-end suite's pinned Windows Terminal, 120 by 30. It reads each after every chunk with `verbatim_uia_rops::terminal_snapshot`, the caret classically, and every screen cell's colours, and writes `conformance/expected/<stream>.<flavor>.json`. It opens terminal windows, so it runs on demand on a desktop, never in continuous integration: when a stream is added, when the simulator's behaviour is changed, and when Windows or Windows Terminal updates. The changed expectation files are reviewed in their commit.

The terminal runs the same executable's `conformance-player`, which connects back over a local socket, writes each chunk it is sent to its console, and then sets the window's title to a token naming the chunk, with a title sequence written after the chunk, or, after a chunk that ends inside a character, whose first bytes the terminal holds and shows nothing for, with the console's title call, since a sequence would end the character. A terminal changes its title only once it has parsed everything written before, and Windows Terminal parses it itself, where a cursor-position query could be answered by the pseudoconsole in front of it; so the runner waits for the token in the title before it reads. The console host is started through `cmd /c start`, since given any standard handles it serves them as a pseudoconsole instead of opening a window; the player opens `CONOUT$` itself for the same reason, and sets the console mode programs run with (processed output, wrapping at the end of a line, VT processing, and a line feed that does not return the carriage, which is why the simulator's line feed never does).

Colours are recorded cell by cell, the reference nothing about the providers' answers over stretches can disturb: a stretch answers its first cell's colour when every cell but its last has it, and "mixed" otherwise, and the format unit stops only at rows (measured 2026-10-24). Every read also takes the read program's colours, at the width the last read found, and the run fails when they differ from the cells'. A read that falls back from the remote program to the classic one fails the run rather than being recorded.

The expectations depend on the terminals' settings on the machine that recorded them: the default history (9,001 rows in all in the console host, 9,001 above the screen in Windows Terminal), the Campbell colour scheme and the 120 by 30 size.

`cargo xtask record-terminal-streams [--recipe <name>]...` (`xtask/src/record.rs`) adds recorded programs to the corpus. Each recipe in `conformance/recipes` names a command and the input to type into it; the program runs in a pseudoconsole of 120 by 30, and what it writes is saved with when it came (`Stream::at_ms`), split into chunks where the output paused for 30 milliseconds or input was typed. A recipe with `write_log` is saved instead from the program's own log of its writes, one chunk a write with its time, since the pseudoconsole's rendering merges and redraws what the program wrote; its command names the log as `{write_log}` and the folder the recorder was built into as `{bin}`, where `verbatim-stream-test` is built too (`cargo build -p verbatim-test-console` first). The pseudoconsole's own requests to its terminal, programs' questions to the terminal (whose answers would arrive in the player's input) and title changes are left out. The recordings are bash with readline from Git for Windows, vim, less, Windows PowerShell with PSReadLine 2.0, Claude Code (an Ink program) with nothing sent and, in `claude-code-reply`, with its screen reader mode off, a question sent and its reply drawn under its spinner, Pi in its full-screen and regular modes and, in `pi-changed-above`, its regular mode clearing and printing everything again when Control+O changes what is above the screen, a cargo check whose progress bar is redrawn below about a hundred lines of output, a script drawing progress the ways programs do, and `verbatim-stream-test`'s phases (`stream-<way>-<placement>`, with `-alternate` for repainting on the alternate screen): each way of streaming at 60 frames a second beside its status row as a footer, a header and a row between the output and the prompt, 26 recordings of 39 to 54 chunks, each of about 500 to 700 kilobytes of expectations a terminal.

## Tests

- `src/tests.rs`: the emulator's own behaviour, one test per operation: padding and the document's end, wrapping, rotation under fixed coordinates, row identity through a scroll, a kept range on the alternate screen, a kept start, reverse video, and Windows Terminal's line breaks.
- `tests/oracle.rs`: the oracle against readers whose faults are known, each asserted with its exact violations. A reader that speaks every row differing from the same place in the previous read, with its review position on the read's last row, is caught on a scroll repeating the lines it moved, mismapping its rows and moving its review position, and on a flood missing the lines pushed into the history; it passes a row changed from "cat" to "dog" and back, each spoken as a line. A reader that diffs the lines of whole texts by a longest common subsequence, reading from the kept start as the read program does, is caught on the flood missing the lines rotated above its kept start. A silent reader is caught on plain output missing every line, on a line longer than the screen, and on the corpus's Ink stream missing the first frame, the line streamed over the frame's first row and the frame's changed cost, but not the frame redrawn on the same rows, drawn a row lower or drawn again after a read found it erased. A reader whose summary counts a wrapped line's second row and a row past the read is caught naming them, and repeating the line at the last read. A reader that reports what each test scripts, or withholds every changed row for one reason, is caught on a false premise of each reason and passes a true one: a spinner claimed for a changed word or a counter's tick from "5s" to "6s", beside its unchanged line, or twice for one vanished line; lost text claimed for a row that gained or changed text or lost only a space, or for a row the rotation moved up to where a longer row was, and accepted for a list entry that lost its marker, a line split in two, a row that lost text while its old text is shown elsewhere, and a row that lost text as the full history rotates, followed up by the rotation; a volatile hold claimed for a row that had not changed before or was blank, or for a row the rotation moved up, or the history's growth brought, to where another row had changed, never resolved, resolved only by a redrawn row's speech that names the row, or whose row a flood rotated out of the buffer, and one accepted when replaced, spoken once settled or at the same read, deleted, covered by the alternate screen, or rewritten as the full history rotates or grows, followed to where it was, or as a row inserted above it pushes it down with the row below it, followed through the move, and caught when it was drawn a row lower alone; an unfinished word claimed for a line that did not grow, beside the line it extends, twice for one line that grew, or never spoken, and accepted when the line grows past it or it is spoken after the quiet period; growth is judged against what was said of the line itself after the full history rotates, so growth that completes a held line moved up by the rotation is accepted, and growth said of a whole line moved into the held line's place resolves nothing; a row held for keys with no key pressed; lines a blank screen hid reported once text appears, and missed when they are not; a blank screen stated where the screen shows text; text written back accepted, and text never erased caught; and the reader that holds every line of a flood as volatile. A redrawn row's speech is accepted when it says what changed and caught when it says one letter of a changed word or speaks a row that had no text. A line the alternate screen showed, with no history on the main screen, is not new when printed again on the main screen, and a line growing from it is reported by its growth; a line printed again after its text left the full history, or was erased with the history, may be reported again; and a held line spoken as it was last read after a flood pushed it out of the full history resolves its hold when what it says covers the held change, and not when it says less, while speech no hold or line ends is `Unheld`. A line repeating a text on the screen may be reported once more but not twice, a line that lost text grows from its shorter text, and one that gains its lost text back and more is reported by speech of what it gained beyond what was said of it, but not by speech of the text it got back, a line joined to a row of spaces is not new, a line whose start left the history is not new and grows from what is left, a review position is not judged where the history was erased, and one above the document's first row or below the read is outside it. A reader that reports by text passes the fixed streams, 512 random streams of 1 to 39 operations with random read points on `Size::SMALL` in either terminal (`random_steps` under `random_config`, so every run checks the same streams), every stream of up to four operations of `TINY_ALPHABET` on `Size::TINY` in both terminals, and the conformance corpus. These tests run in continuous integration; in a debug build on Dickson's x64 machine, `cargo test -p verbatim-term-sim --test oracle` runs its tests in about 17 seconds once built.
- `tests/conformance.rs`: every stream of the corpus replayed and compared field by field with both terminals' expectations, failing with each differing field's first difference. The corpus holds hand-written streams, one per kind of operation; streams that pin rules found in review (a pending wrap cancelled, cursor moves inside margins and from outside them, the saved cursor across screens, erasing in a background colour, deleting characters and entering the alternate screen in one, modes 47 and 1047, erasing while bold, wrap marks through erasing and through index and next line, bold over each kind of colour, faint, Windows Terminal's last cell, the colours of the buffer's last cell, and rows of spaces at the document's end); and the recordings. A second test replays every stream with more than one colour on a row and compares the colours as the read program reads them (`Terminal::program_colours`) with the colours the real terminals showed cell by cell.

The read program's own behaviour, both paths, is tested in `mockapp` (`crates/mockapp/tests/terminal_snapshot.rs`).
