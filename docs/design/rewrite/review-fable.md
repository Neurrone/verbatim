# Adversarial review of design-v2 (Fable, 2026-11-28)

Scope: `docs/design/rewrite/design.md` read whole, checked against today's code at 671aa993, NVDA's pinned source under `nvda/`, the slice design, features A, the pattern audit and the oracle experiments' saved outputs. Nothing was built or run. "v2" below is design-v2. Line numbers are v2's unless a file is named.

Assessment: the skeleton's direction survives, but its three central claims (one ending per request, scopes released exactly at their owner's end, three hooks are enough) each break on a sequence below, and the verification pipeline as written passes in several cases where Verbatim is wrong. None of these contradicts a ruling of Dickson's; most need a state or a rule the design does not yet have.

## Must-fix

### M1. A request cancelled while its job runs leaves a standing request and its elements with no owner

v2 line 259: a `Cancel` with any reason but the foreground stop "ends at once in Core, the worker runs on, and its late answer is dropped by its number". Line 301 makes the due time and every end cause such a cancel. The focus read's batch opens a `Follow` inside it and mints the line into it (`MintInto::Standing`, line 97); the slice design records the follow's number in Core's `Out` when the batch is sent (`design.md` line 296).

Sequence: the front focus entry waits at `focus.walk` with batch B out, holding `Follow { n2 }`. A pending focus arrives, or B's due time passes. Core resolves the ticket and drops B and n2 from `Out`. The outpost has B `Running`; a job cannot be interrupted (line 255). The `Cancel` naming n2 finds no request n2, because the step that opens it has not completed. The job completes: the root creates `Following` n2, with its UI Automation registration and the whole line minted into its scope. Core holds no record of n2, so every later item is "dropped as late", for the outpost's life, and the elements are never released.

The same shape for a record's scope: a say-all read out with `MintInto::Standing(K)`; a key ends say-all, which cancels K; the running job then mints positions into a `Keep` that no longer exists.

The reverse crossing breaks too: the outpost has already sent B's `Final` when the `Cancel` arrives. Core drops the answer as late. The `Cancel` for B names a finished request, and n2 stays open unless the `Cancel` also names n2 and the outpost acts on it.

The outpost's `Request` enum (line 254) has no variant for "cancelled while running", and the design has no rule for minting into an ended scope. This breaks "every id a reader mints belongs to at least one scope" (147) and the quiet-world leak invariant (428). The simulator would find it, but the fix is a state the design must count: either a cancelled-running variant whose completion releases what it minted and never opens its standing requests, or Core keeping the inner numbers until the batch's own ending.

A hung variant has no stated answer at all: the `Follow` step's registration is made, a later step's call hangs, the foreground stop abandons the worker. Line 254's `Running { batch, slot, gen }` has one slot, yet registration belongs to the registering worker (245). Whether the registration made before the hang is ever removed is not stated.

### M2. Three hooks cannot express what today's queue does to waiting entries

Checked against `crates/verbatim-core/src/event_queue/mod.rs` 259 to 486 and `inbox.rs`.

- A waiting entry whose reader ends. Today `inbox.rs` 618 to 631 drops every waiting entry whose told token the ended listener generation kept. In v2 a peer's end "ends every scope it held, in Core" (153), but `Entry` (310 to 314) has no way to be told, and an entry scope or item scope has no ticket. Sequence: the listener restarts with focus entries waiting; one reaches the front and asks the new listener with a token of the old one. Either the debug assertion at 173 fires, or, if tokens are not namespaced by watch number (v2 does not say; today's are paired with the listener generation, `entries.rs` 543 to 549), the new listener resolves the number to a different element and Core speaks it. The same holds for a waiting caret key whose adopted node's outpost generation ends. "One answer path per request" does not cover these, because they are holds, not requests; the design needs a general rule that a waiting entry whose scope ended is ended, with its effect.
- A waiting entry ended at a foreground change has an effect today. `entries.rs` 690 to 698: a held caret key ended while waiting passes its stroke, in press order, and tells the terminal. v2's `foreground_changed(&self, f) -> Keep` returns only keep or end, with no access to `Keys`. The pattern audit itself says "keep ended_at_foreground separate" (`patterns.md`, "Queue hooks"). As designed the key is either swallowed or waits for the 150 ms release.
- End causes are edges, but two of today's three judges are levels. v2 line 351: a cause raised while the front has no request out "is not stored". Sequence: a caret key waits behind a focus entry; a later key arrives (`LaterKey` raised, front is the focus entry, nothing happens); the caret key reaches the front and opens `caret.watch`; nothing withdraws it, and it runs to its bound. NVDA checks the level at every poll (`nvda/source/editableText.py` 94 and 97, `isScriptWaiting` and `isPendingEvents("gainFocus")`). Today `Cause::Settled` is re-judged on facts (`inbox.rs` 391, `entries.rs` 528). Each stage must therefore also check its causes when it starts, which puts the rule in two places unless the declaration is defined as a level the driver evaluates at the ask.

### M3. Stamping at arrival (rule 8) changes the cap's exemption against NVDA

v2 line 318 stamps "the readers' last focus's ids for the cap's exemption" on the entry at arrival. Today `exempt_from_cap` reads `facts.versions.last` live each time the limiter judges (`limiter.rs` 358, `entries.rs` 964 to 972). NVDA computes the always-allowed objects from `lastQueuedFocusObject` at the pump, not when the win event arrived (`nvda/source/IAccessibleHandler/__init__.py` 1025 to 1035). Sequence: an MSAA event for object X arrives while the last focus is W; a focus on X executes; the thread then passes ten events. Today and in NVDA X's event is exempt; stamped, it is dropped. The stamp is also an id held outside the record that owns its scope (against 172), and stamps are exempt from the world-change check (297: "the entry's own data"), so anything laundered into a stamp escapes rule 4. `place` is likewise judged live today (`limiter.rs` 126 to 178, `driver.rs` 1015).

### M4. The comparison passes on steps where a reader read something the world lacks

v2 lines 459 and 534: a miss "excludes its step from comparison", and line 687 counts a mutant as caught only by a difference, an invariant or a compile failure. A Verbatim defect that reads the wrong element or an extra property is the typical cause of a miss, so the defect removes its own step from judgement. A miss by Verbatim on a world that NVDA read without one must be a failure, and the count of excluded steps per world must ratchet.

The experiments already show the weaker form: `nvda-oracle/text/out_ia.txt` reports the Run dialog's description wrong ("Type the name of a program..." against the capture's "Run DLL") with an empty "misses" section. A world missing children answered "none" rather than "unknown", NVDA's code ran confidently on it, and nothing flagged it. Verbatim over the same world would agree with the wrong answer.

### M5. Reactions to a reader's own actions are not in any recording, contrary to line 498

Line 498: "Actions both make, or neither makes, need no model, because the recording already holds Windows' answer." The recorder runs with no screen reader and does not send reader gestures (473). So nothing either reader does as a result of a gesture is recorded: setting focus, invoking, moving the caret during say-all, moving the caret to the review position, selecting. Harness rule 14 gates only application keys. For these the world either never reacts (both readers then agree on a world that could not happen) or a hand-scripted step plays the reaction on the timeline whether or not Verbatim made the call, so a Verbatim that forgot to act still hears the reaction. The reaction must be gated on the action being observed from each reader, and the features whose worlds need this (say-all, caret-follows-review, activation, the shell list's click) must be listed as R4 for both readers, not only NVDA.

### M6. The measurement gate measures the skeleton and a tuned corpus, not the verifier

- Line 687 counts a compile or check failure as a catch. Most historical bugs are of the classes the skeleton forbids by construction, so they cannot be written as mutants or fail to compile, and the rate is high before any world is replayed. Report the rate over mutants that compile and pass the checks, separately.
- Line 691: between 50 and 80 percent the misses are fixed "and the rate measured once more", on the same mutants. A world or invariant written for a known miss will catch it. The second measurement needs fresh mutants.
- Harness defects (684, 692): the twelve are known, and the strict stand-ins and pass-through list were written to close exactly them, so "every one caught" is true by construction. The informative fact is the one already observed: the prototype matched two live captures while carrying all twelve, so the copy check does not detect harness defects. The gate should state which of the twelve the copy check alone catches.
- Scope: only focus-slice bugs count (682). The audit's costly areas are the terminal, caret keys, dialogs and say-all (`patterns.md`). A pass says nothing about them, yet line 691 lets "the verification grow past the slice as planned".
- With about 20 mutants, 80 percent is 16 of 20; the interval is about 18 points either side, so 75 and 85 cannot be told apart.

## Should-fix

- S1. Flood control is removed in the slice and returned only in M6.1. Line 101 drops the transport's merge, priority and drop. Line 233's `coalesce` holds "waiting items", but nothing waits until credit exists (812, 815), so between the slice and M6.1 nothing bounds a flood. Credit is also returned only by `scopes.ended`, that is, when Core's entries end: with the front stuck on a hung call and credit spent, the listener holds the foreground tell that would let Core abandon that call. And pongs lose their priority (`docs/design/communication.md` 105 and 229): behind a backlog on a first-in, first-out pipe, three missed intervals end a healthy reader.
- S2. The cost per focus is understated. The readers' last focus's `Keep` (161) is a `Batch` step, so it is an `Ask` with its own `Final`: a fourth crossing into Core and a wake per commit, plus a `Cancel` of the old one. Line 141 claims no wake added and line 175 claims both ride on steps that already send to that peer; at commit Core otherwise sends the listener nothing. Each ended entry likewise flushes a scopes-only `Cancel` to the listener (98), one message and one listener wake per told event. CLAUDE.md asks for a measurement when a change adds wakes.
- S3. The `!Send` rule adds little beyond the closure's borrow. `cx.read`'s borrow already cannot cross a wait. What the marker adds is stopping an owned clone of a whole record. Ids, booleans, strings and `Arc`s of entry types are `Send` and pass. The real guard is the world-change mode (294 to 298), which is dynamic, depends on a world and seed reaching that wait, and uses per-record versions: `Application` holds terminal models, the hung set, the backend cache and identity (196 to 201), so either any change flags every reader of the record or the versions must be per field. State the granularity.
- S4. Core's "zero cells" ratchet (701) conflicts with the mechanism. A ticket whose drop cancels (51), `cx.apply` through a scoped thread-local giving `&Core` (291), and futures stored in the `Queue` that is itself inside `Core` all need interior mutability, a sweep of `Out` against live tickets, or unsafe code. Today's driver uses `shared.borrow_mut()` (`inbox.rs` 601). Say which, and list it.
- S5. M5's `Wants` as a `Final` (778 to 796) leaves a suspended guest that belongs to no request. Between the host's `Final(Wants)` and Core's `Resume`, the lane holds a paused Wasm store. If the entry ends there (a foreground change, the outpost's `Ended`), no request is out to the host, so no `Cancel` can name the lane: a waiter with no ending. With three message kinds Core cannot send into an open request, which is the root.
- S6. `Destroyed` narrows the reused-handle race, it does not close it. The outpost learns of a destruction two hops late (listener, Core, outpost); a queued read naming an MSAA address runs against the new window in between. Also, destroys lost while the listener restarts leave the `Application`'s window entries (hung set, probe verdicts, follow) with no ending, the audit's own leak ("when a destroy is lost"); a handle reused by another process then sits in two `Application` records.
- S7. `Watch` replacement ends the old watch "just before the first item of the new" (108). With no event, the old watch never ends and `Listener::Ready` (191) holds one `watch`. Whether item scopes of the old watch survive its `Final` is unstated; if they do not, waiting entries lose their tokens.
- S8. The set-of-outputs comparison is lenient and partly an artefact. `nvda-oracle/time/out_threads.txt` shows the silent outcome only with `STABILIZE_DELAY` at 0; at 0.03, the console's value (`nvda/source/NVDAObjects/window/winConsole.py` 23), all 13 orders say "42". The count is 8 and 5, not v2's 9 and 4 (601). Where a set contains silence, a Verbatim that never speaks passes. Sets need each member's order named as reachable under NVDA's real constants, and Verbatim's own schedules should be enumerated at the same point.
- S9. Races in speech are not compared. Comparison runs use fault profile "none" (628) and the world serves the newest snapshot by simulated time (487); NVDA's clock advances 1 microsecond per read, Verbatim's by an invented latency model. Both read the same snapshot, so a defect that exists only because Verbatim reads later than NVDA is judged only by invariants.
- S10. The allowlist entry (638 to 643) matches by decision and world tag, so one entry hides every difference of that decision across every tagged world. Pin the step, or the expected count, and fail when the count changes.
- S11. The limiter's oracle is the design author's 32-line model, against line 664 ("NVDA's own code, never Verbatim's design"). See U1.
- S12. Patterns reintroduced: the hung fact is judged in four places (the callback's stamp at 234, `hung_marks` at 232, Core's hung set at 198, the shell's refusal at 303); `Out` holds an owner address that is a lookup into another record (337); the front view carries stamped facts to record transitions (54); the speech manager's locks and atomics stay (215).
- S13. File counts. The toggle example lists seven files as written (property table, outpost parse, announcement module, the `.ftl`, the message id, which today is a second file, `verbatim-i18n/src/lib.rs`, the test, the world), before the parity entry and `test-coverage.md` that CLAUDE.md requires. e812a792 touched ten, eight without documents, so the like-for-like saving is about one file. The name-change example omits the stage list (287), the decision-point table (563) and the module registration: about nine.
- S14. Sizes. Core's queue, driver, records, tickets, end causes and plain answerer are put at 2,050 lines; today's `event_queue` alone is 4,036 non-test lines and v2 adds record-owned requests, scopes and end causes to it. The supervisor is 1,300 against 4,047 with spares kept. The recorder line reads as 3,300 in total against 3,134 reused, which leaves about 170 lines for snapshots, text models, window messages, MSAA and scripted steps; if it means 3,300 added, say so. The stand-ins beyond the slice, where the R2 class's 37,600 NVDA lines wait, are not estimated, and the experiments already hold about 7,000 lines of Python for four hand-built worlds.

## Could not verify

- U1. "The limiter is modelled exactly" and "the run decides". The source does support keeping the oldest: `rateLimitedEventHandler.cpp` 46 to 50 erases through a structured binding that aliases the map entry it has just overwritten. But the saved `nvda-oracle/time/out_rl.txt` shows the DLL delivering three events and the model four, and it is older than both `ratelimit.py` and `run_ratelimit.py`. No saved output shows model and DLL equal.
- U2. Recorded-world fidelity. Every match so far is on a hand-built world ("zero misses on a world built by hand", 459; `textmodel.py` written for Notepad). No recorder exists; build step 7 is the first test of the claim the pipeline rests on.
- U3. That a provider's word and line boundaries can be captured "as boundary lists or a named rule" by a recorder with no knowledge of what a reader will ask.
- U4. The readers' last focus as "a state of the committed focus" (207). Today `LastFocus` is its own record with `names(&focus)` (`focus/versions.rs` 147 to 175), which implies the two can differ; I did not establish whether they can outside Core's own focus.
- U5. What cancel a record-owned request gets at a foreground change, and so whether a record's read stuck in a hung call is ever abandoned. Only the foreground reason abandons (`verbatim-model/src/request.rs` 685), and v2 says no cause ends a record's request.
- U6. The caret watch's four outcomes and the 25 Notepad steps: I read the outputs' headers, not every line.
