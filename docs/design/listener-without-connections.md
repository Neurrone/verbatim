# The focus listener without connections: a design

Status: ready for implementation; revised a third time on 2026-10-19 after a third adversarial review and Dickson's scope decisions of that day. Built: section 10, step 1's raw probe in the listener, with its unit tests and its leak measurement (risk 7); its scenario runs and `measure_repeat_explorer` A/B were made on 2026-10-19 and the step passes its criterion (section 10, step 1); step 2, the listener's Windows Forms check from the class name; step 3, the window's provider process as a function of its own; step 4, the listener's console check from a held element; step 5, the listener's walks stopping below the process boundary, a top-level window's owner included, and the outpost's MSAA continuation, with the boundary test; step 6, the listener reading every focus the classic way, with the connection count and the rule's test, measured (section 10, step 6); step 7, the outposts' checks against another process's window and the MSAA continuation above a window with a provider; step 8, the connection timeout at 2 seconds in every process, the proxy rule and the timeout split, measured (section 10, step 8); step 9, the end-to-end agent's desktop reading through MSAA; step 11, Core's tray dialog's shell items as a query to File Explorer's outpost; step 10, the lock removed. What the reviews left unsettled is measured during implementation, each with its pass criterion, in section 10's steps. Out of this design's scope, by those decisions: conhost's 3-second false "no" from `UiaHasServerSideProvider` (decision 2), UIA's late delivery of a new File Explorer process's focus events and the burst of gone senders (risks 2 and 13), which are fixed after the rest of the focus work, and any mockapp simulation of a UIA fault that cannot be reproduced reliably (section 7). Written against main at 01254292. The replacement calls were prototyped and measured in a scratch worktree of that commit; the experiments, scripts, patches and outputs are in `target/e2e-evidence/listener-no-connections/` (its `readme.md` describes each) and are cited here by number (`e40` to `e60`); that folder, under the uncommitted `target/`, is the only copy of their evidence. Experiments `e1` to `e39` are those of `target/e2e-evidence/launch-collision/collide/` and `docs/design/focus-pipeline.md`, section 20. The reviews' findings are answered where the design changed; the lists at the end say where.

Terms used here:

- A remote operation is a small program a UIA client sends into an application's process, where UIA runs it against the provider and returns the results in one round trip (`verbatim-uia-rops`).
- A connection is a UIA call that reaches a window by its handle: `ElementFromHandle`, `ElementFromHandleBuildCache`, `UiaHasServerSideProvider`, and `GetFocusedElement` and its cache-building form, which reach the focused window the same way.
- A held element is one the calling process already has: an event's sender, as UIA delivers it with its cache, or an element reached from one by a call on it.
- The process boundary is where an element's window has, above it in UIA's raw view, a window whose provider process is another: its parent window (`GetAncestor(hwnd, GA_PARENT)`), such as the Settings page's content window inside `ApplicationFrameHost`'s frame, or, for a top-level window, its owner (`GetWindow(hwnd, GW_OWNER)`) while the owner is visible, such as a dialog owned by another process's window. UIA's raw view puts an owned top-level window under its owner when the owner is visible and under the desktop when it is hidden (checked on 2026-10-19 with two mockapp processes, the owner set with `GWLP_HWNDPARENT`, and with File Explorer's folder window owning the Windows Security credential dialog of `CredentialUIBroker.exe`). Both sides are compared by provider process, so a console window above counts as its console host's (`verbatim_uia::parent_across_process_boundary`, local calls only).
- The raw probe is the arbitration probe as one window message, `SendMessageTimeoutW(hwnd, WM_GETOBJECT, 0, UiaRootObjectId)` (`experiments/uiahasserversideprovider-says-no-for-a-new-conhost-window-while-another-process-has-an-event-handler/design-raw-probe.md`).
- A window's provider process is the process that serves its UI Automation: its owner by `GetWindowThreadProcessId`, except a console window (`ConsoleWindowClass`), whose owner by that call is the console's client, such as `cmd.exe`, and whose provider is its console host, read locally with `NtQueryInformationProcess`'s `ProcessConsoleHostProcess` (`provider_process` and `console_host_of` in `crates/verbatim-uia/src/provider.rs`). A console window whose console host cannot be read from its client, such as one whose client runs as another user, has no provider process.
- The application's lock is the per-application mutex of `crates/verbatim-uia/src/lock.rs` (focus pipeline, section 20).

## 1. The problem, and the collision as now established

`docs/uia-bugs.md` ("A new connection fails while another process's remote operation is queued") records that while a remote operation from one process waits in an application, from its arrival until UIA begins evaluating it, a new connection from another process to any window of that application fails without an error: `UiaHasServerSideProvider` answers "no" after about 3 seconds, and the lookups return UIA's window proxy after the connection timeout. A walk from a held element up into a window of the application from another process fails the same way (`e24`). Calls from one process do not collide with each other (`e12`); calls on held elements are only delayed (`e10`, `e23`, `e25`, `e26`); MSAA calls do not collide (`e29`); and two remote operations from different processes do not collide (`e27`).

The lock exists because, in Verbatim, the remote operation is the listener's focus read and the connection is the outpost's probe or lookup, or the reverse. Its review found that a process's threads wait for each other, that the focus-now path took the wrong lock, and that a stale answer of "no lock" let a thread read unguarded; Dickson wants it removed rather than fixed. Each application has one outpost. If only that outpost sends remote operations into the application and connects to it, and the listener only calls held elements, the collision cannot arise from Verbatim's own calls, and the lock has nothing to guard. UIA's own focus tracking in the listener's process still connects to windows without a provider; section 2 says why that is expected to have no spoken effect.

This design's experiments ran against mockapp's UIA backend, its window thread suspended for 300 ms and, where said, another process's remote operation waiting there; each client was a separate process with Verbatim's process-wide timeouts of 5 seconds.

- The positive controls collided as that entry says: a lookup by handle made during the queued operation returned the window proxy after about 5.3 seconds, 6 of 6 in each of `e40`, `e41` and `e51`.
- Calls on held elements did not collide, beside the same queued operation. The listener's whole classic read from an element 62 levels deep returned mockapp's own elements at every level, 6 of 6 (`e41`); the same held as an event delivered it, by a process that had never connected to mockapp, 6 of 6 (`e51`); the console check from a held text area answered correctly, 6 of 6, where the check by handle took 5.3 to 10.3 seconds (`e40`).
- The second form, with no remote operation anywhere: a connection beside another process's connection and a third process's call on a held element failed, one of the two connections returning the proxy after about 5.3 seconds, 6 of 6 against mockapp (`e44`) and 6 of 6 against the collide server's own busy loop (`e50`); three connections at once failed one in 2 of 6. Not failing: two connections from two processes alone (18 of 18), a connection beside held calls of other processes (18 of 18), beside the raw probe (12 of 12), two connections from two threads of one process with a held call beside them (`e55`, 12 of 12), and a connection beside another that was still waiting when its process was ended (`e55`, 6 of 6 with a held call, 6 of 6 without). The mechanism inside UIA is not known.

The rule below excludes both forms as far as Verbatim's own calls go; UIA's focus tracking in the listener's process is not a call of Verbatim's (section 2).

## 2. The rule the design enforces

For each application, by process:

- The application's outpost may send remote operations into its application, connect to its windows by handle, and walk up within it. It does not connect to, send a remote operation into, or walk up into a window whose provider process is another. A window of another process that it meets in its application's ancestry it reads through MSAA, whether or not that window has a provider (the Settings frame has none; a WinUI host of WebView2 has one), without probing it (sections 4.3 and 4.4). UIA's client side still sends that window, when it is the parent of the outpost's own window, the message a connection begins with whenever the outpost's read returns its window's element, and connects to it when it has a provider: the same accepted limit as the listener's, below (decision 13).
- The listener makes no remote operation and no connection. Its calls into applications are calls on held elements: the event's sender, the last focus accepted, and the elements its walks reach from them. Its walks up stop below the process boundary. It also sends the raw probe, a window message and not a UIA connection: the probe answered correctly beside a queued operation (`e34`, 6 of 6), never disturbed another process's connection (`e38-rawpath`, `e42`, `e44`: 52 of 52, besides `e37`'s 120 early console trials), and left no state a later walk could see (`e53`: a held sender's nearest window was found alike before and after another process's raw probe, and with one made during a stall, 6 of 6).
- No other Verbatim process calls into an application through UIA, with the exceptions of section 4.5.

The rule restricts only new connections and remote operations. Plain calls on elements a process already holds are allowed to every Verbatim process: beside a queued remote operation they are only delayed, never answered by a proxy (`e23`, a walk up within one process, 6 of 6; `e41` and `e51`, the listener's whole classic read, 6 of 6 each).

Three things in the listener's process are not its own calls and stay. UIA's own focus tracking, which reacts to every focus `WinEvent` a process with a focus registration receives, was seen to send a native UIA window one `WM_GETOBJECT` with `UiaRootObjectId` and wParam 0 per event, and no message with a nonzero wParam, which is how a connection begins (`e49`: in the window's message log, exactly that one extra message after the event, 5 of 5, none in the control, 5 of 5; another process's lookup made at the same moment beside a held call succeeded in every trial). That shows no connection begun by message; it cannot show that UIA made none by other means. That message is the raw probe's, which `e42`, `e44` and `e53` found takes no part in either form. For a window without a provider it connects through UIA's MSAA proxy (`target/e2e-evidence/hung-hold/`, `h2_base`: `UiaServerConnection_HookMessage`); so the listener's process does connect to applications through it, and the rule cannot exclude that connection from either form. The first form can arise in the shell's `explorer.exe`, which serves both proxied windows, such as the desktop list focused at every scenario's start, and windows with providers into which its outpost sends remote operations. By `e1` it is the connection, not the operation, that fails, so the outpost's operation is unaffected and UIA's focus tracking in the listener gets a proxy for a window it would have reached through a proxy anyway; the listener drops such a window's UIA focus events at its native check, so no spoken effect is expected. Neither that first form nor whether this connection can take part in the second form is measured (risk 9). UIA's client side also sends a parent window across the process boundary two `WM_GETOBJECT`s with `UiaRootObjectId` and a nonzero wParam, the message a connection begins with, whenever a listener call on a held element returns the child window's element (decision 13; section 10, step 5): for the parents seen, the Settings frame and Quick Assist's WebView2 host, windows without a provider, the window answers that it has none and, in the mockapp pair, nothing more is sent to it, but for a parent with a provider, such as a WinUI host of WebView2, UIA's client side connects into that process and calls its provider, which the rule otherwise forbids. They make the listener's read wait for the parent's UI thread, drop the focus when that thread is busy longer than the connection timeout, and, for a parent with a provider whose process has another process's remote operation queued, wait one connection timeout more (risk 14, measured). An outpost's read of a focus in the child window, as it continues the listener's chain and in a focus-now query, returns that element too, so the same messages reach the parent from the outpost's process, and a parent with a provider is connected to (section 10, step 7). Decision 13 accepts this as a known limit, shared with NVDA, for the listener and the outposts alike. And UIA delivers the event's sender with its cache filled in the application; a delivery is not a connection.

## 3. Each of the listener's calls, and its replacement

From the code at 01254292 (`crates/verbatim-outpost/src/focus_read.rs`, `crates/verbatim-uia-rops/src/focus.rs`, `crates/verbatim-outpost/src/arbitration.rs`), in the order a focus meets them.

1. The hung window (`IsHungAppWindow` on the sender's cached handle): local. Unchanged.
2. The comparison with the current focus (`CompareElements`): both elements held, and it reaches no provider. Unchanged.
3. The sender's nearest window (`NormalizeElementBuildCache` on the sender, `verbatim_uia::nearest_window_handle`): a call on a held element that walks up to the first element with a window. It needs no connection to the window by anyone (`e53`: in a window no client had connected to, found 4 of 4 before any connection, and 24 of 24 during a stall, with another process's first lookup, raw probe, or nothing beside it). A failure is fast, not bounded by the connection timeout: File Explorer's early items failed in 0.3 to 11 ms, one in 223 ms (`e54`). A call on a held element is bounded by the transaction timeout, not the connection timeout: with the connection timeout at 500 ms and the transaction timeout at 5,000, a name read, this walk and a parent step on a held element all waited out a 3-second stall and answered (9 of 9); with the transaction timeout at 500 they failed after about 540 ms whatever the connection timeout (18 of 18), the walk answering no window (`e59`). So a busy application can also make the listener drop a focus as having no window, after 5 seconds. Its boundary: the walk stops at the first element with a window, the host of the sender's fragment root; that window has been the sender's own process's in every case seen, but the walk cannot check that before it takes the step, so a provider hosted in another process's window (none seen) would make this one step a walk into another process. In File Explorer's first seconds it finds no window for many focuses, for any client (decision 2). Unchanged.
4. The native check's probe (`UiaHasServerSideProvider`, a connection): replaced in the listener by the raw probe (the outposts keep `UiaHasServerSideProvider` on their own application's windows, decision 2), the lParam sign-extended as UIA sends it, with `SMTO_ABORTIFHUNG | SMTO_BLOCK | SMTO_ERRORONEXIT` and 3,000 ms, exactly `0x10000` counting as a provider (Dickson's decision; `design-raw-probe.md` and its amendments). The framework sweep `e45` compared the two probes on every visible top-level window of a .NET Framework 4.8 Windows Forms window, a .NET 8 Windows Forms window, a .NET 8 WPF window, Firefox, a Qt 6 application, the Environment Variables dialog, mockapp's comctl32 list view and File Explorer: 75 windows, 75 agreements; with `e39`'s 88, 163. Office and Java were not installed and are not covered.
5. The post-probe checks, made once per window when the probe finds a provider:
   - Windows Forms, for a `SysListView32` (today `ElementFromHandleBuildCache` and the framework id): replaced by the raw class name's `WindowsForms10.` prefix, a local call. Over 170 windows of the same applications the prefix and the framework id agreed on every one (`e45`). The .NET 8 list view answered the probe `0x10000` and has the prefix; the comctl32 list views and the .NET Framework one answered 0 and never reach the check.
   - The console (today `ElementFromHandle` on the console window and a search for its text area): replaced by the same reads from the held sender, the text area itself when its automation id is "Text Area", else that child of the held window element, found by a search down from it: its text pattern, its visible ranges and the first range's font. Correct beside the queued operation, 6 of 6 (`e40`), in a real console, 5 of 5 (`e46`), and in the prototype's console scenarios. Kept with the window's verdict.
6. The repeat check's keyboard focus of the last focus accepted (`CurrentHasKeyboardFocus`): held. Unchanged.
7. The sender's plain keyboard focus (`CurrentHasKeyboardFocus`): held. Unchanged.
8. The focus read (today one remote operation): replaced by the classic read that exists today as the remote operation's fallback (`focus_ancestry_classic`), without the two calls the listener has just made: it does not read the sender's keyboard focus again (the plain read of step 7 was made microseconds before), and does not ask for the nearest window again when its walk found none (step 3 found it). What remains: the previous element's keyboard focus, the sender's cache refreshed (`BuildUpdatedCache`), a list's or tab control's selection, then one `GetParentElementBuildCache` per ancestor until a known ancestor, the depth limit, the desktop root or the process boundary. Every call is on a held element (`e41`, `e51`); a step between two windows of one application is a walk up within one process, which a queued remote operation does not expose (`e23`, focus pipeline section 20, 6 of 6). The remote operation's second keyboard-focus check, for a focus that moved on while it was read, has no counterpart; a focus that moved on is followed by the focus event of where it went, which Core takes as newer (reasoning, not measured: the scenario runs of section 8 show no repeated or stale announcement). `uia.remote_operations` then governs the outposts only.
9. The legacy MSAA state of a menu item (`LegacyIAccessibleState`): one property read on the held sender (`e41`).
10. `mark_window` (`ElementFromHandleBuildCache` on the console window): removed. The classic read's parent step from the console's text area returns the console window's element with its handle and title (`e35`, `e46`, 11 of 11), so `mark_window` is not reached: the prototype logged none of its would-be calls.
11. `reach_the_top_level`: stops at the process boundary. The Settings content's step into the frame took 5.30 seconds in 2 of 3 trials beside a remote operation queued in `ApplicationFrameHost` (`e47`); stopping below the boundary cost no call. The boundary includes a top-level window's visible owner served by another process (terms): UIA's raw view goes up from an owned dialog's window to its owner, so the classic walk would otherwise step from the dialog into the owner's application.
12. A selection's or notification's nearest window (`check_fact`): held. Unchanged.
13. The classic walk's lock (`lock_up_from`, `lock_up_into`): removed with the lock.

## 4. The outposts' side

### 4.1 What an outpost may do

An outpost keeps every call it makes into its own application today: remote operations, lookups by handle, the probe (still `UiaHasServerSideProvider`, decision 2), the console's post-probe check by handle on its own windows, and walks within its application. The Windows Forms check of a list view is the raw class name's `WindowsForms10.` prefix, a local call, for the outposts as for the listener (section 3, item 5). It makes none of these into a window whose provider process is another (sections 4.3 and 4.4).

### 4.2 The Settings page and its frame

The Settings page's content (`Windows.UI.Core.CoreWindow`, in `SystemSettings.exe`) is a child of its frame (`ApplicationFrameWindow`, in `ApplicationFrameHost.exe`), which has no UIA provider. NVDA reads the frame as the window's MSAA client object: role pane, named by the title, presentable only with `WS_SYSMENU`. Its capture of the System page (alpha-58022, the pinned build) says "Settings", "Settings window", "Search box, Find a setting edit blank", and Verbatim says the same.

Under the rule the listener's chain ends at the content's window, and the outpost continues it from that window's parent through MSAA, as it already continues a chain that reaches a window read through MSAA. The prototype passed `settings_system_page` ten times this way with its exact expected speech. MSAA rather than local window calls: both read the same facts (`e47`: role client, name "Settings", focusable through MSAA in 296 to 297 ms beside a queued operation; title, `WS_SYSMENU`, visible and not disabled from local calls in 0.03 to 0.05 ms); MSAA is the object NVDA reads and Verbatim's path today, and does not collide. Local calls are the alternative if a hung frame host is ever seen to hold the Settings outpost.

The content was the frame's child whenever its focus arrived (`e47`, `e48`). Were it its own top-level window, the walk would end at the root with no frame, as NVDA's would. A pre-launched, suspended Settings was not observed.

### 4.3 Other places an outpost reaches another application

Reading the code at 01254292, an outpost reaches a window of another process in these places. Each is checked against the window's provider process, a local call:

- A console window, from `focused_control` and `focus_now` (the line references in this section are of main at 1fb52ac7, having drifted since 01254292): its owner by `GetWindowThreadProcessId` is the console's client, not the console host whose outpost answers for it. The window's provider process, read locally, says which process serves it with no call into any application, where the outpost once looked the window up by handle to read it. When the host cannot be read from the client, the window has no provider process, so no outpost takes it as its application's by that answer, the client's least of all, and the listener, which reads the host from the thread of each `WinEvent`, drops that window's events when the thread cannot be read rather than sending them to the client's outpost (`routed_process` in `listener.rs`, logged at debug once per window; Dickson, 2026-10-19); a console host's outpost still recognizes its own console window by `EnumThreadWindows` over its application's threads, which goes by the thread that created the window (`verbatim_uia::served_by`). `provider_process`, `served_by` and `console_host_of` are `verbatim-uia`'s `provider` module, and `verbatim-outpost` re-exports `provider_process` and `console_host_of`, so `verbatim-app`, which uses `provider_process` for its routing below, gains no dependency on `verbatim-uia`.
- Core's routing of a console client's focus-now query (`want_focus_now`, `crates/verbatim-app/src/main.rs` 1165 to 1176): until the console host's outpost has reported, the query goes to the client's own outpost, which would then read the focused element of conhost's window, a connection into another application. Core resolves the client to its console host locally, with the same call, and asks the console host's outpost; when the host cannot be read, it asks no outpost.
- The window filters of `crates/verbatim-outpost/src/outpost/window.rs` (`focus_window_of`, `foreground_window_of`, `main_window_of`, `top_level_windows`) decide a window is the outpost's application's by its owner, which for a console window names the console's client. They use the window's provider process instead (`served_by`), so a console window is its console host's and never its client's. With that and Core's routing above, no outpost is started for a console client on account of its console window. The supervisor checks it where it starts an outpost for a process on account of a fact in a window: the window's provider process must be that process (`check_served_by` in `supervisor/owner.rs`), and a debug build fails where a release build logs an error; a window closed since is not checked.
- `focused_control`'s `GetFocusedElementBuildCache`: refused, answering no focused control, when the focus window's provider process is not the outpost's application, so a race in routing can never become a connection into another application.
- `msaa_ancestors` (`outpost/read.rs` 556): the MSAA walk asks `read_by_other_api` of every ancestor's window before it takes the step (`crates/verbatim-ia2/src/acquire.rs` 743 to 746), and the outpost answers with `window_uses_uia`, which through `arbitration::uses_uia_keeping` (`arbitration.rs` 346 to 352) probes the window with `verbatim_uia::has_server_side_provider`: a new connection into another application's window, such as the Settings outpost probing `ApplicationFrameHost`'s frame or a WebView2 outpost probing its WinUI host. For a host window with a provider the answer also makes the walk return `Walked::Crossed`, so the chain is cut at the boundary instead of continuing through MSAA. Decided (Dickson, 2026-10-19): `read_by_other_api` answers false, without probing, for an ancestor window whose provider process (`GetWindowThreadProcessId`, a local lookup that sends the application nothing, a console window mapped to its console host) is not the outpost's application, so the MSAA walk continues through it whether it has a provider or not; the probe, `window_element` and the UIA walk above stay for the outpost's own application's windows. Its effect on the calls: for a focus whose ancestry crosses into another process's window, the outpost makes no probe of that window (one connection fewer at its first sight and after each "no" expires), and above a host window with a provider no `window_element` lookup and UIA steps, but one MSAA parent step per ancestor up to the root.
- `corrected_backend`, for a navigation neighbor that is the root of another window: `window_element` on it; refused for another process's window, the neighbor kept as read.
- Object navigation's step (`Uia::neighbor`, called from classic navigation in `outpost/read.rs`): a raw-view step from an element of the outpost's application can land in another process's window, as the parent of the Settings page's content is `ApplicationFrameHost`'s frame, and that step reads the neighbor through UIA, in the other process, before `corrected_backend` switches it to MSAA. Accepted as an exception to section 2's rule (Dickson, 2026-10-20): the step is user-initiated, one per key press, and off the per-event path. A stop before a step across the boundary, judged with local calls as the walks judge it, is a follow-up (below, "Follow-ups outside this design").
- `dump_tree`, the outpost's answer to `verbatim-inspect`'s tree query, which can name any window handle: refused, answering an error that names the window's provider process, when that process is not the outpost's application.
- `focus_path::find`, for a route whose window belongs to another process: with the listener's chain stopping at the boundary no such route is built; the check guards it.
- `uia_chain_onward` (`outpost/read.rs`), which continues a chain the listener's read or a focus-now read stopped below the boundary, asks `window_uses_uia` of the parent or owner window before it continues through MSAA, and so probes that other process's window with `UiaHasServerSideProvider`, a new connection into it, at its first sight and after each "no" expires. And `uia_ancestors`, the outpost's own UIA walk for the ancestors query and for a focus-now read's classic fallback (`uia_enrichment`), takes the step up from its application's window into another process's window and then asks `read_by_other_api` (`window_uses_uia`) of it: a walk step and a probe into that process. Decided with `msaa_ancestors` above, and built in section 10, step 7: neither probes a window whose provider process is not the outpost's application, which is continued through MSAA whether it has a provider or not, and `uia_ancestors` stops below the boundary and continues from the parent or owner through MSAA, as `uia_chain_onward` does.

### 4.4 A window of another process above the focus

WebView2 content is served by `msedgewebview2.exe` inside a window of its host application. In Quick Assist (`e56`), the web content's chain ran up to its `Chrome_WidgetWin_1` window, whose parent `Chrome_WidgetWin_0` belongs to `QuickAssist.exe`; that window and the top-level window above it have no provider (UIA gives them only its HWND proxy, and MSAA a nameless client), so they are read through MSAA, as the Settings frame is, and what NVDA would read through UIA there is the same proxy. WinUI hosts, such as the new Outlook, Teams, Widgets and Xbox, put WebView2 under windows that do have providers, whose names and roles NVDA reads through UIA, the top-level window's "title window" among them. None of those is installed here, and the Widgets board could not be opened while Quick Assist, which runs at a higher integrity level, held the foreground; so that family is not measured.

A top-level dialog owned by another process's window is the same case: UIA's raw view goes up from the dialog's window to its owner while the owner is visible (terms), so the listener stops at the dialog's window and the dialog's outpost continues the chain from the owner window through MSAA. The dialogs that open over another application's window on this machine were looked for on 2026-10-19: File Explorer's "Open with" (`OpenWith.exe`'s window is not owned), its "Run as different user" prompt (`CredentialUIBroker.exe`'s "Windows Security" window, not owned), and its Share window and Notepad's print dialog (each an unowned `ApplicationFrameWindow` of `explorer.exe`, the application kept modal by a `ProxyModalWindow` of `explorer.exe` that it owns and that never takes the focus). One owned dialog was found: the same Windows Security credential dialog when its caller passes its own window to `CredUIPromptForWindowsCredentials`, as an application asking for credentials does. Opened by a script with File Explorer's folder window as that window, the dialog's window (class `Credential Dialog Xaml Host`, UIA framework Win32) was owned by the folder window, `explorer.exe`'s, and UIA's raw view put it under it. NVDA (alpha-58022, the pinned build) said as it opened: "unavailable" (the folder window, disabled by the dialog), then "Windows Security window", then "User name edit". It did not speak the folder window, but that window was the previous focus's top-level window, which NVDA does not speak again whichever way its walk went, so the capture does not show whether NVDA's chain reaches the owner. The dialog's window reports UIA's Win32 framework, which suggests it has no provider of its own (not probed); Verbatim reads a dialog window without a provider through MSAA, as NVDA does, whose parent is the desktop, so the owner is not reached; for a dialog whose own window has a provider, the chain is continued through the owner's MSAA object, which is not the node the previous focus's chain held for that window, and whether Verbatim then speaks the owner again is not tested. From this session's processes, which lack `uiAccess`, UIA found none of that dialog's descendants, and keys the end-to-end agent sent to the run-as prompt were not received, which suggests the broker runs above the session's integrity level; not checked further.

For a window with a provider above the boundary (decision 7, Dickson 2026-10-18): the outpost of the focus's application continues the chain from the boundary's parent window through MSAA, as it does for a window without one. MSAA reaches such a window through UIA's MSAA bridge when the window answers MSAA with it, which does not collide (`e29`), and through MSAA's standard proxy otherwise, as mockapp's provider window, which answers only `UiaRootObjectId`, is read; neither makes a UIA call of the outpost's. UIA's client side still connects to such a parent as the outpost's read returns the child window's element (section 2, decision 13). What is spoken can differ from NVDA's: a window NVDA reads as "title window" through UIA may read through the bridge as a client or pane named by its title. The parity ledger records the family, with the owned dialog above (`docs/parity/windows-and-processes.md`, "Content hosted in another process's window"): the ancestors above the host window are read through MSAA, where NVDA reads them through UIA. The later step, asking the host window's own outpost for those ancestors through UIA as a query and joining the two chains, is listed with decision 11 and is to be measured when built: one more query and hop per focus that crosses such a boundary.

### 4.5 Verbatim's other UIA clients

- Core's tray dialog's shell item reads connect to File Explorer's windows from verbatim.exe while the dialog is open. Decision 9 moves them into File Explorer's outpost: Core sends the outpost a query naming the window, the outpost reads the items with its own UIA client, within its own application, and answers with their names, as it answers a navigation query.
- The end-to-end agent (`crates/verbatim-agent/src/focus.rs`) makes UIA connections: `GetFocusedElement` in `focused_element` (87) and `misspelt_words` (137), and `ElementFromHandle` in `focus_by_automation_id` (48). ## 5. The timeout change

Why the connection timeout was raised. 13dcd2db (2026-10-03) raised it from UIA's default 2 seconds to 10 because Notepad, busy starting, answered the outpost's lookups later than 2 seconds and the lookup of its focused element returned UIA's window proxy, a nameless edit in place of its "Text editor" document. 523ee6fb (2026-10-11) set both timeouts to 5 seconds once per process. Nothing in the history records a failure at 5 seconds that a shorter value would avoid. The case for 2 seconds is the hung window (`target/e2e-evidence/hung-hold/`): a hung window named in a focus `WinEvent` holds all of the listener's UIA event delivery for twice the connection timeout per such event, 10 seconds at 5 and 4 at 2; NVDA leaves 2 seconds. `e54` saw such holds of 2 to 3 seconds in the listener's delivery while a new File Explorer window was built.

What it governs after this design. The timeout is process-wide.

- In the listener, no call of Verbatim's: it bounds UIA's own focus tracking, which is the hung-window hold. The listener's calls on held elements run under the transaction timeout, unchanged at 5 seconds. A read is several such calls since the remote read went (section 10, step 6), so the whole read, its checks included, has one time budget, `READ_BUDGET` (2 seconds), asked before each of its calls after the first: once it has passed, the read makes no more calls and reports what it has read, its ancestors unknown, or drops a focus whose window it has not yet judged. A transaction timeout bounds each provider call, not each UIA operation (`docs/uia-bugs.md`, "A timeout bounds each provider call, not an operation"), so no count of calls gives a true worst case; the budget does instead. `READ_DEADLINE`, the watchdog, is the budget, the one call that can be under way when it passes (5 seconds; the raw probe's 3 is shorter) and a second to spare, 8 seconds, which also bounds how long an application's later events wait behind the read's marker. An application answering each call in about 1.5 seconds has its focus reported with its ancestors unknown rather than abandoned.
- In an outpost, its lookups by handle (`focus_path::find`, `window_snapshot`, `resolve_uia_element`, the lookups in `msaa_ancestors` and `corrected_backend`, `dump_tree`, the post-probe checks on its own windows) and the focus-now answer's `GetFocusedElementBuildCache`. At 2 seconds a lookup of a window whose application is busy for longer returns UIA's window proxy: 13dcd2db's failure, in the outpost.
- In an outpost, its event registrations: the group it registers on each focus element (`Scope::Elements`) and the removal of the previous focus's group. By `e57` and `e58` (`docs/uia-bugs.md`, "A registration that times out reports success"), an `AddEventHandlerGroup` on an application busy longer than the connection timeout returns success after the timeout and then hears nothing for about 2 seconds; in that gap every registration the process has on that window is silent, and a `RemoveEventHandlerGroup` fails with `UIA_E_TIMEOUT` and leaves the group live. The outpost forgets a removed group whatever the result (`crates/verbatim-uia/src/subscribe.rs`, `RemoveEventHandlerGroup`), so the previous focus's group is leaked and delivers afterwards. At 5 seconds an application busy 2 to 5 seconds never meets this; at 2 seconds it does, and the A/B's new File Explorer windows were busy 2.7 to 3.3 seconds. How often it happens, and what it loses, is measured in section 10, step 8. The loss itself is not reproduced reliably by any test (`docs/uia-bugs.md`), so no mockapp test is planned for it.
- The listener's probe no longer uses UIA, so its wait is its own 3,000 ms. The outposts' probe stays `UiaHasServerSideProvider`, whose "no" comes about 3 seconds after the call (`docs/uia-bugs.md`: `e1`; `e38`, 3,022 to 3,058 ms).

The proxy rule. An outpost's lookup by handle of a window whose verdict says it has a provider is not answered when the element it returns is served only by UIAutomationCore's own proxies; it is not read or kept, and the next event or query that needs it looks it up again. The test reads the provider description cached with the lookup (UIA property 30107, no extra call): answered when any provider it names comes from a module other than `UIAutomationCore.dll`, under whatever role (main, override, nested). The first draft's test, "the main provider is a proxy", would have refused File Explorer's 11 child windows (`CtrlNotifySink`, `NamespaceTreeControl`, `ScrollBar`, `SHELLDLL_DefView`) that answer the probe `0x10000` but whose main provider is the MSAA proxy under an override from `DUI70.dll`, and `msaa_ancestors` crossing into `NamespaceTreeControl` would have lost the ancestors above it. Against recorded descriptions the corrected test answered every case: the 30 windows of `e39` that answer `0x10000` (the 11 among them), the 164 lookups that returned mockapp's, conhost's or the collide server's own provider, and the 50 that returned UIA's proxy in a collision (`e40` to `e55`). An empty or missing description (the property unsupported, or its read failed) counts as answered: the rule refuses a lookup only on positive evidence that only UIA's proxies served it. Its unit test runs over recorded strings, one of each kind (`proxy-rule-descriptions.txt`: File Explorer's `CtrlNotifySink`, `NamespaceTreeControl`, `SHELLDLL_DefView` and `ScrollBar` override windows, mockapp's, conhost's and Edge's own providers, a collision's proxy, and the empty description).

Repeated refusals are bounded without a retry: a refused lookup is not made again for that window until a new event from that window, or a new query naming it, needs it, so the refusals of a window busy for a long time are at most one per such event; and a window refused three times in a row has its next lookup, when one is needed, logged at info with the count, so a window that never answers shows in the logs. A window whose application stops answering altogether is a hung application, whose events the listener drops before any call (focus pipeline section 12, the hung-window check).

The focus-now answer (decision 8). Under the rule an outpost reads its application's focused element itself, at Verbatim's start, after the outpost's replacement, and on Core's other focus-now queries. At 2 seconds an application busy for longer answers with UIA's proxy; by the proxy rule the answer then has no focused control, and nothing asks again until the application's next focus event, which arrives once it is responsive and is spoken as any focus is. Retrying is not proposed. Measured: 10 runs of `outpost_crash_recovery` made 10 focus-now reads, each answered by the application's own provider; 10 runs of `return_after_retirement` made none through UIA.

The other cost (`h6`): a focus change in a window busy for longer than the connection timeout is dropped by UIA's focus tracking; NVDA, at 2 seconds, loses it too. The transaction timeout stays at 5 seconds.

## 6. The verdict caching

As Dickson decided and as built today: a "yes" kept for the window's lifetime, a deliberate difference from NVDA's half-second cache, which `docs/parity/backends.md` describes and should mark as a difference in a sentence of its own with its reason; a "no" kept for 500 ms, as NVDA keeps both. The listener's verdict, the console check's included, goes to the outpost with the focus (`record_probe`).

## 9. Risks and open questions

1. The second form of the collision is not understood. The rule keeps Verbatim from making its mix alone; UIA's focus tracking for windows without a provider, Core's tray dialog, the agent's remaining UIA calls and other software can still make it.
2. UIA delivers a newly started File Explorer process's focus events late or not at all (decision 2): in today's build and the prototype alike, the listener's WinEvent hook saw File Explorer move its focus through every item while UIA delivered none, or none of the first 50 to 130. How often varied over the day, from 0 of 19 runs of today's build to 3 of 6. It is fixed after this design and the rest of the focus work are built (Dickson, 2026-10-19), as a later, separate step (decision 11): when a WinEvent focus has no UIA focus event following it, Core asks that application's outpost for its focus. Whether NVDA loses these focuses is not captured.
3. The classic read costs one more round trip per focus than the program, more in a list, and more wake-ups in the listener (section 8; decision 12).
4. A slow application holds the listener's read longer: each call waits for it, where the program waited once. `READ_BUDGET` (2 seconds), asked before each of the read's calls, ends the read, which reports what it has; a call that times out (5 seconds) fails the read, which is then reported from the event, as today.
5. Windows with a provider above the boundary are read through MSAA's bridge (decision 7), which can name and role them differently from NVDA; the family is not installed here, so the difference is not measured. The query through the host's outpost is a later step.
10. The listener's nearest-window walk takes one step it cannot check in advance: the step from the sender's fragment root to its host window, which has been the sender's own process's in every case seen (section 3, step 3).
11. Variant d is not needed: the outposts' probe as `UiaHasServerSideProvider` does what it was kept for, with no second call (decision 2).
13. A new folder window's first focuses can arrive in a burst after their senders are gone, and are then dropped for having no window, in today's build (1 of 24 runs, and that one, base 1, possibly beside a build) and with the decided change at the 2-second connection timeout (1 of 14), more often at 5 seconds (3 of 6) and without any early outpost connection (20 of 20) (decision 2); the runs cannot tell the decided change's rate from today's. The trigger is not established: the bursts followed outposts' first probes of the items view of 5.2 and 3.3 seconds, whose verdicts this design does not record; in three other runs (H 5, H 7, base 9) an outpost's first probe answered "no" after about 3,043 ms, UIA's 3-second "no", on a `DirectUIHWND` window other than the items view the listener later read, and no burst followed. Every client that reads those focuses late loses them; it is fixed after the rest of the focus work (Dickson, 2026-10-19), not by this design.
12. A registration that times out still reports success, and starts delivering about 2 seconds after it returns, whatever the timeouts; events raised in that gap are lost, and once the first event arrives nothing more is lost (`experiments/a-registration-that-times-out-reports-success/`, its `e57`; `docs/uia-bugs.md`). In the gap every registration the process has on that window is silent, and a removal fails and leaves the group live (`e58`). The listener's desktop-wide registration is made once at its start. The outposts register a group on each focus element and remove the previous focus's, so at the 2-second connection timeout an outpost meets the gap whenever its application is busy for longer than 2 seconds as the focus moves, which 5 seconds avoided for applications busy 2 to 5 seconds (section 5); section 10, step 8 measures how often, with its pass criterion. This design does not trust a registration only after its first event; a later step that did so would pay two costs: a gap of about 2 seconds during which the listener must keep serving with what it has, and an application that raises no event never confirming the registration at all.
6. The content window of a pre-launched, suspended Settings as its own top-level window: not observed.
7. The raw probe's open items: Office and Java windows, and the memory it leaves in the probed process. Step 1's one-off measurement (2026-10-19, debug build, mockapp's `uia` window from `small.json`, its handle count and private bytes as `Get-Process` reports them, no change to mockapp; the raw probe's experiment record, "The leak measurement"): every probe answered `0x10000`. Handles went from 116 before any probe to 165 after the first, which is UIA's server-side setup in mockapp, then 166 after 1,100, and stayed at 166 through 12,000. Private bytes grew about 2.3 KB a probe for the first 1,000 probes, about 2.2 MB in all, then did not change through 12,000; the growth had stopped by 1,000 probes in each of four runs, and between 900 and 1,000 in the run read every 100 probes. So the raw probe does not leak without bound, but each window answering it keeps about 2.3 KB in the application for up to about 1,000 probes; the cause is not established (the inference is that UIA keeps the provider handed to `UiaReturnRawElementProvider` for a request with wParam 0 in a table of bounded size, which mockapp's root provider, created afresh for each request, would fill), nor whether the bound is per window or per process. `UiaHasServerSideProvider`, the control, left handles at 186 after one probe and 195 after 2,000, flat after, and private bytes about 0.1 MB higher after 1,000 and flat after. The listener probes a window that has a provider once while its verdict is kept, so in use this is about one retained entry a window. It probes the window again in three cases: a console or list view whose post-probe check does not answer gets no verdict and nothing recorded, so it is probed on each focus until the check answers; each new `FocusReader` starts with no verdicts; and a window whose handle is found owned by another thread than the one its verdict was recorded for is probed as new. Not kept as a test (Dickson, 2026-10-19).
8. A request still queued in the application after its call timed out stays possible between an outpost's own calls, which do not collide with each other, and beside a queued connection whose process ended (`e55`: no collision). Not measured: a remote operation queued by an outpost that has since ended, met by its replacement (crash recovery or retirement) in a busy application, which is the first form with a dead sender; `outpost_crash_recovery` ran 10 times against an idle application. Its cost is bounded: the replacement's lookup returns UIA's proxy, which the proxy rule refuses, and its probe's "no" lasts 500 ms. This is the only case left in which two outposts' calls meet one application: under section 4.3 a console client gets no outpost on account of its console window, so a client's and its conhost's outposts never probe the same window.
9. UIA's own focus tracking in the listener's process connects to windows without a provider (section 2). Not measured: whether that connection, beside the shell's outpost's queued remote operation in `explorer.exe`, meets the first form (by `e1` its connection would fail, with no spoken effect expected, since the listener drops such windows' UIA focus events), and whether it can be one of the second form's two connections.
14. UIA's client side sends a parent window across the process boundary `WM_GETOBJECT`s with `UiaRootObjectId` and a nonzero wParam when a listener call returns the child window's element (decision 13). Measured in mockapp on 2026-10-19 (`experiments/the-listeners-read-below-another-processs-window/`), with a parent with a provider and one without, each in a process where another process's remote operation was queued behind the busy UI thread, by both read paths, 10 trials of each against 10 controls and 10 with the thread only busy: the listener's read waits for the parent's UI thread whenever it is busy, and drops the focus when that lasts longer than the connection timeout, with or without a queued operation; with a provider and a queued operation it waits one connection timeout more after the thread is free (about 5,319 ms against 317 ms busy only, at 5 seconds; about 2,325 ms at 2 seconds) and still returns the control's chain, while the outpost's operation answers correctly; without a provider a queued operation adds nothing. The Settings frame's share of these messages, and a real WinUI host's behaving as mockapp's window does, are inferred, not measured. Stopping the walks one step lower passed in mockapp by the classic path (no message to the parent while the listener reads, reads of about 2 ms in every arm), but the wait moves to the outpost that reads the child window's element, and no general rule for where the walk stops exists. Accepted as a known limit shared with NVDA (Dickson, 2026-10-19; decision 13).

## 10. Implementation order

Dependency-ordered, smallest step first; each step builds, passes `cargo xtask ci` and its scenarios, and is measured before the next.

1. The raw probe in the listener only, as `design-raw-probe.md` and its amendments specify, with its unit tests; the outposts keep `UiaHasServerSideProvider` for their own application's windows (decision 2).
