# The focus listener without connections: a design

Status: adopted and built. Only an application's own outpost connects to it or sends it a remote operation; the focus listener reads every focus from elements it holds. This rule replaced a per-application lock that the listener and an outpost took around each remote operation and each connection by handle, which the rule leaves nothing to guard and which is removed. This document keeps the problem, the choices and their reasons, the costs, the risks and the limitations. The mechanism is described in the crate guides: the listener's reader, the outposts' checks and the gap read in `docs/crates/verbatim-outpost.md`, the raw probe, the provider process, the boundary, the held console check, the proxy rule and the timeouts in `docs/crates/verbatim-uia.md`, and the remote programs in `docs/crates/verbatim-uia-rops.md`. The UIA defects it works around are in `docs/uia-bugs.md`. Experiments `e1` to `e36` are in `experiments/a-new-connection-fails-while-another-processs-remote-operation-is-queued/`, `e37` to `e39` in `experiments/uiahasserversideprovider-says-no-for-a-new-conhost-window-while-another-process-has-an-event-handler/`, `e57` and `e58` in `experiments/a-registration-that-times-out-reports-success/`, `h2` and `h6` in `experiments/a-hung-window-named-in-a-system-focus-event-holds-a-processs-uia-events/`, and the boundary experiment of risk 14 in `experiments/the-listeners-read-below-another-processs-window/`; the other measurements cited here were made in a scratch worktree, and their figures here are the only record kept.

Terms used here:

- A remote operation is a small program a UIA client sends into an application's process, where UIA runs it against the provider and returns the results in one round trip (`verbatim-uia-rops`).
- A connection is a UIA call that reaches a window by its handle: `ElementFromHandle`, `ElementFromHandleBuildCache`, `UiaHasServerSideProvider`, and `GetFocusedElement` and its cache-building form, which reach the focused window the same way.
- A held element is one the calling process already has: an event's sender, as UIA delivers it with its cache, or an element reached from one by a call on it.
- The process boundary is where an element's window has, above it in UIA's raw view, a window whose provider process is another: its parent window (`GetAncestor(hwnd, GA_PARENT)`), such as the Settings page's content window inside `ApplicationFrameHost`'s frame, or, for a top-level window, its owner (`GetWindow(hwnd, GW_OWNER)`) while the owner is visible, such as a dialog owned by another process's window. UIA's raw view puts an owned top-level window under its owner when the owner is visible and under the desktop when it is hidden. Both sides are compared by provider process, so a console window above counts as its console host's (`verbatim_uia::parent_across_process_boundary`, local calls only).
- The raw probe is the arbitration probe as one window message, `SendMessageTimeoutW(hwnd, WM_GETOBJECT, 0, UiaRootObjectId)` (`experiments/uiahasserversideprovider-says-no-for-a-new-conhost-window-while-another-process-has-an-event-handler/design-raw-probe.md`).
- A window's provider process is the process that serves its UI Automation: its owner by `GetWindowThreadProcessId`, except a console window (`ConsoleWindowClass`), whose owner by that call is the console's client, such as `cmd.exe`, and whose provider is its console host, read locally (`verbatim_uia::provider_process`). A console window whose console host cannot be read from its client, such as one whose client runs as another user, has no provider process.

## Decisions

1. The rule of section 2 replaces the per-application lock. Under it no Verbatim process but an application's own outpost sends a remote operation into it or connects to it by a call of Verbatim's, so the collision the lock guarded cannot come from two of Verbatim's own calls. UIA's own focus tracking in the listener's process, and the messages UIA's client side sends a parent window across the process boundary (decision 13), are the exceptions section 2 describes.
2. Every probe is the raw probe, the outposts' of their own application's windows as well as the listener's, and no connection to the window follows it (Dickson, 2026-10-18; amended 2026-10-20 and 2026-10-21). Reasons: `UiaHasServerSideProvider` answers a false "no" after 3 seconds for a new conhost window while another process has an event handler, which made `conhost_two_windows` fail about 1 run in 20, and did the same on File Explorer's `DirectUIHWND` windows (`docs/uia-bugs.md`); and an outpost's connection after a "yes" held its worker for seconds while File Explorer built a new window (risk 11). A window too busy to answer the raw probe within its 3 seconds is a "no" for that read only; the next read probes again. File Explorer's early-focus loss in new windows has two mechanisms, UIA's late delivery of a new process's focus events (risk 2) and a burst of focuses whose senders are gone (risk 13); an outpost's early connection made the burst rarer, and both are left to decision 11's read.
3. The second form of the collision, which needs new connections from two processes (section 1), is accepted and recorded in `docs/uia-bugs.md`. Under the rule only the application's outpost connects by a call of Verbatim's, so Verbatim's own calls cannot make the mix; Core's tray dialog before decision 9, the end-to-end agent (decision 10), UIA's focus tracking (risk 9) and other software still can.
4. UIA's connection timeout is UIA's default 2 seconds in every process and the transaction timeout 5 seconds (decided, Dickson, 2026-10-19), with the proxy rule of section 5 for the outposts' lookups by handle. The outposts were to keep 5 seconds if the 2 seconds made their event registrations enter UIA's registration gap; measured, none did (section 10, step 8).
5. The Settings frame is read through MSAA, as NVDA reads it (section 4.2).
6. The console host is found locally (section 4.3): an outpost's check that it hosts a console window, and Core's routing of a console client's focus-now query, use the window's provider process, not a lookup by handle; an outpost refuses to read the focused element of a window whose provider process is not its application.
7. A window of another process above the focus, such as a WinUI or Win32 window hosting WebView2: the listener's chain stops at the boundary as for the Settings frame, and the outpost of the focus's application reads the remaining ancestors through MSAA, which reaches a window that has a UIA provider through UIA's MSAA bridge and does not collide (`e29`) (Dickson, 2026-10-18). Such a window is then named and roled as MSAA gives it, where NVDA reads it through UIA; the difference is recorded in the parity ledger. Asking the host window's own outpost for those ancestors through UIA is not done (Dickson, 2026-10-20): the only family where the spoken result could differ, a WinUI host of WebView2 that NVDA reads as "title window", is not installed here and the difference has never been measured, while the query would add a hop, a wait for that outpost's readiness and a dependency on another outpost to the focus path. An NVDA capture of entering such a host that differs from Verbatim's announcement reopens it.
8. The focus-now answer at an outpost's start or after its replacement, at 2 seconds: an application busy for longer answers its focused element with UIA's proxy, which the proxy rule makes no answer, and the application's next focus event speaks, as it would for NVDA. Not met in 10 runs each of `outpost_crash_recovery` and `return_after_retirement`.
9. Core's tray dialog does not read shell items from verbatim.exe: it asks File Explorer's outpost for them as a query, the outpost reading them within its own application, so verbatim.exe makes no UIA connection.
10. The end-to-end agent reads the desktop's focus through MSAA, not UIA (section 4.5); `focus_by_automation_id` and `misspelt_words` keep UIA for the two scenarios that need UIA facts (`settings_system_page` and `notepad_spelling_errors`), and their slow answers are logged apart.
11. For UIA's late delivery of a new process's focus events (risk 2) and the burst of gone senders (risk 13): when a `WinEvent` focus in a UIA window has no UIA focus event following it, that application's focus is read (Dickson, 2026-10-18; after the rest of the focus work, Dickson, 2026-10-19). Amended (Dickson, 2026-10-21): the outpost reads its own focus when its worker sees the gap, since the worker alone sees both halves of the evidence, the `WinEvent` focus copy and the UIA focus marker's resolution, and reading there needs no round trip through Core and no new message. It waits 1 second from the first unanswered copy's observation (`UIA_FOCUS_BOUND`), and reads at once when the listener drops the UIA focus because its sender has no window above it. A drop that ends the wait is a verdict of NVDA's checks on the event, or the listener giving up on the application when its read's time ran out; any other drop leaves the wait to its bound, since such drops come in ordinary fast navigation, where the next key's focus event follows. Two of these are judgement calls of their own (Dickson, 2026-10-21): the drop whose event said it lacked the keyboard focus when the read's time was up ends the wait, and the one a newer event of its element replaced is inconclusive. A read answers only a copy in its own top-level window, and the idle barrier's answer waits while the wait is open. Recorded as an optimization outside the current scope (Dickson, 2026-10-20): the outposts make their lookup by handle once per window instead of once per focus, which would remove one connection per focus change.
12. The costs of section 8: in steady state the listener's read makes 5 UIA calls where the remote read made 4, and the listener's reading thread is woken about 1.8 times as often in Notepad's menu bar, of which about a third has an established cause. Dickson decided on 2026-10-20 to proceed without measuring the rest, because the change is required for correctness.
13. UIA's client side sends a parent window across the process boundary two `WM_GETOBJECT` messages with `UiaRootObjectId` and a nonzero wParam, the message a UIA connection begins with, when a listener call on a held element returns its child window's element: one in the nearest-window lookup (`NormalizeElementBuildCache`) and one in the read; an owner receives none. For a parent with a provider, UIA's client side also connects to it. The outposts' reads of a focus in the child window return that element too, so the same happens from the outpost's process. Accepted as a known limit, shared with NVDA, whose UIA client receives the same elements (Dickson, 2026-10-19). Its costs are risk 14's. Stopping the walks one step lower, below the child window's own element, works in mockapp, but has no general stopping rule: obtaining the window's element is what sends the messages, and the outpost that then reads it, for its name, takes the wait instead. So it is not built.

## 1. The problem

While a remote operation from one process waits in an application, from its arrival until UIA begins evaluating it, a new connection from another process to any window of that application fails without an error: `UiaHasServerSideProvider` answers "no" after about 3 seconds, and the lookups return UIA's window proxy after the connection timeout (`docs/uia-bugs.md`, "A new connection fails while another process's remote operation is queued"). A walk from a held element up into a window of the application from another process fails the same way (`e24`). Calls from one process do not collide with each other (`e12`); calls on held elements are only delayed (`e10`, `e23`, `e25`, `e26`); MSAA calls do not collide (`e29`); and two remote operations from different processes do not collide (`e27`).

In Verbatim, the remote operation was the listener's focus read and the connection the outpost's probe or lookup, or the reverse. A lock per application kept them apart, but a process's threads waited for each other behind it, the focus-now path took the wrong lock, and a stale answer of "no lock" let a thread read unguarded; Dickson chose to remove it rather than fix it. Each application has one outpost. If only that outpost sends remote operations into the application and connects to it, and the listener only calls held elements, the collision cannot arise from Verbatim's own calls.

Against mockapp, its window thread suspended for 300 ms beside another process's queued remote operation: a lookup by handle returned the window proxy after about 5.3 seconds in every trial, while the listener's whole classic read from an element 62 levels deep returned mockapp's own elements at every level, and the console check from a held text area answered correctly where the check by handle took 5.3 to 10.3 seconds.

A second form needs no remote operation: a new connection waiting in a busy application fails when another process's new connection waits there too and a third process has a call pending (6 of 6 against mockapp and against the collide server). Not failing: two connections from two processes alone, a connection beside held calls of other processes or beside the raw probe, two connections from two threads of one process beside a held call, and a connection beside another that was still waiting when its process ended. The mechanism inside UIA is not known.

## 2. The rule the design enforces

For each application, by process:

- The application's outpost may send remote operations into its application, connect to its windows by handle, and walk up within it. It does not connect to, send a remote operation into, or walk up into a window whose provider process is another. A window of another process that it meets in its application's ancestry it reads through MSAA, whether or not that window has a provider, without probing it (sections 4.3 and 4.4). UIA's client side still sends that window, when it is the parent of the outpost's own window, the message a connection begins with, and connects to it when it has a provider (decision 13).
- The listener makes no remote operation and no connection. Its calls into applications are calls on held elements: the event's sender, the element of the focus Core holds when the listener read it, and the elements its walks reach from them. Its walks up stop below the process boundary. It also sends the raw probe, a window message and not a UIA connection: the probe answered correctly beside a queued operation (`e34`), never disturbed another process's connection, and left no state a later walk could see.
- No other Verbatim process calls into an application through UIA, with the exceptions of section 4.5.

The rule restricts only new connections and remote operations. Plain calls on elements a process already holds are allowed to every Verbatim process: beside a queued remote operation they are only delayed, never answered by a proxy (`e23`).

Three things in the listener's process are not its own calls and stay:

- UIA's own focus tracking reacts to every focus `WinEvent` a process with a focus registration receives. It sends a native UIA window one `WM_GETOBJECT` with `UiaRootObjectId` and wParam 0 per event, the raw probe's message, and no message with a nonzero wParam, which is how a connection begins. For a window without a provider it connects through UIA's MSAA proxy (`h2`), so the listener's process does connect to applications through it. The first form can then arise in the shell's `explorer.exe`, which serves both proxied windows and windows with providers; it is the connection that fails (`e1`), so the outpost's operation is unaffected, and the listener drops such a window's UIA focus events at its native check, so no spoken effect is expected (risk 9).
- UIA's client side sends a parent window across the process boundary the messages of decision 13 whenever a listener call on a held element returns the child window's element, and for a parent with a provider connects into that process and calls its provider. They make the listener's read wait for the parent's UI thread (risk 14).
- UIA delivers the event's sender with its cache filled in the application; a delivery is not a connection.

## 3. Each of the listener's calls, and its replacement

In the order a focus meets them; `docs/crates/verbatim-outpost.md` describes the reader as built.

1. The hung window (`IsHungAppWindow` on the sender's cached handle): local.
2. The comparison with the current focus (`CompareElements`): both elements held, and it reaches no provider.
3. The sender's nearest window (`NormalizeElementBuildCache` on the sender): a call on a held element that walks up to the first element with a window. It needs no connection to the window by anyone. A failure is fast, 0.3 to 11 ms for File Explorer's early items. A call on a held element is bounded by the transaction timeout, not the connection timeout, so a busy application can make the listener drop a focus as having no window after 5 seconds. Its boundary: the walk stops at the host of the sender's fragment root, which has been the sender's own process's window in every case seen, but the walk cannot check that before it takes the step (risk 10). In File Explorer's first seconds it finds no window for many focuses, for any client (decision 2).
4. The native check's probe: the raw probe, in the listener and in the outposts alike (decision 2), the lParam sign-extended as UIA sends it, with `SMTO_ABORTIFHUNG | SMTO_BLOCK | SMTO_ERRORONEXIT` and 3,000 ms, exactly `0x10000` counting as a provider (Dickson's decision; `design-raw-probe.md`). It agreed with `UiaHasServerSideProvider` on 163 windows across .NET Framework and .NET 8 Windows Forms, WPF, Firefox, Qt 6, comctl32 dialogs, mockapp and File Explorer. Office and Java were not installed and are not covered.
5. The post-probe checks, made once per window when the probe finds a provider:
   - Windows Forms, for a `SysListView32`: the raw class name's `WindowsForms10.` prefix, a local call, which agreed with the framework id on 170 windows.
   - The console: the same reads from the held sender, the text area itself when its automation id is "Text Area", else that child of the held window element: its text pattern, its visible ranges and the first range's font. A sender gone before its check gets no answer, so the window is checked again at its next focus.
6. The repeat check's keyboard focus of the element of the focus Core holds (`CurrentHasKeyboardFocus`): held.
7. The sender's plain keyboard focus (`CurrentHasKeyboardFocus`): held.
8. The focus read: the classic read (`focus_ancestry_classic`), not a remote operation, without repeating steps 3 and 7: the previous element's keyboard focus, the sender's cache refreshed (`BuildUpdatedCache`), a list's or tab control's selection, then one `GetParentElementBuildCache` per ancestor until a known ancestor, the depth limit, the desktop root or the process boundary. Every call is on a held element; a step between two windows of one application is a walk up within one process, which a queued remote operation does not expose (`e23`). The remote read's second keyboard-focus check, for a focus that moved on while it was read, has no counterpart; a focus that moved on is followed by the focus event of where it went, which Core takes as newer. `uia.remote_operations` governs the outposts only.
9. The legacy MSAA state of a menu item (`LegacyIAccessibleState`): one property read on the held sender.
10. The console window's own element: no lookup by handle. The classic read's parent step from the console's text area returns the console window's element with its handle and title.
11. The walk to the top level stops at the process boundary, including a top-level window's visible owner served by another process, because the Settings content's step into the frame took 5.3 seconds beside a remote operation queued in `ApplicationFrameHost`, while stopping below the boundary costs no call.
12. A selection's or notification's nearest window: held.
13. The classic walk takes no lock.

## 4. The outposts' side

### 4.1 What an outpost may do

An outpost makes every call into its own application: remote operations, lookups by handle, the raw probe (decision 2), the console's post-probe check by handle on its own windows, and walks within its application. The Windows Forms check of a list view is the class name's prefix for the outposts as for the listener (section 3, step 5). It makes none of these into a window whose provider process is another (sections 4.3 and 4.4).

### 4.2 The Settings page and its frame

The Settings page's content (`Windows.UI.Core.CoreWindow`, in `SystemSettings.exe`) is a child of its frame (`ApplicationFrameWindow`, in `ApplicationFrameHost.exe`), which has no UIA provider. NVDA reads the frame as the window's MSAA client object: role pane, named by the title, presentable only with `WS_SYSMENU`. Its capture of the System page (alpha-58022, the pinned build) says "Settings", "Settings window", "Search box, Find a setting edit blank", and Verbatim says the same.

The listener's chain ends at the content's window, and the outpost continues it from that window's parent through MSAA, as it continues any chain that reaches a window read through MSAA. MSAA rather than local window calls: both read the same facts, MSAA is the object NVDA reads, and it does not collide (`e29`). Local calls are the alternative if a hung frame host is ever seen to hold the Settings outpost. The content was the frame's child whenever its focus arrived; were it its own top-level window, the walk would end at the root with no frame, as NVDA's would.

### 4.3 Other places an outpost reaches another application

Each is checked against the window's provider process, a local call (`served_by`, `docs/crates/verbatim-outpost.md`):

- A console window's owner by `GetWindowThreadProcessId` is the console's client, not the console host whose outpost answers for it. The provider process says which process serves it with no call into any application. When the host cannot be read from the client, the window has no provider process, no outpost takes it as its application's, and the listener drops that window's events rather than sending them to the client's outpost (Dickson, 2026-10-19); a console host's outpost still recognizes its own console window by the threads that created it.
- Core's routing of a console client's focus-now query resolves the client to its console host locally and asks the console host's outpost, and when the host cannot be read asks none, because the client's own outpost would otherwise read the focused element of conhost's window.
- The outposts' window filters decide by provider process, so a console window is its console host's and never its client's, and no outpost is started for a console client on account of its console window. The supervisor checks this where it starts an outpost for a process on account of a fact in a window.
- The outpost's read of its application's focused control is refused, answering no focused control, when the focus window's provider process is not the outpost's application, so a race in routing does not become a connection into another application; the check and `GetFocusedElementBuildCache` are two steps, though, and a focus that moves to another application between them is read there, a connection into it (20 of 20 with the order forced, `experiments/getfocusedelement-after-the-focus-moves/`), which `docs/design/focus-pipeline-rework.md`, "Open items", decides.
- The MSAA walk of ancestors, the continuation of a chain the listener or a focus-now read stopped below the boundary, and the outpost's own UIA walk for the ancestors query and a focus-now read's classic fallback probe no window whose provider process is another, and continue through MSAA from the parent or owner whether it has a provider or not (Dickson, 2026-10-19).
- A navigation neighbor that is the root of another process's window is kept as read, with no lookup by handle.
- Object navigation's step can land in another process's window, as the parent of the Settings page's content is `ApplicationFrameHost`'s frame, and reads the neighbor through UIA in the other process. Accepted as an exception to section 2's rule (Dickson, 2026-10-20): the step is user-initiated, one per key press, and off the per-event path. A stop before such a step is a follow-up.
- The tree dump for `verbatim-inspect` reads only the application's main window, which the filters find by provider process; a focus route whose window another process serves is not followed.

### 4.4 A window of another process above the focus

WebView2 content is served by `msedgewebview2.exe` inside a window of its host application. In Quick Assist, the web content's chain ran up to its `Chrome_WidgetWin_1` window, whose parent belongs to `QuickAssist.exe`; that window and the top-level window above it have no provider, so they are read through MSAA, as the Settings frame is, and what NVDA would read through UIA there is the same proxy. WinUI hosts, such as the new Outlook, Teams, Widgets and Xbox, put WebView2 under windows that do have providers, whose names and roles NVDA reads through UIA, the top-level window's "title window" among them. None of those is installed here, so that family is not measured.

A top-level dialog owned by another process's window is the same case: the listener stops at the dialog's window and the dialog's outpost continues the chain from the owner window through MSAA. The one owned dialog found on this machine is the Windows Security credential dialog when its caller passes its own window to `CredUIPromptForWindowsCredentials`. NVDA said as it opened over File Explorer's folder window: "unavailable", "Windows Security window", "User name edit"; its capture does not show whether its chain reaches the owner. Whether Verbatim speaks the owner again, for a dialog whose own window has a provider, is not tested.

For a window with a provider above the boundary (decision 7): MSAA reaches it through UIA's MSAA bridge when the window answers MSAA with it, which does not collide (`e29`), and through MSAA's standard proxy otherwise; neither makes a UIA call of the outpost's. What is spoken can differ from NVDA's: a window NVDA reads as "title window" through UIA may read through the bridge as a client or pane named by its title. The parity ledger records the family (`docs/parity/windows-and-processes.md`, "Content hosted in another process's window").

### 4.5 Verbatim's other UIA clients

- Core's tray dialog's shell items are read by File Explorer's outpost, within its own application, as a query from Core (decision 9).
- The end-to-end agent creates `CUIAutomation`, the older client, with no timeout; with that client a connection's second message waits until the window answers (`h2`), so an agent call into a hung application would not return. It reads the desktop's focused item at every scenario's start through MSAA (decision 10). `focus_by_automation_id` and `misspelt_words` keep UIA; their two scenarios are the ones the agent can still expose.

## 5. The timeouts

The connection timeout is 2 seconds, UIA's default and NVDA's, and the transaction timeout 5 seconds, where NVDA leaves UIA's default of 20; both are settings of the process. The case for 2 seconds is the hung window: a hung window named in a focus `WinEvent` holds all of the listener's UIA event delivery for twice the connection timeout per such event, 10 seconds at 5 and 4 at 2 (`docs/uia-bugs.md`, "A hung window named in a system focus event holds a process's UIA events"). A focus change in a window busy for longer than the connection timeout is dropped by UIA's focus tracking (`h6`); NVDA loses it too.

What the connection timeout governs:

- In the listener, no call of Verbatim's: it bounds UIA's own focus tracking. The listener's calls on held elements run under the transaction timeout. Since a read is several such calls, and a transaction timeout bounds each provider call rather than each operation (`docs/uia-bugs.md`, "A timeout bounds each provider call, not an operation"), the whole read has one time budget, `READ_BUDGET` (2 seconds), not counting the raw probe's wait (Dickson, 2026-10-20), because the probe can wait 3 seconds and would otherwise use up the budget alone. A focus whose budget has passed and whose window's verdict is known is spoken, with every check of NVDA's that needs no call into the application still made (Dickson, 2026-10-20); the repeat check is skipped, a deliberate divergence (`docs/parity/focus-events.md`, "Duplicate focus suppression"). The watchdog's `READ_DEADLINE` is 11 seconds: the budget, the probe's wait, one call under the transaction timeout and a second to spare. `docs/crates/verbatim-outpost.md` describes the budget, the deadline and the watchdog.
- In an outpost, its lookups by handle and its focus-now answer's focused-element lookup: at 2 seconds a lookup of a window whose application is busy for longer returns UIA's window proxy, which the proxy rule below refuses.
- In an outpost, its event registrations: an `AddEventHandlerGroup` on an application busy longer than the connection timeout returns success after the timeout and then hears nothing for about 2 seconds, and a `RemoveEventHandlerGroup` then fails and leaves the group live (`e57`, `e58`; `docs/uia-bugs.md`, "A registration that times out reports success"). At 2 seconds an application busy 2 to 5 seconds meets this, which 5 seconds avoided; measured, no registration of the outposts took longer than 32 ms (section 10, step 8).

The values, by Dickson's choice (2026-10-20): `CONNECTION_TIMEOUT` 2 seconds, `TRANSACTION_TIMEOUT` 5 seconds, `READ_BUDGET` 2 seconds and `READ_DEADLINE` 11 seconds. NVDA's flusher thread keeps calling, each call for up to 20 seconds, and speaks the full ancestry when the application resumes (`docs/nvda/main-loop-and-watchdog.md`, "What a live capture shows"), so an application answering in 2 to 20 seconds is spoken earlier and with less by Verbatim, its ancestors unknown and the repeat check skipped, and later and in full by NVDA. Moving the budget and the transaction timeout toward NVDA's values was considered and not taken. A read the user has moved on from is abandoned after half a second, as NVDA's watchdog recovers (Dickson, 2026-10-20; `docs/parity/focus-events.md`, "A slow read the user has moved on from").

The proxy rule. An outpost's lookup by handle is not answered when the element it returns is served only by UIAutomationCore's own proxies, in two cases: a lookup of a window whose verdict says it has a provider, and the runtime-id search's lookup of a window a kept probe found to have a provider and whose class lists do not decide it, because a window UIA by class, such as the taskbar's `Shell_TrayWnd`, may be served only by UIA's proxies. The test reads the provider description cached with the lookup (no extra call): answered when any provider it names comes from a module other than `UIAutomationCore.dll`, under whatever role. A test of the main provider alone would refuse File Explorer's child windows that answer the probe `0x10000` with the MSAA proxy as main provider under an override from `DUI70.dll`. An empty or missing description counts as answered, so the rule refuses a lookup only on positive evidence. Against recorded descriptions the test answered every case: 30 provider windows, 164 lookups that returned an application's own provider, and 50 that returned UIA's proxy in a collision. A refused lookup is made again only when a new event or query needs it, so refusals are at most one per such event, and a window's refusals in a row are logged at info from the fourth; a window whose application stops answering altogether is a hung application, whose events the listener drops before any call.

## 6. The verdict caching

A "yes" is kept for the window's lifetime, a deliberate difference from NVDA's half-second cache (`docs/parity/backends.md`), as Dickson decided; a "no" for 500 ms, as NVDA keeps both. The listener and the outpost each judge the window with the same code and keep their own verdicts; no verdict passes between them, and the outpost's decides which backend's focus counts in each of its windows (`docs/design/focus-pipeline-rework.md`, "Who owns each duplicated fact", the verdicts).

## 7. What the rule replaced, and the tests that pin it

The per-application lock is removed: its module in `verbatim-uia`, its guards around the probe, the lookups and `Execute`, its protocol messages and command-line handle, its table in the listener and the supervisor, and its tests. The listener's remote focus read is removed with it, since the rule allows the listener no remote operation. The supervisor's table still holds each application's process handle until the process exits, which stops a process id being reused while a reading thread serves it.

Tests, each meeting `docs/testing.md`. No mockapp simulation is built for a UIA fault that cannot be reproduced reliably; such faults are recorded in `docs/uia-bugs.md` (Dickson, 2026-10-19).

- The rule, in mockapp's `call_counts` (`the_listener_makes_no_connection`): the listener's real reader reads a cold focus, a steady one, one into a list and one to a list item, and the test asserts 0 connections each, read from the connection count `verbatim_uia::calls` keeps in `CallCounts`, not from the absence of log lines. The test does not reproduce the collision: the rule is that the listener makes no connection, and the counter shows that directly.
- The boundary, in `call_counts`: one mockapp's UIA window is made the child of another mockapp's MSAA window with `SetParent`, or owned by it with `SetWindowLongPtrW(GWLP_HWNDPARENT)`; the listener's chain ends at the child's window, the outpost continues from the parent or owner through MSAA, and a focus-now query gives the same chain. The parent's messages while the listener reads are exactly the 2 of decision 13; an owner's are none. A second test places the child under a window with a UIA provider and pins the outpost's MSAA continuation and the parent's connections by object id and wParam.
- The console host: unit tests of the provider-process function and of the window filters with a real console window from the dev-only `verbatim-test-console`, and an outpost test that the focused-control read refuses a focus window whose provider process is another.
- The proxy rule: unit tests over recorded descriptions. No mockapp test drives a lookup to UIA's proxy, because a lookup into a window that does not answer fails with `UIA_E_TIMEOUT` rather than returning the proxy, and the collision that returns the proxy is not reproduced by any test.
- Which timeout bounds a held read: `a_read_while_its_provider_is_held_fails`, with the connection timeout set longer than the test waits, shows the transaction timeout bounds a call on a held element. The reverse, that a read waits out a hold longer than the connection timeout, is not tested, since it needs the hold released at a chosen time rather than on evidence.
- The checks: the raw probe's verdict unit tests; class-name unit tests for the Windows Forms check; the console check from mockapp's console fixture.
- The gap read of decision 11: unit tests in the outpost and mockapp tests in `focus_reports` and `caret_watch`.
- The ledger: exact counts for the listener's read in `call_counts.rs`, and `docs/performance.md`'s entries for a UIA focus.

## 8. The costing

Calls per focus, from the ratchet (`call_counts.rs` against mockapp's `counts.json`), the classic listener read against the remote read it replaced:

- Steady state: 5 UIA calls against 4 (the nearest window, the comparison and the keyboard focus, as before; then the refreshed cache and one step to the known ancestor, where one remote operation read both). The minimum is 5 for a sender without a window of its own and 4 for one that is a window.
- Cold: 6 UIA calls and the raw probe, against 3 and the probe.
- Into a list: 7 against 4 (the selected item's two calls). To the next list item: 5 against 4.
- Per window, once: the console check from the sender, four calls on held elements where the check by handle made five, a connection among them.
- The outposts' calls are unchanged.

Wake-ups. Each cache-building call wakes the calling thread and UIA's I/O thread about twice, a plain read about once. Measured in mockapp, 1,000 reads of each kind: a steady-state classic read adds about 3.0 wake-ups and 0.14 ms of the reading thread over the remote read, and a menu item's, its legacy state read live, about 4.0 and 0.20 ms. Notepad's menu bar, six traces of each build on a quiet machine: the listener's reading thread was woken 2,423 to 2,670 times in 7 seconds before and 4,487 to 4,754 after, about 2,000 more, about 13 a focus at an estimated 150 focuses, and its processor time went from 258 to 290 ms to 345 to 379 ms, about 0.5 ms more a focus. The calls the classic read adds account for about a third; the rest has no established cause (decision 12). In File Explorer's list the reading threads were woken 17 to 27 percent more, in Settings' list 27 to 44 percent more; the listener's processor time rose 0 to 2 percent in File Explorer's list and 5 to 30 percent in Notepad's menu bar; Verbatim's total wake-ups stayed within the runs' spread.

Latency, from the event to its sound, P50 and P95 in ms, two runs before and two after: steady state did not change beyond the runs' spread.

- File Explorer, Down and Up held in a list of 120 files: P50 8.4 and 8.7 before, 8.8 and 8.7 after; P95 29.3 and 28.9 before, 29.6 and 28.3 after.
- Settings, Down and Up held in the System page's list: P50 8.0 and 8.9 before, 7.9 and 7.8 after; P95 28.0 and 23.9 before, 26.5 and 27.5 after.
- Notepad, Right held across its menu bar: P50 6.9 and 7.2 before, 6.8 and 6.8 after; P95 28.1 and 29.2 before, 27.6 and 28.7 after.
- A new application's first focus: P50 50.2 and 49.6 before, 39.1 and 40.0 after.

A new File Explorer window's first focus is slow in both builds, and not because of the classic read: the read's own calls took 1.4 ms at P50 and 8 to 10 ms at P95 over 360 reads. The time is the nearest-window walk into a window File Explorer is still building: over 11,077 walks it took 1.3 ms at P50 and 5.0 ms at P99, but a new folder window's first walk took over 200 ms in 28 of 93 runs of the new build and 7 of 14 of the old, up to 821 ms. The raw probe took 0.5 ms at P50 and 3.6 ms at P95 over 53 first sights, the slowest 211 ms.

## 9. Risks and open questions

1. The second form of the collision is not understood. The rule keeps Verbatim from making its mix alone; UIA's focus tracking for windows without a provider, the agent's remaining UIA calls and other software can still make it.
2. UIA delivers a newly started File Explorer process's focus events late or not at all (`docs/uia-bugs.md`, "A new process's focus events reach the desktop-wide handler late or not at all"): the listener's `WinEvent` hook saw File Explorer move its focus through every item while UIA delivered none, or none of the first 50 to 130, in some runs and in none on other days. Decision 11's read recovers it: when no UIA focus follows a `WinEvent` focus in a UIA window within a second, the application's outpost reads its focus itself and reports it, so a focus UIA delivers late is spoken a second after its `WinEvent`, and one it never delivers is spoken at all. Not recovered: a focus whose `WinEvent` is not raised either. NVDA, by its source, drops a `WinEvent` focus in a native UIA window and so loses these focuses (`docs/parity/focus-events.md`, "A focus UIA does not deliver"); that is not captured.
3. The classic read costs one more round trip per focus than the remote read, more in a list, and more wake-ups in the listener (section 8; decision 12).
4. A slow application holds the listener's read longer: each call waits for it, where the program waited once. The read's budget ends it, and it reports what it has (section 5).
5. Windows with a provider above the boundary are read through MSAA's bridge (decision 7), which can name and role them differently from NVDA; the family is not installed here, so the difference is not measured.
6. The content window of a pre-launched, suspended Settings as its own top-level window: not observed.
7. The raw probe's open items: Office and Java windows, and the memory it leaves in the probed process. Measured once (2026-10-19, mockapp's UIA window, as Windows reports its handles and private bytes): handles went from 116 to 165 after the first probe, UIA's server-side setup, and stayed at 166 through 12,000 probes; private bytes grew about 2.3 KB a probe for the first 1,000 probes, about 2.2 MB in all, then did not change. So the raw probe does not leak without bound; the cause of the retained memory is not established, nor whether the bound is per window or per process. The listener probes a window with a provider once while its verdict is kept, so in use this is about one retained entry a window. Not kept as a test (Dickson, 2026-10-19).
8. A request still queued in the application after its call timed out stays possible. Not measured: a remote operation queued by an outpost that has since ended, met by its replacement in a busy application. Its cost is bounded: the replacement's lookup returns UIA's proxy, which the proxy rule refuses, and its probe's "no" lasts 500 ms. This is the only case left in which two outposts' calls meet one application, since a console client gets no outpost on account of its console window.
9. UIA's own focus tracking in the listener's process connects to windows without a provider (section 2). Not measured: whether that connection, beside the shell's outpost's queued remote operation in `explorer.exe`, meets the first form, and whether it can be one of the second form's two connections.
10. The listener's nearest-window walk takes one step it cannot check in advance: the step from the sender's fragment root to its host window, which has been the sender's own process's in every case seen (section 3, step 3).
11. An outpost's probe is the raw probe and no connection to the window follows it (decision 2; Dickson, 2026-10-21), so nothing but the probe holds the worker at its first sight of a window. A connection by the window's handle after a "yes" held the worker 2,003 to 2,516 ms in 1 to 6 of the 10 new File Explorer windows of each `measure_focus_explorer` run while File Explorer built them. Measured on 2026-10-21, alternated, five complete runs an arm: without the connection every new window's first item was announced once from its UIA event, queued 62 to 323 ms after it; with it, every first item was announced once too, 5 of the 50 from decision 11's read, queued 23 to 2,280 ms after its event. File Explorer sometimes leaves the focus on its "Working on it..." placeholder and raises no later focus (`docs/roadmap.md`, M4, "Follow-ups from M4.1").
12. A registration that times out still reports success and starts delivering about 2 seconds after it returns; events raised in that gap are lost, every registration the process has on that window is silent, and a removal fails and leaves the group live (`e57`, `e58`; `docs/uia-bugs.md`). The listener's desktop-wide registration is made once at its start; the outposts register on each focus element, so an outpost meets the gap when its application is busy for longer than 2 seconds as the focus moves (section 5). Trusting a registration only after its first event would cost a gap of about 2 seconds with no events, and an application that raises none would never confirm it.
13. A new folder window's first focuses can arrive in a burst after their senders are gone, and are then dropped for having no window. In alternated `measure_repeat_explorer` runs on 2026-10-20 it came in 10 of 10 runs with no early outpost connection, the configuration built, against 3 to 6 of 10 with the outposts' probe as `UiaHasServerSideProvider`; its trigger is not established. Every client that reads those focuses late loses them, NVDA too by its source. Decision 11's read recovers the focus the burst ends on, not the items dropped before it: a drop for "no window above it" makes the outpost read its application's focus at once (Dickson, 2026-10-21). The mockapp tests show the recovery; the scenario runs of 2026-10-21, none of which holds a key down from a new window's first focus as `measure_repeat_explorer` does, did not meet the burst.
14. UIA's client side's messages to a parent across the process boundary (decision 13), measured in mockapp on 2026-10-19 (`experiments/the-listeners-read-below-another-processs-window/`): the listener's read waits for the parent's UI thread whenever it is busy, and drops the focus as having no window above it when that lasts longer than the connection timeout, with or without a provider. For a parent with a provider, with another process's remote operation queued in its process, the read waits one connection timeout more once the thread is free, about 2,325 ms at 2 seconds against 317 ms busy only, then returns the right chain, the queued operation unharmed; without a provider a queued operation adds nothing. The Settings frame's share of these messages, and a real WinUI host's behaving as mockapp's window does, are inferred, not measured. Accepted (Dickson, 2026-10-19; decision 13).

## 10. What each step established

The design was built in these steps, each with its scenarios.

1. The raw probe in the listener, with its leak measurement (risk 7). The outposts kept `UiaHasServerSideProvider` in this step, and in its runs some of their probes of File Explorer's `DirectUIHWND` windows took about 3 seconds, one answering "no" (`docs/uia-bugs.md`, "UiaHasServerSideProvider says no for a new conhost window while another process has an event handler"), which decision 2 then removed. `windows_terminal_two_windows` failed 2 of 10 runs on Windows Terminal's focus events under two runtime ids (`docs/uia-bugs.md`, "Windows Terminal raises focus from a second runtime identity").
2. The Windows Forms check from the class name, for the listener and the outposts alike.
3. The window's provider process as a function of its own, used by the window filters, Core's routing of a console client's focus-now query, the listener's routing of a console window's `WinEvent`s, and the focused-control refusal (section 4.3).
4. The listener's console check from a held element: the console window's own focus costs 4 UIA calls where it cost 5, and a text area's first focus check 2 where it cost 5.
5. The classic walks stop below the process boundary, owner included, and the outpost continues such a chain through MSAA; this step found the messages of decision 13.
6. The listener reads every focus the classic way, with the connection count and the rule's test. A read of an application that answers takes milliseconds; in mockapp a steady-state read adds about 3.0 wake-ups and 0.14 ms over the remote read (section 8). The Notepad comparison of the reading thread's wake-ups was not made, by Dickson's decision (decision 12).
7. The outposts' checks against another process's window and the MSAA continuation above a window with a provider (section 4.3).
8. The connection timeout at 2 seconds in every process, the proxy rule, and the timeout split into `CONNECTION_TIMEOUT` and `TRANSACTION_TIMEOUT`. Over 20 runs of `measure_repeat_explorer` and `explorer_folder_window`, the outposts made 3,621 registrations, the longest 32 ms, and 3,602 removals, the longest 18 ms, every one successful, so the outposts keep 2 seconds (decision 4).
9. The end-to-end agent's desktop reading through MSAA (decision 10).
10. The lock removed: code, protocol, tests and documents (section 7).
11. Core's tray dialog's shell items as a query to File Explorer's outpost (decision 9). The outpost answers within its worker's walk deadline, `WALK_DEADLINE` (5 seconds), and Core waits `SHELL_ITEMS_DEADLINE`, 6 seconds, that deadline and a second for the way through the pipes and the reducer thread, so the items read by the outpost's deadline reach the dialog; a query queued behind a busy outpost worker can still time out in Core, which is accepted (Dickson, 2026-10-20).
12. Decision 11's read after a `WinEvent` focus with no UIA focus following it (`outpost/gap.rs`).

## Follow-ups outside this design

- The `window_element` lookups (the foreground read, the tree dump and the focus route) apply the proxy rule by the window's verdict, so a window UIA only by class whose lookup returns UIA's proxies is refused there; whether they should require a kept probe, as the runtime-id search does, is Dickson's question.
- Object navigation's step into another process's window (section 4.3): stop before a step across the process boundary, judged with local calls as the walks judge it, and continue through MSAA from there.
- After Escape closed Verbatim's Settings dialog in one `rapid_tabbing_in_settings` run, Windows gave the foreground to Core's hidden frame rather than back to the desktop, so nothing was spoken; where the foreground goes when the dialog closes is Core's to fix.
- A double press of Verbatim+F11 opens the system tray list, as a single press does, instead of the taskbar list. Recommended: a second press switches to the taskbar list.
