# The focus pipeline rework

Status: scope and order of work agreed by Dickson on 2026-10-25, to be built in a session of its own (`docs/roadmap.md`, M4, "The focus pipeline rework"). The study of the present pipeline follows them, with file and line references into the code as of 2026-10-25; the process and thread model it starts from is `docs/architecture.md`, section 1. The scope was widened on 2026-10-29 to every reconciliation below and every difference in [the NVDA audit](focus-pipeline-audit.md).

## What was found

The pipeline's shape is forced: three measured facts about UI Automation (an element cannot leave the process that received its event; a focus registration is desktop-wide; a hung window named in a focus WinEvent holds every UIA event of a process registered for focus), decision D9, and Dickson's refusal of a one-process host on 2026-10-20 require a focus listener that reads every UIA focus, one outpost per application that holds everything else, and a Core that decides. What has grown is the machinery that reconciles the two routes and the copies of "the focus" they leave: of about ninety mechanisms, about half port NVDA or answer a constraint and about half make Verbatim's processes agree, and six of those were each patched three to seven times between 2026-10-20 and 2026-10-25, each round tuning a disagreement between two copies of one fact. The rework removes reconciliations inside the present shape; no smaller shape meets the requirements.

## Decisions (Dickson, 2026-10-25)

- Repeats are judged in Core. The listener keeps the last focus each application's reading thread read. A focus event on that element, judged by NVDA's own checks, `CompareElements` against the kept element and a live `CurrentHasKeyboardFocus` on it, never inferred from a cache, is not read again: the listener sends the kept facts, with the event's cached states, as an ordinary focus fact, and Core's duplicate rule drops it or takes it. The outpost does the same for a repeated MSAA focus on the object it read last. There is no protocol change. This removes `CoreFocus` and its recipients, the listener's held elements, the outpost's queue of unjudged focuses, the trace handshake, and the follow-up on reads a watchdog abandons with the fix approved for it, and it closes the hole where a focus returning to an element while Core's notice is in flight is never spoken. It replaces the judging before the read of the D14 rework of 2026-10-25.
- The wait for a missing UIA focus (the gap) moves into the listener, whose desktop-wide hook already receives every focus WinEvent beside the UIA focus, on one clock. At its one-second bound the listener tells the application's outpost the focus is missing, and the outpost reads it in its ordered stream, as now, so the order of the application's events stays the outpost's. This replaces the placement of 2026-10-21.
- The outpost's own read of a focus UIA did not deliver stays (decision 11).
- The hand-over to a new outpost holds the outpost's own hook copies until the moment forwarding ended is known, instead of matching the listener's copies by counting.
- Every observation is stamped in microseconds from one clock read, in every process, so the order of focuses is a total order.
- The marker that holds an application's later events until the listener's read arrives stays.

Added by Dickson on 2026-10-29, with the scope widened to every reconciliation and every difference in [the NVDA audit](focus-pipeline-audit.md). The aim is NVDA's focus logic wherever the multi-process model allows, with one owner for each fact and as few calls as possible:

- A window in the focus's ancestry is named by NVDA's rule: a plain window named by `InternalGetWindowText` for the desktop, a ghost window or a hung application, otherwise the window's UIA or MSAA object chosen as NVDA chooses it. When that read has not answered within Verbatim's existing bounds (`MOVED_ON_GRACE` when the focus has moved to a new window, the read's budget otherwise), the window is named by `InternalGetWindowText`, a deliberate difference.
- Core's second test of an accepted focus against the foreground goes: a focus is judged against the foreground once, as NVDA does.
- Where calls and judgements are made. Core makes no call into Windows or any application; the app thread of `verbatim.exe` may read only Windows' own state, such as the foreground window, which never waits on an application. The listener and the outposts make every accessibility call. They may drop an event only on what they observe of it at that moment, with no information from Core (the window is hung, the element no longer has the keyboard focus, the window is not related to the foreground, the window is UIA), as NVDA does before its read. They may use their own last read only to skip a call, and then still send Core the fact. Core alone judges anything that depends on the state it holds: the focus, repeats, the foreground and staleness.
- NVDA's two foreground tests (the focus already in the window; the focus's own event) are Core's. An outpost whose own last report says a foreground change repeats its focus sends it unread, with the window and the event's identity, and Core judges it against its focus, asking the outpost to read the window only when it is not a repeat, after any focus the outpost has already queued; an outpost whose own report says it is no repeat reads the window at once, as now.

## Terms

- The listener is the one permanent `verbatim-outpost.exe --listener` process: it holds the desktop-wide UIA focus registration and desktop-wide `WinEvent` hooks, and reads each UIA focus on a thread per application.
- An outpost is the process that serves one application: its own process-scoped `WinEvent` hook, its UIA property and text registrations, one worker thread, a watchdog.
- Core is the reducer in `verbatim-core`, the only decider; the app is `verbatim.exe`'s thread that feeds it and routes its answers.
- A route is the path one of an application's events takes to its outpost: the listener's (a UIA focus, read in the listener and relayed through the app) or the outpost's own (its hook and registrations). The two-route structure is that one application's events arrive by both.
- A judge is a process that applies a focus rule before an element is read: the listener or an outpost. Core judges after the read.
- The marker is the message the listener's UIA focus callback sends an outpost the moment a focus event arrives; the outpost holds every later entry behind it until the listener's read or drop takes its place.
- The gap is an outpost's wait of one second after a `WinEvent` focus in a UIA window for the matching UIA focus, after which it reads its application's focus itself.
- The hand-over is the protocol by which the listener stops forwarding an application's `WinEvent`s once its outpost's hook is in, and the outpost drops the second copy of an event both hooks took.
- `CoreFocus` is the notice Core sends the judges after every focus event it judges and every change of its focus, so they judge repeats and change targets against the focus Core holds rather than a copy of what they last reported.
- A verdict is a window's backend decision, UIA or MSAA, from NVDA's class lists or a probe; each process keeps its own verdicts.

## The constraints that force the shape

Each is measured or decided, with its record.

- A UIA element cannot leave the process that received its event. `CoMarshalInterface` succeeds and `CoUnmarshalInterface` fails in the other process, by every route (E-marshal, focus pipeline section 11; `docs/uia-bugs.md`, "Elements cannot be passed to another process"). So whoever receives a UIA focus event must judge and read it; that is why the pre-October design, in which the outpost found the element again by runtime id, name and position, grew the moved-on rules and failed (section 1 of the focus pipeline).
- A UIA focus registration is desktop-wide. The scoped form is undocumented, refused by the direct call, and misses every proxied Win32 window (`docs/uia-bugs.md`, "Focus events cannot be limited to one application"). So one process receives every application's UIA focus events, or every outpost receives all of them at 47 to 90 provider calls each per focus change (E-cost), which Dickson ruled out.
- A hung window named in a focus `WinEvent` holds every UIA event delivered to a process that holds a focus registration, for twice the connection timeout per event (`docs/uia-bugs.md`, "A hung window named in a system focus event"). So the process holding the focus registration must hold nothing whose stall would be heard; Dickson declined one UIA host process on 2026-10-20 for this reason (D13 as amended that day).
- A thread blocked in a cross-process call cannot be cancelled; only process exit reclaims it (D9). So one outpost per application, abandonable workers and reading threads, and the kill-and-replace ladder.
- UI Automation delivers a newly started process's focus events late or never, and sometimes after their senders are gone (risks 2 and 13 of the listener design; `docs/uia-bugs.md`, "A new process's focus events reach the desktop-wide handler late"). NVDA loses those focuses by its source; Dickson decided to recover them (decision 11, 2026-10-18 and 2026-10-21), which is Verbatim's one deliberate departure from NVDA on the focus path.
- `WinEvent`s and UIA callbacks arrive with no common order; NVDA restores one by passing both through one process's threads and one queue. Verbatim has no one process, so every ordering it needs it must build.
- Windows names a console window's owner as the console's client while the console host serves it, so one application's events arrive from two processes' windows.
- Two Verbatim processes connecting to one application collide inside UIA (`docs/uia-bugs.md`, the first entry), so only an application's outpost connects to it and the listener reads with calls on held elements only.
- NVDA parity for everything spoken, from captures and the pinned source: the acceptance test, the limiter, the duplicate rule, the foreground tests, presentability, the shell's and the switcher's rules.
- Verbatim's own GUI lives in a watched process, so its hidden frame must never be announced.

From the first four, the listener must receive and read every UIA focus and each outpost must hold everything else; Core, the only process that sees every application, must decide. The two-route structure is therefore forced, given Dickson's two decisions (outposts kept, no one-process host). Nothing below proposes undoing it.

### Counts

About ninety mechanisms. About twenty-five port NVDA's rules and about twenty answer a hard constraint directly; the remaining forty-odd exist so that Verbatim's processes agree, and of those about twenty trace to the two-route structure in particular (the hand-over and its ordering rules, the marker and the old-focus reorder, the gap with its news and evidence classes, the copied verdict, the element found by path, the chain from the listener's read, `CoreFocus` with the held elements and the unjudged queue, the stamps). ## Where state is duplicated, and how the copies disagree

Each item names the copies and gives one sequence from the code in which they differ.

- The focus: Core's `FocusContext` (`state.rs` 17 to 68); the listener's held elements marked waiting, held or done by `CoreFocus` (`focus_read.rs` 571 to 700); the outpost's `Held` with Core's focus, the unjudged reports and another application's observation time (`held.rs` 70 to 200); and the outpost's older `Tracking` (`worker.rs` 279 to 379). Sequence one, a correctness hole: Core holds E from a listener read; an MSAA focus in another application is applied and the notice that E is done is written; before it reaches the listener, E regains the focus and raises a UIA event; the reading thread finds E held and still focused and drops it as a repeat (`focus_read.rs` 1236 to 1323), so Core never hears the focus return. NVDA has no such window because its last queued focus is set synchronously. Sequence two, conservative by design: an outpost reads a state change for a report Core has meanwhile judged stale, and Core drops it; the cost is a read, not speech. Sequence three: `adopt_focus` runs as the report is published, before Core judges it, so an outpost follows and reads the caret of a node Core refused (`worker.rs` 1470 to 1479, 2372). Sequence four: a focus-now answer is pushed to the unjudged queue under the query's trace, and unless Core's notice names that trace it stays until a later judged trace drains past it (`held.rs` 104 to 108; `worker.rs` 4247 to 4257).
- The verdicts: the listener's arbitrator, one per reading thread and started empty (`focus_read.rs` 790), and the outpost's, which copies only the listener's yes (`worker.rs` 3861). Sequence: the listener's probe of a new File Explorer window times out and its focus is dropped as not UIA, which reaches the outpost as inconclusive; the outpost's own probe of the same window says UIA and is kept for life; the outpost drops its `WinEvent` copy and waits a second for a UIA focus the listener has already refused (`focus_read.rs` 455 to 464 names this). The negative verdicts also expire on two clocks, so one process can say MSAA and the other UIA within one event.
- Which applications have hooks in: the listener's hooked map and the supervisor's records (`owner.rs` 1419 to 1425). Sequence: a ready outpost dies; until `HookRemoved` reaches the listener, it forwards none of that application's `WinEvent`s and no hook holds them, so a menu or alert raised then is lost to both routes; the focus is recovered by the replacement's focus-now, the menu is not.
- The gap's "a UIA focus answered it" and the listener's knowledge: matched by observation time within 100 ms across two processes' stamps, and by top-level window for a read but by time alone for a drop (`gap.rs` 27 to 29). Sequence: a `WinEvent` focus in window A opens a gap; the listener's read of a focus in window B of the same application times out and is dropped with focus evidence; the news closes A's gap and nothing is read, though nothing is known of A (`gap.rs` 144 to 152).
- The foreground: Core's attention window, foreground node, latest focus's top level and the hook's foreground view; the supervisor's attention by message; the app's live read for the fake focus; the listener's test at read time; the outpost's test when handled and again when reported. Sequence: a UIA focus the listener accepted with its window in front is handled after Alt+Tab and reported `in_foreground: false`, and Core drops it, where NVDA's one earlier test would have spoken it. A foreground report for the window already holding the focus "changes nothing" yet moves the latest-focus stamp and its outpost (`reduce.rs` 655 to 664 against 856 to 861).
- The hand-over: the outpost's `hook_from` and `forwarded_through` against the listener's tick. If `ForwardingEnded` never arrives, `may_have_twin` is true for the outpost's life and the two maps are never trimmed (`handover.rs` 241 to 243); a listener backlog that replaced a waiting fact leaves a hook copy unmatched and misorders that tick.
- The last reported focus the intake reorders by, which lags a read taken in but not handled (`intake.rs` 136 to 139, stated as a limit).
- The event thread's narrowing pair against the worker's tracked window: a destroyed focus window is forgotten in the window cache but not in the pair (`narrowing.rs` 134 to 141).
- The states last read by the outpost against Core's, when the outbound merge replaces a waiting state change (`outbound.rs` 148 to 163; `held.rs` 197 to 207).
- Readiness known by the owner as it reads `Ready`, by the app when the frame reaches the reducer, and by the control plane.
- The listener's per-thread last focus against Core's: the next walk stops under a focus Core refused, so a container between two focuses can be reported or omitted differently from Core's own ancestry.

Eleven of these exist because one fact is held in two or three processes with no single owner; the gap's look-back, the marker news, the copied verdict, the hand-over's counting and the unjudged queue are each a patch on one of them.

## Which mechanisms exist only because of earlier design choices

The two-route structure itself is forced, as the constraints section says. Within it, these follow from choices rather than constraints:

- The gap's placement in the outpost's worker (decision 11 as amended 2026-10-21). The reason given, that only the worker sees both halves, is not so: the listener's desktop-wide hook receives every focus `WinEvent` and drops a served application's in its backlog (`listener.rs` 227 to 231, `Waiting.hooked`), and its UIA callback receives every UIA focus. The listener is the one process that holds both halves with one clock.
- The hand-over's matching by counting rather than a hold. Counting was chosen so no copy waits; its price is the seven ordering commits, because the two sources' copies arrive interleaved.
- Judging repeats before the read in the judges, which needs Core's focus there (`CoreFocus`, the held elements, the unjudged queue, the trace handshake). The repeat is dropped by Core either way; the pre-read drop saves two to three calls per repeat and costs the notice machinery and the hole above.
- The listener making several calls where one remote operation was one transaction, hence the budget, the deadline's derivation and the out-of-time paths. This is the price of the no-connection rule (2026-10-19 and 2026-10-20), which removed the lock; it is a decided trade and not revisited here.
- The reading threads ended by `HookRemoved` rather than by the application's exit.
- Stamps in two units.
- Core's four notions of the foreground and two of the latest focus, updated at different points of `reduce_event`, with three patches reconciling them.
- The in-ignored-window judgement in the app.

## The design

Status: proposed on 2026-10-29 for Dickson's review, replacing the recommendation of 2026-10-25, and revised the same day after an adversarial review. It keeps the shape the constraints force and every decision above, settles each of the eleven duplicated facts and each difference in [the audit](focus-pipeline-audit.md), and names what each change costs. Call cancellation stays out (`docs/roadmap.md`, M6.1): every bound below is one Verbatim has today, abandoned threads and `READ_DEADLINE` stay, and nothing here depends on whether a stuck call can be ended, so cancelling one later only frees its thread.

Terms used in this section:

- A plain window: what NVDA makes for a window when it makes no UIA or MSAA object for it (`NVDAObjects/window/__init__.py` 85 to 97 choose it for the desktop, a ghost or a hung window): role window, named by `InternalGetWindowText` (190 to 194), invisible when not visible and unavailable when disabled by its style, and focused when it is its thread's focus window (`GetGUIThreadInfo`). Read from Windows' own state, with no call into any application.
- A kept read: the last focus a reader read in full, with its chain: the listener's per application, holding the element, and the outpost's, holding the MSAA event address and whether it was a simple child. A cache that saves calls; nothing is judged against it.
- A kept read is current while its reader has observed no focus or foreground event in another top-level window since the event it was read for.
- The followed focus: the node whose text, caret and terminal output an outpost reads and on which its focus-following subscription sits.
- A read request: Core asking an outpost to read an event the outpost sent unread.
- The bound of a walk: `MOVED_ON_GRACE` (500 ms) from the read's start when the focus's top-level window is not that of the previous focus its reader observed, and the read's budget otherwise (the decision of 2026-10-29).

### Open questions for Dickson

Decisions needed, each with options and the recommendation:

- Who owns a window's backend verdict (the second duplicated fact below). Options: the listener and the outposts each probing and keeping their own, as now, which disagree; one table in shared memory written by every process, NVDA's one cache, which is still several writers judging one fact and needs claims, reclamation and per-slot sequencing; the listener owning every focus window's verdict, which makes every MSAA focus wait on the listener's reading thread; and the application's outpost owning its windows' verdicts. Recommended: the outpost owns them, the only process that probes; the listener makes no probe, skips a read only on a verdict the outpost has told it, and sends every drop it makes that way to the outpost, which judges it again. Cost: a UIA focus in a window the listener has not yet been told about is read in full once, five calls, before the outpost's verdict reaches it.
- The outpost's followed focus and the caret rework's baseline (the first duplicated fact below). Recommended: the outpost follows its newest focus report at once; Core tells it when it refuses a report while holding another node; and the caret rework's "the baseline's life follows the focus Core holds" becomes "a node's last read lives while Core holds the node" (`NodesHeld`), Core's choice between a new and a repeated focus deciding what is spoken. This amends that rework's decision of 2026-10-25, which relied on `CoreFocus`; the speech is the same in every case its design lists, and the same as NVDA's after a refused focus.
- Whether Core tells an outpost that its focus has left the application. Today `CoreFocus` empties `Held`, so the outpost targets no changes while Core's focus is elsewhere. Options: (a) a departure notice, which must name the newest report of that application Core had received so that a report already in flight (Alt+Tab away and straight back) is not undone; that is a trace handshake on a notice of Core's state, the pattern this rework removes from `CoreFocus`. (b) No notice: the outpost targets changes against its own newest report whatever Core holds, and Core drops a change on a node that is not its focus. (c) The outpost learning the departure itself: it cannot, since its hook sees only its own application's events. Cost of (b), in real cases: a change in a background application reaches targeting only after passing the acceptance test, which the outpost makes against the real foreground window before any read, on observation (`Worker::accepts` for UIA events and MSAA events alike), and which drops it unless its window is topmost, in the foreground's owner chain, or a `Windows.UI.Core` window under the active window. So (b) reads and sends what the acceptance test lets through from a background application's focus and its ancestors: in practice its topmost windows, such as a media player's always-on-top slider whose value changes once a second, one read and one message each; for an ordinary window left behind, none. UIA events from the focus-following subscription, which stays on the left node as now, are dropped the same way before any read. Recommended: (b), which removes the last notice of Core's focus but the refusal, and whose cost is bounded by the acceptance test.
- A repeated MSAA focus is sent unread. The decision of 2026-10-25 has the outpost resend the kept facts of a repeated MSAA focus; an MSAA event carries no cache, so every fact resent is as old as the kept read, and Core, taking it as new when its focus is elsewhere, would speak the kept name. Recommended: the outpost sends the event's address and the node, as the decision of 2026-10-29 has it send a foreground change, and Core sends a read request when it is no repeat. Never for a kept read that was a simple child, whose id NVDA never treats as a repeat (`NVDAObjects/IAccessible/__init__.py` 863 to 872).
- Change and selection targeting under the rule of 2026-10-29. The rule lets a reader use its own last read only to skip a call, still sending Core the fact. Sending each change unread would add a message and a wake-up of Core per change event of the application. Recommended: targeting stays NVDA's focus gate made before the read, against the followed focus, sending nothing for other objects, named as the rule's one exception, since the followed focus is Core's own but for one message's latency after a refusal, and while Core's focus is in another application changes there pass the acceptance test only from topmost or related windows (the departure question above).
- A UIA menu opening judged in Core. NVDA drops a menu opening while a focus it queued is not yet executed (`UIAHandler/__init__.py` 772 to 777) and redirects one whose sender is its focus object (805 to 817); both depend on Core's focus. Recommended: the listener reads the menu opening as any focus and sends it marked as one; Core drops it when a focus it took was observed before it and applied after it was observed, and takes it as the redirect when it names Core's focus. Cost: one read per menu opening NVDA would have dropped, against today's none. The alternative, the listener judging against its kept read, is the judgement from a reader's own copy that the rule forbids.
- NVDA's meeting of chains in place of the kept chain. A focus read with its ancestors unknown keeps the previous focus's chain (Dickson, 2026-10-21), which loses the window when the focus moved to another window (audit difference 1). Recommended: every report carries a chain complete to the desktop, and Core joins it to the previous chain where they meet, as `api.setFocusObject` does (`api.py` 111 to 147). This reverses that part of the decision of 2026-10-21: within one window, a container a walk cut by its bound did not reach is taken as left and said again at the next focus, as NVDA's fallback would.
- Where a walk cut by its bound starts its plain windows: at the outermost element's nearest window, included, where NVDA's `Window._get_parent` (`NVDAObjects/window/__init__.py` 287 to 293) goes to that window's parent and so has no window at all for a focus in a top-level window. Recommended: include it, the purpose of the decision of 2026-10-26; a deliberate difference. Likewise at the depth limit of 64, where NVDA jumps to the desktop after 100 objects.
- Hooks in after an outpost ends. Recommended: `HookRemoved` stays for the supervisor's decisions (the failure path and retirement), sent when it decides as now, and the listener also waits on each outpost's process, so a crash resumes forwarding at once; both keyed to the outpost, so a newer outpost's `HookInstalled` supersedes the older's signals. The alternative, the exit alone, leaves a failed outpost's application unheard during its graceful end, up to `SHUTDOWN_LIMIT` (21 seconds, `supervisor/retire.rs`).
- Proposed as deliberate differences: a UIA dialog's own text bounded by the read's budget, 2 seconds, in place of 1, rather than NVDA's wait (audit difference 9), as the decision of 2026-10-29 bounds the window name; the virtual desktop's rename unspoken if no process-scoped name-change hook is possible (difference 23); Excel's UIA cells, whose duplicate focuses NVDA allows (`NVDAObjects/UIA/excel.py` 114), left to the application modules of M5; and the synthesizer stopping all it was handed when any of it expired, an audio constraint (recorded but undecided).

Questions a capture or a measurement settles, all in the first step of the order of work: what NVDA says when its UIA parent read is cancelled; how long `UiaHasServerSideProvider` waits on a window that does not pump (difference 15); NVDA's speech for a keyboard-opened context menu (difference 17); whether UIA's raw view puts a same-process owned window under its owner (difference 21); which process raises the desktop window's name change and whether its text is the virtual desktop's name (difference 23); NVDA after a UAC prompt (difference 22); NVDA's focus-now onto a hung window and a nameless foreground window; and the audit's questions on a stall after an MSAA focus is judged, NVDA's object for a gone UIA element, File Explorer's `Start` window, and a repeated MSAA focus on a simple child.

### Where calls and judgements are made

The rule of 2026-10-29: Core makes no call into Windows or any application; the app thread reads only Windows' own state; the listener and the outposts make every accessibility call, drop an event only on what they observe of it at that moment, and use their own last read only to skip a call, still sending the fact to the fact's owner; Core alone judges what depends on the state it holds. Per process:

- The listener holds the UIA focus registration and its global group, the global `WinEvent` hooks with the desktop switch added (difference 22), one reading thread per application with its kept read, each application's gap records (below), the verdicts the outposts told it, the applications whose outposts have their hooks in with each outpost's process handle and forwarding tick, and the time of the newest change of top-level window among the focus and foreground events it observed. It decides from observation only: NVDA's checks before a read (the hung window, the acceptance test against the real foreground window, the console window's and the hidden frame's refusals, the sender's keyboard focus), the native check where a class list or a told verdict decides it without a call, the watchdog's publishing and abandonment, the gap's bound, whether to resend a kept read rather than read, and how far its walk may run. It makes no probe and no connection. It sends markers; reads, resent kept reads and drops; focus-missing; forwarded `WinEvent`s until an outpost's hook is in; forwarding's end; selections, notifications, menu and switcher ends; the foreground window for the keyboard hook; and the desktop switch.
- An outpost holds its hook, registrations, worker and watchdog; its application's backend verdicts, the only ones; its followed focus as one record (node, role, window, chain, the states last read, the MSAA event address and simple-child mark, the dialog memo); the per-node last reads of the caret rework; its held nodes; its intake with the marker wait and the hand-over's hold. It decides from observation: the acceptance test, once, for every event but a UIA focus; the window's readability, ghost and hung state; each window's backend, including whether a UIA focus the listener read or dropped belongs to UIA; NVDA's focused-state test and child redirect for an MSAA focus; File Explorer's search band refusal (difference 24); and, as the named exception, change and selection targeting. It sends focus reports with complete chains, an MSAA repeat or a foreground change it judged a repeat unread, each new verdict to the listener, and everything else as today; it answers read requests in its queue's order.
- The app, `verbatim.exe`'s thread and its supervisor, holds the single input channel, the held facts for starting outposts, the spares and the outposts' records. It decides nothing about a focus: the in-ignored-window drop moves to Core. It reads only Windows' state (the foreground window to route a focus-now query), stamps each input's arrival, and runs Core's timers.
- Core holds the one focus, one foreground record (below), the newest focus's stamps, and speech validity. It judges repeats, staleness, application refusals with the ignored processes among them, NVDA's two foreground tests, a menu opening's pending focus and redirect, a fake focus's duplicate test, whether a focus is new or repeated for the caret rework, where a new chain meets the previous one, the speech cut, and presentability. It sends refusal notices, read requests, focus-now queries, text requests and `NodesHeld`.

### Who owns each duplicated fact

Each of the eleven of "Where state is duplicated" goes or is left as the only mechanism of its kind.

- The focus. Core's `FocusContext` is the one copy. The listener's held elements and `CoreFocus` go: each reading thread keeps only its kept read, which it resends for a repeat (below) and reuses up the chain while current. The outpost's `Held` goes with its unjudged queue, its other-application time and the trace handshake. The outpost's `Tracking`, weighed three ways: adopting nothing until Core says it took a focus, a message per focus and Core's round trip before the subscription moves and the caret is followed; adopting at the report with no word from Core, which leaves the outpost following a refused node and targeting changes for a focus Core left; and the recommendation, adopting at the report, corrected by the refusal notice. Core sends a refusal notice when it does not take a focus the outpost reported and the focus Core holds is not that node, naming the report's trace and the node Core holds in that application, or none; the outpost follows the named node, or nothing, only when the trace is its newest report. Whether Core also tells the outpost its focus has left the application is an open question; recommended, it does not, the acceptance test dropping a background application's changes before any read, and text, caret and terminal following staying on the node as today. One message per refusal, none per focus taken. The followed focus is the only record of its kind in the outpost: the intake's `focused()` copy becomes a reading of it, and the worker's `is_focus` asks it. Sequence three of the study ends at the notice; sequence four goes with the queue.
- The verdicts. The outpost owns its application's windows' verdicts, kept as now (UIA for the window's life, D15, a deliberate difference from NVDA's half-second for both answers in `isUIAWindow`, `UIAHandler/__init__.py` 1368 to 1380; not UIA for 500 ms), and is the only process that probes them. A probe that runs out of time is kept as not UIA for 500 ms from its end, so a UIA read arriving just after it is dropped and the MSAA read already made stands. The probe has one bound, `UiaHasServerSideProvider`'s own wait once measured (difference 15), within the worker's deadline. The outpost tells the listener each verdict it decides that differs from the last it told for that window, through the relay. The listener's native check: a class list decides it with no call; a told "UIA" is taken as the native check's answer and the read goes on; a told "not UIA" skips the read, the focus dropped with its nearest window named in the drop; anything else is read in full and marked native-unknown. The outpost judges every listener read and every not-UIA drop by its own verdict, probing if it has none: a read in a window it decides is not UIA is dropped, the window's MSAA events speaking; a not-UIA drop in a window it decides is UIA makes it read its focus at once, stamped with the event's observation. So one process decides each window, the other only skips calls on what it was told, and a disagreement in flight costs a read, never a lost or doubled focus. The listener's told verdicts end with the outpost's process; the outpost tells the listener when it forgets a destroyed window, and a handle reused before that costs a needless read or a relay's delay, never a lost focus. Removed: the listener's per-thread arbitrators and its probes, its console check from the held sender (the outpost checks by handle), `record_probe`'s copy, and expiry on two clocks. One raw probe per window instead of up to two.
- Which applications have hooks in. The listener learns that a hook is in from `HookInstalled`, which now carries a handle to the outpost's process that the supervisor duplicates into the listener, as does a replacement listener's `--hooks-in`. It learns that a hook is gone from `HookRemoved`, which the supervisor sends when it decides an outpost's end, and from its own wait on that process, which fires the moment the process exits. Either resumes forwarding and ends the reading thread, keyed to the outpost; a signal for an outpost older than the application's newest `HookInstalled` changes nothing. A crash's window in which neither route takes the application's events (difference 25) closes to the wait's latency.
- The gap's evidence. In the listener, as decided, on one clock and with no call and no verdict. The `WinEvent` callback records each focus copy in a window of an application whose outpost has its hook in, in the readers' shared state beside today's backlog drop. A UIA focus event of that application observed within 100 ms before the copy or after it, whose cached window's top-level window is the copy's, answers the record as the UIA callback observes it; a windowless sender, which the callback cannot place, answers it when its read finds its nearest window, under the same readers' lock; so does a focus or foreground `WinEvent` observed later in another window of the application or in another application, and the listener giving up on the application, a timed-out nearest-window lookup included (difference 14). A drop for no window above the sender, which the callback cannot place, makes the outpost read at once. The record's one-second bound is armed by the `WinEvent` callback when the application has no read running or waiting, as for a new process whose UIA focus never comes, and otherwise by the reading thread when the application's reads finish with the record still open, and timed by the readers' watchdog, so no reading thread waits on it and a record answered first costs no wake-up. Unanswered at the bound, the listener sends focus-missing with the window and the copy's time; the outpost reads its focus, stamped with the copy's time, only when it dropped that copy as a UIA window's and has reported no focus in that top-level window observed within 100 ms before the copy or after it. A not-UIA drop may answer a record, since the outpost judges that drop itself. Removed: `gap.rs`, `MarkerNews`, the wake of `next_with_gap`, `DropEvidence` and its three classes, `Held.elsewhere`, the look-back across two clocks. Weighed against keeping the gap in the outpost: there it would need the listener's drop classes again; in the listener it needs neither them nor a verdict.
- The foreground. Core keeps one record, set at one point of `reduce_event`: the attention, the real foreground window, and the foreground object, the entry of the focus's chain whose window is the foreground window read with the focus, else the chain's top below the desktop, as NVDA takes `objectInForeground()` and falls back to the ancestors (`eventHandler.py` 402 to 411; the first item recorded but undecided). A foreground report for the window already holding the focus then changes nothing. Attention following a focus in the system's foreground window with no foreground report stays inside the record, as the only way Core learns a foreground Windows announced with no event. The keyboard hook's foreground stays Windows' own window, from the listener's report, and judges nothing. The test when an event is handled, the outpost's, or before a read, the listener's, is the one test; the test again when the outpost reports (`in_foreground` judged by `classify`, and `report_foreground`'s second look) goes (difference 12). `in_foreground` stays on the wire for attention. The windowless focus adopting the attention window goes, since every report now has window facts.
- The hand-over. The outpost holds its hook's whole stream until `ForwardingEnded` arrives, while the listener's forwarded copies are handled as they come; then it drops its copies of the forwarded kinds raised at or before the tick named and queues the rest held, in raise order. The tick is the listener's `WinEvent` clock when `HookInstalled` reached it, as now (`listener.rs` 1086 to 1088), and the listener forwards exactly the copies raised at or before it, whenever they are delivered, so each event is handled once; a listener copy delivered after the end is handled after held copies raised later, a stated limit. If the listener ends first, `UiaFocusReadsEnded` releases the hold, dropping hook copies raised at or before the newest listener copy handled; copies the dying listener held unforwarded are lost, a limit of the listener's death only. Holding the whole stream keeps a state change raised after a focus behind it. Removed: the two multisets, `TWIN_BOUND`, `ListenerBeforeHook`, `in_raised_order`, the raise-time half of the foreground change's removal of a waiting focus, `focus_raised_first`.
- The intake's reference for moving the old focus's state changes becomes the newest focus the intake has taken in, from the read in hand, which removes the stated limit. Core's `reported_focused`, which drops a state change of a focus that has reported itself no longer focused, stays as this reorder's companion and the only mechanism of its kind, and goes with the reorder if the marker's measurement removes it.
- The narrowing pair. The event thread forgets a window from the pair on the same destroy event that clears its window cache. One owner, the event thread.
- The states last read move into the followed focus's record; Core's copy is the reported one. The outbound merge's effect is checked in the order of work.
- Readiness stays: one event, `Ready`, observed at three points along one ordered route, each acting on what it has seen, and no rule compares two of them.
- The listener's per-thread last focus against Core's. The kept read is reused only while current, and Core joins chains itself.

Also kept, each the only one of its kind: the stale rule, the one order across outposts, with its same-top-level exception because one top-level window can hold several applications' content; and the background drop at a foreground change, the one stand-in for NVDA's queue cancelling a background event queued before the change, now in microseconds.

### A window's name, and the one fallback

The decision of 2026-10-29, as built here. A window in the focus's ancestry is the object a walk reaches for it: the listener's UIA walk, the outpost's MSAA walk, or the outpost's continuation, which goes on from a window by its object chosen as NVDA chooses it, UIA when the outpost's verdict says so and MSAA otherwise, and through MSAA above the process boundary (decision 7 of the listener design). A plain window is taken instead, with no call, in two cases only: the desktop, a ghost window or a window of a hung application, by NVDA's rule; and the windows a walk has not reached by its bound.

Where a walk ends by failure rather than by time, it goes on as NVDA's does: a failed or empty MSAA `accParent`, a boundary window that cannot be acquired, and a failed UIA parent step continue from the parent window's object (`Window._get_parent`), within the bound. The listener, which may not connect to a window, ends its read there and names the window to continue from; the outpost continues from it, as it continues a chain at the process boundary today. So after a failed `accParent` in a "Save As" dialog, the dialog's own object is reached and "Save As dialog" is said (difference 2). The depth limit of 64 is a walk's end by count, completed by plain windows.

Meeting the bound when a call is stuck. A call under way cannot be ended, so the bound is met by a publisher other than the reader, and only for a read that has passed every check NVDA makes before queueing a focus and is stuck in its walk: a read still in its checks at the bound (the nearest-window lookup, the sender's keyboard focus, the outpost's focused-state test) is left to the moved-on rule and the deadline, as today, so a focus NVDA would drop is never published early. The bound is measured from the event's observation on the shared clock, in the listener and in the outpost's continuation alike, so the relay's latency counts against it. In the listener, the reading thread shares its progress (the event's cached facts, the facts read so far, the ancestors read and the nearest window) with the watchdog under `WatchedRead`'s claim; at the bound the watchdog publishes the focus from that progress, completed by plain windows read on its own thread, and takes the claim, so the reader's late result is discarded when it returns. The thread is not abandoned for this: it reads the application's next event when its call returns, is abandoned only at `READ_DEADLINE` or by the moved-on rule as now, and so counts against `ABANDONED_LIMIT` only then. The outpost's worker and watchdog do the same for its walks, under the generation claim; an entry whose focus object itself has not been acquired has nothing to publish and is left to the deadline. Speech: the Windows Terminal launch says its window by its text 500 ms after the read began, whatever its parent step is doing.

Core's use. Every report's chain is complete to the desktop, so `ancestors_unknown` leaves the wire. A plain window carries its handle in the node's details, as do UIA window elements and MSAA window and client objects, and no other node does; a nearest-window handle is never put there. Core walks the new chain from the focus outward and stops at the first entry equal to one in the previous chain, by node id, or by handle when one of the two is a plain window; the result is the previous chain to that entry and the new entries below it, as `api.setFocusObject` builds the focus line. Entered containers are those below the meeting point. Speech is cut when the chains differ just below the desktop (difference 21). A focus whose walk reached only its nearest window in a new top-level window says "Windows PowerShell window" and then the focus.

Also served by the fallback: the MSAA focused-state test past a failed `accParent` continues along parent windows, a plain window being focused when it is its thread's focus window (difference 19); and the focus-now answer onto a hung window is the plain window, as NVDA's (pending its capture).

Costs: nothing on the steady path. On a fallback, about four local calls per plain window and one `GetGUIThreadInfo`, and about a hundred bytes per window in the message; a continuation after a failure makes the calls NVDA makes. Removed: Core's unknown-ancestor handling (13dcd2db, 2b13487b), the windowless-focus adoption and `ancestors_unknown` in the protocol, the outpost's focus path and the focus-now answer.

### Resent repeats, stamps and the hold

What Core takes from a resent kept focus. The listener resends its kept read for a focus event whose sender `CompareElements` equates with the kept element while that element still has the keyboard focus, read live, and only while the kept read is current; otherwise it reads in full. The resend is the kept node with its name, value and states from the event's own cache, as fresh as the event, and the kept chain, current by construction, so the stale kept name cannot arise. An MSAA repeat is sent unread. Core keeps two things per focus: the reading it holds, which a duplicate's newer reading updates, for review; and what it last spoke of the focus, which only speech updates, and against which name, value, description and state changes are compared, as NVDA compares with the properties it last spoke (`speech/speech.py` 768 to 781). A name change carried in by a repeat is then spoken when its event comes (difference 20). A resent repeat of a text focus still brings the outpost's caret read, so the caret rework's repeated-focus case is said once. A focus whose report marks it a simple child is never a repeat in Core of a held focus that was one, so a reused child id is announced as NVDA announces it.

Currency. In the listener it is one stamp: the time of the newest change of top-level window among the focus and foreground events either source observed, written by the callbacks with one `GetAncestor` on the event's window, from the `WinEvent` or the UIA event's cached window, never by the nearest-window lookup; a UIA sender with no cached window counts as elsewhere. In the outpost, a kept chain is current while its hook has delivered no foreground change since it was read. This fixes stale reused ancestors (difference 3): after Alt+Tab back to Windows Terminal, whose title a program changed meanwhile, the first focus reads the whole chain and says the new title, at the cost NVDA pays, the chain's calls once per return.

The time unit, first. Before anything else that compares stamps from two processes is built, every observation is stamped in microseconds from one clock read, in the listener's two callbacks, the outposts' hook and subscription callbacks, and the focus-now answer; Core compares only microseconds, in the stale rule, the background drop at a foreground change (`foreground_changed_at`, in milliseconds today), the fake focus's guard and the menu opening's pending focus. A report made for a read request, focus-missing or a not-UIA drop carries the observation stamp of the event it answers, never the time of its read, so the stale rule drops a late report of a window the user has left (the outpost's gap read today stamps its read, `worker.rs` 1399). The millisecond field stays on the wire, derived, compared nowhere. A `WinEvent`'s own tick stays only as the hand-over's identity of an event.

The hand-over's hold, as above; its cost is the relay's latency once per outpost start, on the hook's copies only.

The idle barrier. A read request counts as in progress in Core until its report has been reduced, and a notice as in progress until written, so the harness's idle round, and a key's evidence under `docs/testing.md`, wait for what Core began after an outpost answered idle.

### Mechanisms removed and added, with their costs

Removed: `CoreFocus` and its builder in the app; the listener's `HeldElements`; the outpost's `Held`; the "no repeat while unjudged" rule; `Reading::abandoned`'s keeping for Core and the follow-up on abandoned reads; the outpost's gap with `MarkerNews`, `DropEvidence` and the drop classes; the hand-over's multisets, `TWIN_BOUND`, `ListenerBeforeHook` and run ordering by raise time; the listener's arbitrators, probes and held console check, and `record_probe`; Core's second acceptance test, `report_foreground`'s second look and the intake's second hung test; `ancestors_unknown` with Core's kept chain and the windowless-focus adoption; the app's in-ignored-window judgement (to Core, with the top-level window's process in the window facts); Core's four notions of the foreground.

Added: verdicts told to the listener and the outpost's judging of reads and not-UIA drops; the process wait; the continuation from a parent window's object, plain windows at the bound, the watchdogs publishing at the bound, and Core's meeting of chains; the window handle in node details; the listener's resend and its currency stamp; the unread MSAA repeat and foreground change with Core's read request; refusal notices; Core's spoken record; the menu opening judged in Core; focus-missing; the hand-over's hold; the desktop switch and the desktop's name; the delayed pump, if its capture agrees.

Per focus on the steady path: two or three fewer messages, the `CoreFocus` notices, and as many fewer wake-ups of the listener's and outposts' pipe threads; no call added; one local `GetAncestor` and one atomic store per focus and foreground event in the listener; memory down by the unjudged queues and held elements. A repeated UIA focus costs today's two calls and one more message, and for a text focus the outpost's caret read; a repeated MSAA focus one message where it cost none, and a read request's round trip when Core finds it new. A foreground change the outpost's report says repeats costs one message, and when Core finds it new, as on a return by Alt+Tab, one round trip before the window's read, expected under a millisecond and measured before it is trusted. The first focus after a return to an application reads its whole chain, as NVDA. A menu opening NVDA would drop costs one read. The first UIA focus in a window the listener has not been told about is read in full, five calls, once per window; one raw probe per window instead of up to two. A gap record costs no wake-up when answered before its application's reads go idle, and one at its bound otherwise.

Per outpost start: the supervisor duplicates one process handle into the listener, which registers one wait; the hook's copies wait for the relay's latency until forwarding ends, measured at 0.05 to 0.15 ms at the median, 36 ms at the 90th percentile and 146 ms at worst before communication step 6; `TWIN_BOUND`'s timer goes; each verdict the outpost decides is one message to the listener. Per outpost end: one wake-up of the wait.

Fixed costs: one more global `WinEvent` kind and possibly one process-scoped hook in the listener; the delayed pump's up to 10 ms on a batch beginning with a popup start or foreground change, and one timer, which NVDA has too, only if its capture agrees.

### Edge cases, limitations and what must be measured

- Verdicts. Between the outpost's verdict and the listener's learning it, the listener reads a focus it will be told to skip (cost only) or drops one the outpost then reads (late by a relay, never lost). A window whose verdict flips is told again. Measured: probes per new window in `call_counts` against mockapp, and `conhost_two_windows`, `windows_terminal_two_windows` and `explorer_folder_window` ten times each with no focus lost or doubled.
- Hooks in. An outpost stuck without exiting is covered by `HookRemoved` at the failure decision; its stuck worker's output is refused by the live-incarnation gate. Events raised before a death and not handled are lost, as now. Checked by `outpost_crash_recovery` with a context menu opened during the replacement.
- The fallback. A popup owned by another window is placed under the desktop by plain windows, as NVDA's fallback places it, where UIA's raw view may put it under its owner. A thread whose call returns after the watchdog published goes on to the application's next event, so a second slow window can still reach the abandoned limit only through the deadline, as now. Measured: a mockapp provider stalling only its parent navigation for 15 seconds, with the focus arriving from another application and from within, giving "title window" at 500 ms, compared with NVDA's capture; a mockapp MSAA dialog whose child's `accParent` fails, giving its dialog; the Windows Terminal launch scenarios ten times.
- Core's meeting of chains. A plain window matched by handle to a UIA or MSAA window may differ in name; Core keeps the previous chain's entry, as NVDA keeps its old focus line's. Unit tests in Core per case.
- The resend and currency. A focus in another top-level window raised with no event at all is not observed, so a kept read can be judged current across it. Measured: the repeats per scenario, and a Windows Terminal return after its title changed.
- The unread MSAA repeat and foreground change. Between Core's request and the read the focus may move on; the focused-state test then drops an MSAA focus no longer focused, which NVDA, reading at once, would have spoken and cut. A foreground read answered after a focus the outpost had queued changes nothing, the window having been spoken as the focus's entered container. Measured: the round trip and the words of a return to Notepad by Alt+Tab in `second_application_and_verbatim_menu` and `return_after_retirement`.
- The refusal notice lags by one message, during which the outpost follows the refused node. With no departure notice, a background application's topmost window can have its focus's changes read and dropped by Core. An outpost unit test checks an older report's refusal is ignored.
- The menu opening. A focus observed before it but arriving after it, from another outpost, cannot count as pending, a limit. Unit tests in Core for the duplicate-then-menu case.
- The gap in the listener, the hold and the marker: measured as the order of work says.
- The delayed pump, the speech cut and the desktop's name are built only after their captures and probes.

### Why no smaller design

The recommendation of 2026-10-25 removed four reconciliations and left the copies of the focus, the two verdicts, the hooks-in window, the foreground copies and the audit's fallbacks; the design above replaces it. A one-process host and outposts holding the focus registration stay ruled out for the reasons in the constraints section. The two routes are forced; given them, an application's events need an order (the marker), a new outpost needs its first events (the hold), a missed UIA focus needs a recovery (the gap in the listener), several outposts need a common order at Core (one time unit), the listener needs the verdicts of windows it may not probe (told by their owner), and the outpost needs to know when it follows the wrong node (the refusal notice). Each is kept once, in the process that holds its evidence.
