The process and thread model it starts from is `docs/architecture.md`, section 1.


Amended after the ownership refactor, which is built ([the ownership model](ownership.md)). Three assumptions of the text below changed; the text is not rewritten. Repeats are no longer sent unread: no reader keeps a copy of Core's focus, every repeat is read in full, and Core alone judges it (`ownership-one-judge.md`). The outpost's commit point, its watch lock and lock order, and the watchdog's publication under a lock are replaced by the outpost's reducer on a thread of its own, whose timed wait keeps the bound, the deadline and the gap's timer, with workers running call programs on copies (`ownership-outpost-reducer.md`). The hand-over's counting is replaced by the hold of the hook's whole stream until forwarding's end, decided on 2026-10-25.

Its remaining builds, redesigned after the ownership refactor ([the ownership model](ownership.md)), and the title rule are in [its remaining builds](focus-pipeline-rework-remaining.md).

## In short

The shape stays, forced by measured UIA facts: one listener process reads every UIA focus, one outpost per application reads everything else, and Core, the reducer, decides. Core makes no call into Windows or any application; the listener and the outposts make every accessibility call, drop an event only on what they observe of it then, and use their own last read only to skip a call, still sending the fact; Core alone judges what depends on its state: the focus, repeats, the foreground and staleness.

The main changes: Core's notice of its focus (`CoreFocus`) and every copy of the focus judged against it go; a repeat of a current kept read is sent unread and read only if Core finds it no repeat; the wait for a UIA focus that never comes stays in the outpost, beside the verdict that dropped the copy, on one clock; the outpost's verdict is the one verdict per window, as NVDA's, and the listener makes none, skipping only a focus whose window's class is on NVDA's MSAA list; a slow or failed parent walk goes on through the window hierarchy as NVDA's does, naming a window by its text, with no call, when its object has not answered in time or the window is the desktop, a ghost or a hung application's, so every report carries a complete chain, which Core joins to the previous one as NVDA does; the foreground is tested once, before the read; a new outpost's hook copies wait for forwarding's end; every observation is stamped in microseconds; and each of the NVDA audit's twenty-five differences is fixed, recorded as deliberate, left to M5 (File Explorer's search band) or reduced to a stated limit (a crash's one relay hop, the secure desktop's untested half).

The order of work: measure and capture first; one time unit; the verdicts; the listener's read fixes; the gap; the readers' walks; the bound met when a call is stuck; Core's chains; read requests and Core's foreground; repeats end to end; the followed focus, removing `CoreFocus`; the hand-over's hold; the outpost's MSAA side; the desktop and the delayed pump; the remaining defects; the design records.

## What the study found

The pipeline's shape is forced: three measured facts about UI Automation (an element cannot leave the process that received its event; a focus registration is desktop-wide; a hung window named in a focus WinEvent holds every UIA event of a process registered for focus), decision D9, and Dickson's refusal of a one-process host on 2026-10-20 require a focus listener that reads every UIA focus, one outpost per application that holds everything else, and a Core that decides. What had grown is the machinery that reconciles the two routes and the copies of "the focus" they leave: of about ninety mechanisms, about half port NVDA or answer a constraint and about half make Verbatim's processes agree, and six of those were each patched three to seven times between 2026-10-20 and 2026-10-25, each round tuning a disagreement between two copies of one fact. The rework removes those reconciliations inside the present shape. The study's inventory of the ninety mechanisms, with their commits and the patch chains, is kept in git: it last appears in full in this document at 5b1e3d5a.

## Decisions (Dickson)

The aim (2026-10-29): NVDA's focus logic wherever the multi-process model allows, with one owner for each fact and as few calls into applications as possible.

- Where calls and judgements are made (2026-10-29). Core makes no call into Windows or any application; the app thread of `verbatim.exe` reads only Windows' own state, such as the foreground window, which never waits on an application. The listener and the outposts make every accessibility call. They drop an event only on what they observe of it at that moment, with no information from Core (the window is hung, the element no longer has the keyboard focus, the window is not related to the foreground, the window is UIA), as NVDA does before its read, and use their own last read only to skip a call, still sending the fact to its owner. Core alone judges what depends on the state it holds: the focus, repeats, the foreground and staleness. The one named exception is change and selection targeting: the outpost reads a change only on its followed focus or that focus's ancestors and sends nothing for other objects, as NVDA's focus gate, since sending every change unread would add a message and a wake-up of Core per change event.
- Repeats are judged in Core (2026-10-25; amended 2026-10-29). A reader makes NVDA's duplicate test only while its kept read is current, and sends a repeat unread: the listener a UIA focus whose sender `CompareElements` equates with its kept element while that element still has the keyboard focus, read live; the outpost an MSAA focus at its kept read's event address, and a foreground change its own last report says repeats. A kept read that was an MSAA simple child is never sent unread and never taken as a repeat, as NVDA never treats its id as one (`NVDAObjects/IAccessible/__init__.py` 863 to 872). The unread fact carries only what the event carries; Core judges it and, when it is no repeat, asks the reader that sent it to read it. Kept facts are not resent, since the listener's focus registration caches no value and no states beyond the keyboard focus, as NVDA's base cache request (`UIAHandler/__init__.py` 61 to 74). This removes `CoreFocus` and everything that reconciled with it, and closes the hole where a focus returning to an element while Core's notice is in flight is never spoken.
- NVDA's two foreground tests are Core's (2026-10-29), made on the unread foreground change above; a foreground change the outpost's own report says is no repeat is read at once. A focus is judged against the foreground once, before its read, as NVDA does: Core's second test goes.
- Backend verdicts (2026-10-29; amended 2026-10-30, from [the backend ownership study](focus-backend-ownership-study.md)). One verdict per window, as NVDA's (`UIAHandler/__init__.py` 1368 to 1380), so that Verbatim speaks a focus twice only where NVDA does. The outpost's verdict decides, for both routes, which backend's events count in each of its application's windows: it judges each event on the window NVDA judges it on (an MSAA event's own window, `IAccessibleHandler/__init__.py` 577; a UIA event's sender's nearest window, `UIAHandler/__init__.py` 887 and 1516 to 1545; each window of a chain, `NVDAObjects/window/__init__.py` 319 to 330) by the verdict that stood at the event's observation. The listener makes no verdict (Dickson, 2026-10-30): it reads every UIA focus that passes NVDA's other checks, and skips the read only when the sender's nearest window's class is on NVDA's MSAA class list, by the outpost's own table and code, a function of the class names alone, so the two processes always agree on it; M5's extension tables, which add to the class lists, are reloaded by both. The listener makes no probe and no verdict passes either way. A timed-out probe is unknown, which the outpost takes as not UIA, as NVDA takes a cancelled probe. The outpost probes a UIA window not on the class lists that it has not judged before it reports the listener's read there (Dickson, 2026-10-29), so a first focus waits for a busy window's probe, as on a Windows Terminal launch, whose UI thread is blocked for 0.3 to 1.5 seconds (measured in step 3's scenarios). The listener's reads of focuses in MSAA windows off the class lists, about five calls on held elements each, which the outpost drops, are an accepted cost, not measured (Dickson, 2026-10-30). A backend override forcing UIA reaches the listener for its application, which then skips nothing by class (Dickson, 2026-10-30). A focus is spoken twice only where NVDA's single cache speaks it twice: when its copy's window is judged not UIA and its UIA focus's window UIA, or when a window's verdict changes between the observations of its two events. Every window the outpost judges or acts on is first checked as served by its application, and another application's window is not probed, read or reported (Dickson, 2026-10-29); a window above the process boundary is a plain window, with no call (below).
- A window in the focus's ancestry is named by NVDA's rule (2026-10-29): a plain window named by `InternalGetWindowText` for the desktop, a ghost window or a hung application, otherwise the window's UIA or MSAA object chosen as NVDA chooses it; when that read has not answered within Verbatim's existing bounds (`MOVED_ON_GRACE` when the focus has moved to a new window, the read's budget otherwise), by `InternalGetWindowText`, a deliberate difference. The plain windows of a walk cut by its bound start at its outermost element's nearest window, included, and a walk cut at the depth limit of 64 is completed the same way, both deliberate differences from NVDA's fallback, which skips that window and jumps to the desktop after 100 objects. The reason for the bounds is reliability (2026-10-26): Windows Terminal raises its focus event before it has finished showing its window, and its one UI thread then blocks in `DwmExtendFrameIntoClientArea`, 0.3 to 1.5 seconds on the test VM's software compositor, so a parent step past the read's budget left only "<title> terminal"; every call into a busy application stalls alike, so the rule is not specific to Terminal.
- NVDA's meeting of chains (2026-10-29): every report carries a chain complete to the desktop, and Core joins it to the previous chain where they meet, as `api.setFocusObject` does (`api.py` 111 to 147). This replaces keeping the previous chain for ancestors unknown (2026-10-21).
- The gap (decision 11 of the listener design, 2026-10-18; amended 2026-10-21, 2026-10-25, 2026-10-29 and 2026-10-30). The outpost's own read of a focus UIA did not deliver stays. The wait for it is the outpost's, beside the verdict that dropped the copy, on one clock (Dickson, 2026-10-30): a record opens only for a focus copy the outpost dropped because it judged its window UIA, in every application, a new process's copies forwarded before its outpost's hook is in included (2026-10-29); it takes the stamp of each later such copy and of each foreground change into its top-level window that the outpost accepts, only ever moving forward; and it is answered by each UIA focus's outcome, by the answer the listener sends with it. A drop for no window above the sender has the focus read at once, whether it comes before the copy or after it, and a drop for no keyboard focus or for being replaced leaves the record open (2026-10-21). At its bound, one second from its first copy, timed by a threadpool timer set when the record opens and cancelled when it is answered (2026-10-29), the outpost reads its application's focus through UIA only, in its ordered stream. Another application's focus does not answer it, as the outpost sees none: the acceptance filter (2026-10-29) is replaced by the gap read's refusal of another application's focus window, the restamp and Core's stale rule (Dickson, 2026-10-30).
- A UIA menu opening is read in order by the listener, as the focus it is in NVDA (`UIAHandler/__init__.py` 261), and Core judges NVDA's pending focus and redirect (2026-10-29).
- The outpost's followed focus (2026-10-29): it follows its newest focus report at once; Core sends a refusal notice only when it refuses a report while holding another node of that application; no notice is sent when Core's focus leaves an application. The caret rework's baseline lives while Core holds its node (`NodesHeld`), Core's choice between a new and a repeated focus deciding what is spoken.
- The hand-over to a new outpost holds the outpost's own hook copies until forwarding's end is known, instead of matching the listener's copies by counting (2026-10-25).
- Hooks in (2026-10-29): `HookInstalled` and `HookRemoved` alone, as today, with no keying, since the supervisor's one writer sends an outpost's `HookRemoved` before any replacement's `HookInstalled`.
- The marker that holds an application's later events until the listener's read arrives stays (2026-10-25; amended 2026-10-29: it stays for correctness, with no measurement of whether it is needed, unless something around it changes).
- One time unit (2026-10-25; amended 2026-10-29): every observation is stamped in microseconds from one clock read, in every process, so the order of focuses is a total order; the millisecond field leaves the wire.
- Extensions (2026-10-29): a veto declared as a rule is judged by the listener from the event's cache; a module's code veto or redirect runs in the outpost after the listener's read, as NVDA runs `event_gainFocus` after its walk; no general read request is kept for M5.
- Deliberate differences (2026-10-29): a UIA dialog's own text bounded by the read's budget, 2 seconds, rather than NVDA's wait (audit difference 9); the virtual desktop's rename unspoken if no process-scoped name-change hook is possible (difference 23); Excel's UIA cells, whose duplicate focuses NVDA allows (`NVDAObjects/UIA/excel.py` 114), left to M5's application modules. The synthesizer stopping everything it was handed when any of it expired is not deliberate but a bug, fixed outside this rework.
- From the first step's results (Dickson, 2026-10-29): an outpost's kept read is no longer current after any foreground change its hook delivers; a focus-now answer onto a hung window speaks the window by its text, a deliberate difference, since NVDA says nothing; and a focus whose walk is stuck is published at the bound, a deliberate difference, since NVDA waits for every late parent call and, past the hung judgement, says nothing for the focus. A measurement run is at most 20 runs of a scenario.
- The outpost's read of its application's focus (`focused_control_in`), used by the gap read, the focus-now answer, and this design's desktop's return (Dickson, 2026-10-29): it keeps `GetFocusedElementBuildCache`, with the element's process id added to its cache request and compared with the checked window's provider process before anything else is done with it; on a mismatch nothing more is read or reported. Its local check and the call are two steps, and with the order forced the call connected into the application the focus had moved to in 20 of 20 rounds ([GetFocusedElement after the focus moves](../../experiments/getfocusedelement-after-the-focus-moves/readme.md)); the check stops such a result from being reported, followed or sent a remote operation, and the one connection it cannot prevent is a stated limit, which can meet the second form of `docs/uia-bugs.md`'s first fault or wait on a hung application. A search below the checked window has no race but makes about 16 provider calls for each element it passes, thousands for a late row of a large file list, so it was rejected. NVDA makes this call only when it wants the focus as it is then, at start, a desktop's return and a fake focus, and as one process meets no such fault. Each read logs its trigger, so how often the call is used shows in the scenarios' logs; the design adds it only on recovery paths, the gap read, through UIA only, and the desktop's return.
- Calls that reach another application (Dickson, 2026-10-29), the five decisions of [the cross-process calls study](cross-process-calls-study.md). A window above the process boundary, the parent or visible owner another process serves, is described as a plain window with no call into its process, because the MSAA continuation into a busy host held the focus below it for the host's whole stall and lost it past 12 seconds (instance 1); NVDA's object for the Settings frame in a focus's ancestry is a window named by its title ("Settings"), which it never speaks, the same name and role as the plain window, and the "Settings" said on entry is the frame's own foreground focus, which no boundary touches (`experiments/focus-pipeline-rework-measurements/captures/`); the one case that can differ, the content's focus reaching Core before any focus of the frame, says "Settings window" where NVDA says "Settings", the same name with its role, which proceeds by Dickson's rule of 2026-10-30 that a difference that does not change the meaning does not wait for him (amended by Fable on 2026-10-30, pending Dickson's acceptance: NVDA speaks the frame's MSAA client, a pane, as the capture shows, `experiments/the-settings-frame-comes-in-front-after-its-foreground-event/nvda-capture.txt`; "The foreground wait"). The listener installs its `WinEvent` hooks before its UIA focus registration and makes the registration on a thread of its own, because the registration waits on the foreground application for up to about 6 seconds (instance 2), and says `Ready` only once the registration has returned, since Verbatim's readiness to take input waits on it (amended by Dickson on 2026-10-30). The outpost's runtime-id search is dropped, and a node whose kept element is gone with no focus route answers gone, as is the selection's search under the expected focus, because the search enters another process's child window and waited 2 seconds on a stalled one (instance 3). Wherever the system picks a call's target at call time, the result is checked as served by the outpost's application before use, as for `GetFocusedElementBuildCache` above: MSAA `accFocus`, the MSAA focused-state walk and the walk-up step, because each can name another process's object after the check before it (instances 6 and 8). The second form of `docs/uia-bugs.md`'s first fault inside the listener's read, and the replacement outpost's refused lookup beside its predecessor's calls, are stated limits with the study's figures, because each needs an application already busy and recovers at the next event (instances 4 and 5). The listener's start order and the removal of the runtime-id search are two small changes made outside this rework's order of work.
- Call cancellation is out of scope (2026-10-29; `docs/roadmap.md`, M6.1): every bound here is one Verbatim has today, and abandoned threads and `READ_DEADLINE` stay.
- The alternative in which the listener makes no call and each outpost reads its own UIA focus was rejected (2026-10-29; "Why no smaller design").

## Terms

- The listener is the one permanent `verbatim-outpost.exe --listener` process: it holds the desktop-wide UIA focus registration and desktop-wide `WinEvent` hooks, and reads each UIA focus on a thread per application.
- An outpost is the process that serves one application: its own process-scoped `WinEvent` hook, its UIA property and text registrations, one worker thread, a watchdog.
- Core is the reducer in `verbatim-core`, the only decider; the app is `verbatim.exe`'s thread that feeds it and routes its answers.
- A route is the path one of an application's events takes to its outpost: the listener's (a UIA focus, read in the listener and relayed through the app) or the outpost's own (its hook and registrations). The two-route structure is that one application's events arrive by both.
- A reader is a process that reads an event: the listener for a UIA focus, an outpost for everything else.
- The marker is the message the listener's UIA focus callback sends an outpost the moment a focus event arrives; the outpost holds every later entry behind it until the listener's read or drop takes its place.
- The gap is the wait, after a `WinEvent` focus in a UIA window, for the matching UIA focus, after which the outpost reads its application's focus itself.
- The hand-over is the protocol by which the listener stops forwarding an application's `WinEvent`s once its outpost's hook is in.
- `CoreFocus` is the notice Core sends today after every focus event it judges and every change of its focus, so the listener and the outposts judge repeats and change targets against the focus Core holds. The rework removes it.
- A verdict is a window's backend decision, UIA or MSAA, from NVDA's class lists or a probe.
- A plain window is what NVDA makes for a window when it makes no UIA or MSAA object for it (`NVDAObjects/window/__init__.py` 85 to 97 choose it for the desktop, a ghost or a hung window): role window, named by `InternalGetWindowText` (190 to 194), unavailable when disabled by its style, and never focused, since NVDA's `Window._get_states` adds only invisible and unavailable to a base that has none (Fable, 2026-10-30, pending Dickson's acceptance). It is never invisible (Fable, 2026-10-30, pending Dickson's acceptance): a window not yet shown when it is read, as a Windows Terminal window at its walk's bound, would lose its title for good, where NVDA reads it through UIA and says it. Read from Windows' own state, with no call into any application. A window read so above the process boundary, or past a walk's bound or failure, that is not a ghost's or a hung application's is described as NVDA's MSAA client for it, a pane ("The foreground wait").
- A kept read is the last focus a reader read in full, with its chain: the listener's per application, holding the element, and the outpost's, holding the MSAA event address and whether it was a simple child. A cache that saves calls: the reader makes NVDA's duplicate test against it, while current, only to skip a read, and Core judges the unread fact.
- A kept read is current while its reader has observed no focus or foreground event in another top-level window since the event it was read for, and, for an outpost's kept read, no foreground change its hook delivered (Dickson, 2026-10-29: returning from Verbatim's own menu, which the outpost never sees, otherwise left 5 of 24 unread repeats on a node Core did not hold). The event being judged is not counted; in the listener, another application's UIA sender with no window of its own counts as elsewhere, and its own application's are placed by its reading thread, which finds each one's nearest window in order before the next event.
- The followed focus is the node whose text, caret and terminal output an outpost reads and on which its focus-following subscription sits.
- A read request is Core asking the reader that sent an event unread to read it.
- The bound of a walk is `MOVED_ON_GRACE` (500 ms) when the focus's top-level window is not that of the previous focus its reader observed, and the read's budget otherwise, both measured from the event's observation on the shared clock.

## The constraints that force the shape

Each is measured or decided, with its record.

- A UIA element cannot leave the process that received its event. `CoMarshalInterface` succeeds and `CoUnmarshalInterface` fails in the other process, by every route (E-marshal, focus pipeline section 11; `docs/uia-bugs.md`, "Elements cannot be passed to another process"). So whoever receives a UIA focus event must judge and read it; that is why the pre-October design, in which the outpost found the element again by runtime id, name and position, grew the moved-on rules and failed (section 1 of the focus pipeline).
- A UIA focus registration is desktop-wide. The scoped form is undocumented, refused by the direct call, and misses every proxied Win32 window (`docs/uia-bugs.md`, "Focus events cannot be limited to one application"). So one process receives every application's UIA focus events, or every outpost receives all of them at 47 to 90 provider calls each per focus change (E-cost), which Dickson ruled out.
- A hung window named in a focus `WinEvent` holds every UIA event delivered to a process that holds a focus registration, for twice the connection timeout per event (`docs/uia-bugs.md`, "A hung window named in a system focus event"). So the process holding the focus registration must hold nothing whose stall would be heard; Dickson declined one UIA host process on 2026-10-20 for this reason (D13 as amended that day).
- A thread blocked in a cross-process call cannot be cancelled; only process exit reclaims it (D9). So one outpost per application, abandonable workers and reading threads, and the kill-and-replace ladder.
- UI Automation delivers a newly started process's focus events late or never, and sometimes after their senders are gone (risks 2 and 13 of the listener design; `docs/uia-bugs.md`, "A new process's focus events reach the desktop-wide handler late"). NVDA loses those focuses by its source; Dickson decided to recover them (decision 11, 2026-10-18 and 2026-10-21), which is Verbatim's one deliberate departure from NVDA on the focus path.
- `WinEvent`s and UIA callbacks arrive with no common order; NVDA restores one by passing both through one process's threads and one queue. Verbatim has no one process, so every ordering it needs it must build.
- Windows names a console window's owner as the console's client while the console host serves it, so one application's events arrive from two processes' windows.
- Two Verbatim processes connecting to one application collide inside UIA (`docs/uia-bugs.md`, the first entry), so only an application's outpost connects to it and the listener reads with calls on held elements only.
- NVDA parity for everything spoken, from captures and the pinned source: the acceptance test, the limiter, the duplicate rule, the foreground tests, presentability, the shell's and the switcher's rules.
- Verbatim's own GUI lives in a watched process, so its hidden frame must never be announced.

From the first four, the listener must receive and read every UIA focus and each outpost must hold everything else; Core, the only process that sees every application, must decide. The two-route structure is therefore forced, given Dickson's two decisions (outposts kept, no one-process host). Nothing below proposes undoing it.

## Where state is duplicated

The study found eleven facts each held in two or three processes with no single owner; the design is organised by them. Each with how its copies disagree:

- The focus: Core's `FocusContext`, the listener's held elements marked by `CoreFocus`, the outpost's `Held` (Core's focus, the reports Core has not judged, another application's observation time) and the outpost's older `Tracking`. A focus returning to an element while Core's notice is in flight is dropped as a repeat and never spoken; an outpost follows and reads the caret of a node Core refused; a focus-now answer's entry waits in the unjudged queue until a later judged trace drains it.
- The verdicts: the listener's, one arbitrator per reading thread, and the outpost's, which copies only the listener's "yes". A probe that times out in one process and answers UIA in the other leaves a focus a second late through the gap; negative verdicts expire on two clocks.
- Which applications have hooks in: the listener's map and the supervisor's records. Until `HookRemoved` reaches the listener after an outpost dies, neither route takes the application's events.
- The gap's evidence: the outpost matched the listener's drops to its own copies by time across two processes' stamps, so a drop in another window could close a gap with nothing read.
- The foreground: Core's attention, foreground node, latest focus's top level and the keyboard hook's view; the supervisor's attention; the listener's test before a read and the outpost's tests when an event is handled and again when it is reported, so a focus the listener accepted could be refused by the second test after Alt+Tab, where NVDA's one test would have spoken it.
- The hand-over: the outpost's counts of unmatched copies against the listener's tick, never trimmed when forwarding's end does not arrive.
- The last reported focus the intake reorders by, which lags a read taken in but not handled.
- The event thread's narrowing pair against the worker's tracked window: a destroyed focus window forgotten in one but not the other.
- The states last read by the outpost against Core's, when the outbound merge replaces a waiting state change.
- Readiness, known by the owner as it reads `Ready`, by the app when the frame reaches the reducer, and by the control plane.
- The listener's per-thread last focus against Core's: the next walk stops under a focus Core refused, so a container can be reported or omitted differently from Core's own chain.

## The design

### Where calls and judgements are made

By the decision of 2026-10-29, per process:

- The listener holds the UIA focus registration and its global group, the global `WinEvent` hooks with the desktop switch added (difference 22), one reading thread per application with its kept read and its newest unread repeat, the applications whose outposts have their hooks in with each one's forwarding tick, and the time of the newest change of top-level window among the focus and foreground events it observed. It decides from observation only: NVDA's checks before a read (the hung window, the native check by NVDA's MSAA class list alone, the probe and the post-probe class checks being the outpost's, NVDA's duplicate test against its kept element, the acceptance test against the real foreground window, the console window's and the hidden frame's refusals, the sender's keyboard focus), the rules M5 declares from the event's cache, the watchdog's publishing and abandonment, and how far its walk may run. It makes no connection. A UIA menu opening it reads in order, as the focus it is. It sends markers; reads, unread repeats and drops, each drop with its answer to its outpost's gap record and its top-level window; reads Core asks for; forwarded `WinEvent`s until an outpost's hook is in; forwarding's end; selections, notifications, menu and switcher ends; the foreground window for the keyboard hook; and the desktop switch.
- An outpost holds its hook, registrations, worker and watchdog; the one backend verdict per window of its application; its followed focus as one record (node, role, window, chain, the states last read, the MSAA event address and simple-child mark, the dialog memo); the per-node last reads of the caret rework; its held nodes; its intake with the marker wait, the gap record and its bound, and the hand-over's hold. It decides from observation: the acceptance test, once, for every event but a UIA focus; the window's readability, ghost and hung state; each window's backend, for both routes, each event judged by the verdict that stood at its observation, a listener's read dropped in a window it judges not UIA; the gap record's answers and restamps, and its read through UIA only; NVDA's focused-state test and child redirect for an MSAA focus; M5's node and event rules and its modules' code vetoes and redirects, after the read, on every report it emits; and, as the named exception, change and selection targeting. It sends focus reports with complete chains, an MSAA repeat or a foreground change it judged a repeat unread, and everything else as today; it answers read requests in its queue's order.
- The app, `verbatim.exe`'s thread and its supervisor, holds the single input channel, the held facts for starting outposts, the spares and the outposts' records. It decides nothing about a focus: the in-ignored-window drop moves to Core. It reads only Windows' state (the foreground window to route a focus-now query), stamps each input's arrival, and runs Core's timers.
- Core holds the one focus, one foreground record (below), the newest focus's stamps, and speech validity. ### The outpost's commit point

Designed 2026-10-30, with Dickson's decisions of that day and the amendments of the combined review (Fable, 2026-10-30, pending Dickson's acceptance). It replaces the bound step's per-site generation checks (a9393c04, 45379c5c, c47d57ae, 6dbcca25), because CLAUDE.md's three-rounds rule was reached on that mechanism: a check followed by a write was not atomic, several writes came before a send that could be refused, and some came after a call with no send at all. One place where writes take effect closes all three.

Follow state is what the outpost follows and what it has told Core of it: the followed focus with its chain, window, role, batch, dialog memo and value-from-selection mark; the subscription's target; a caret key's open watch; each terminal's reading state and last sent read; the caret-read stamp; the focus path; the intake's marks about the focus; and what the judgement list records. The rules:

- Follow state changes only in a commit, `Watch::commit`: under the watch lock it checks that the committing worker generation is in charge, and only then applies a value of effects (`Effects`) and sends that value's messages; otherwise it does nothing. So the in-charge check is atomic with every change, and no write site needs a check of its own. The one exception is the release of nodes Core no longer holds, which makes the same check under the watch lock and must take the outbound queue's lock with the registries' inside it, which no commit takes; it removes the released nodes' terminal entries.
- A commit records the report in the judgement list first, then sends, then applies the rest, all in one hold, so Core's judgement of a report always finds it and a commit's messages and state are ordered against every other commit, the watchdog's included.
- Follow state that only worker threads read lives in `WatchState`, so a commit takes one lock. Facts other threads read stay in their own structures, and a commit takes their locks nested inside the watch lock, in the order: the watch lock, the judgement list, the intake, the outbound queue, the UI Automation registry, the MSAA registry. Nothing holding any of these takes the watch lock or calls into an application. The text caches come before the watch lock: they may be held when it is taken, and a commit never takes them.
- No lock a commit takes is held across a call, since the watchdog needs the watch lock to abandon a worker. A terminal read therefore works on a copy of the terminal's entry, taken under the watch lock, and only the commit of its result replaces the entry (Dickson, 2026-10-30); an abandoned worker's copy is dropped.
- A commit's apply code takes the state and the nested locks only: what an accessor of moved state would compute, such as the subscription's group or whether the focus is a terminal, is computed before the commit and carried in `Effects`.
- `Watch::advance` answers whether the walk's worker is still in charge, and both walks stop at their next step on a no, so an abandoned walk ends at the one call it was stuck in.
- Owed work is the calls the followed focus still needs after its commit: a terminal's first read, with its caret read for a terminal published at its bound, and a `SensitiveSlider`'s lookup for a focus published at its bound. The commit that makes a focus followed records it; the worker in charge attempts it once, under a deadline of its own with no moved-on window, and its result's commit clears it, as does a newer focus's commit or the watchdog's abandonment of the worker attempting it. So a replacement never hangs again on a terminal still hung, and the queue waits at most one deadline behind owed work.
- A worker whose commit is refused, or whose walk returns abandoned, makes no further call.
- On every wake the watchdog publishes an armed walk before it abandons, in one hold of the watch lock: when the walk is past its bound, and whenever an abandonment is due, the moved-on rule's included, whatever the walk's bound (Dickson, 2026-10-30, "publish first"). The publication is a commit that makes the published focus the followed one and records its owed work, so nothing is handed over: a replacement's first act is the owed work, read from committed state. A focus published at its bound is followed from the bound (Dickson, 2026-10-30).
- Displaced values, such as a terminal entry replaced, an element the subscription is not moved to, or released nodes' objects, are dropped after the lock is let go, since a Release can call into the application, and never on the watchdog's thread: anything a publication displaces is left for the worker in charge to drop after its next commit.
- A publication is built under the watch lock from the walk's progress as it stands, with local calls only, so it includes every step the walk took in before it, and no worker's commit falls between its build and its commit.
- An entry's result ends its query, so a query never gets a second reply, but not its deadline: a call that hangs after the result is abandoned at the entry's deadline as one before it is.

The walk's return stays a step of its own, `Watch::walk_returned`, so a slow caret read after the walk does not become a publication at the bound. It answers disarmed, published at the bound, past its bound and still armed (the worker then publishes through the same commit the watchdog makes), or abandoned. After either published answer the worker sets its entry's reported flag, which is worker-local, so a focus group's older entries are not tried, as NVDA's pump stops at the first focus it processes.

The registries' element caches, the text caches and the gap record keep their direct writes: each is a cache of what the application said, or a record ordered by observation stamp whose late write is the one an on-time write would have made. A UI Automation registry eviction is "evict only if the kept element is the one that failed", compared by agile reference, so a late eviction never removes an element a replacement has since kept.

What changes in speech: a focus is spoken where today it is lost, when its bound and an abandonment fall due on one wake, or the moved-on rule abandons its worker before a same-window bound; after a late gap read the subscription stays on the followed focus; the judgement list never holds a focus that was not sent; the caret-read stamp counts only committed reads; and a terminal whose owed first read is abandoned gets none, as with a failed first read today. Every other focus, focus-now answer and foreground report sends the same messages in the same order.

Limitations: an abandoned worker still finishes the one call it was stuck in; a focus group's older entries, held in the abandoned worker's planned group, are lost with it; a terminal whose owed first read is abandoned has none. The mutex acquisitions on the worker's path, the cost this changes, are in `docs/performance.md`, "The outpost's commit point".

### One object, one identity

Designed 2026-10-30 at Dickson's request, because "one object, two node ids" kept causing defects; amended by the combined review (Fable, 2026-10-30, pending Dickson's acceptance). Two node snapshots are the same object exactly when they have the same node id, or both are window nodes (`NodeDetails::window` set) carrying the same window handle. The rule lives once, in `verbatim-model`, on a small key, `ObjectKey`, so Core's meeting of chains, its foreground record and the speech manager's validity test use one function. Nothing else in Core or the speech manager decides identity, with one exception: `reads_the_same`, the silent re-read of a dead outpost's focus by its replacement, which compares the focus's reading and has no NVDA counterpart.

- Within one outpost generation, the outpost decides, by the node id it gives, since only it holds the live objects NVDA's comparisons read: a UI Automation element keeps the node its runtime id has, and an MSAA sighting is given a kept node's id only when NVDA's `IAccessible._isEqual` holds ("MSAA object equality"). A UI Automation element and an MSAA object are never one node, as NVDA's classes never compare equal.
- Across outposts, Core decides by window handle alone: no other object can be the same across two processes. A UI Automation window element and an MSAA client or plain window of one handle are one object, a deliberate difference, since Verbatim's two outposts describe one window two ways where NVDA, in one process, reaches it once.
- Speech validity compares by the rule: `FocusValidity` carries the node's window handle, and `FocusNow`'s focus, ancestors and foreground are `ObjectKey`s, as is Core's foreground record. So a window's focus speech reported by one outpost holds while another outpost's node for that window is the focus, an ancestor or the foreground.
- A reused UI Automation runtime id keeps its node. The listener's evidence of the element it read before under that runtime id is three-valued: focused, not focused, or gone (its keyboard-focus read failed other than by time). There is no evidence when it held no such element, or when its read was cut at its bound or timed out before that element's read. On not focused or gone, the focus report's commit evicts the kept element, if it is still the one kept, compared by agile reference, dropped after the lock, and the subscription is not moved to it; on gone the node's text caches and its no-text-pattern entry are dropped before the focus's caret read, a cache's direct write, so that read fetches the new element's pattern, since the commit comes after the read and cannot take the text anchors' lock (Fable, 2026-10-30, pending Dickson's acceptance), and the commit drops its terminal entry. The subscription's target is compared by node, group and element, so a node whose element was replaced is followed again. Core takes a report as a duplicate only when its node is the one Core holds and the evidence is focused; with no evidence, the same node still alive, as today; and on gone it resets its caret and terminal state for the node. A report whose evidence is not focused or gone is not the focus Core holds, so the two lines meet above it. NVDA's `isLastFocusObj` is `CompareElements`, so a terminal that lost and regained the focus keeps its first announcement, where the reissue cut it (`windows_terminal_two_windows`, run 4 of the chains step).
- A replacement outpost's first focus that is not a silent re-read meets Core's line at the first window node by handle: windows are not announced again, windowless containers below them are (Dickson accepted re-announcing; NVDA has no counterpart).

Limitations: a navigator left on a node whose runtime id was reused reaches the new element; on not focused the node's text caches are kept, so a text focus's caret read uses the text pattern fetched from the element that lost the focus; an MSAA window object and its client, one handle, are one object; overlay classes are not compared. Costs: `FocusValidity` grows by 16 bytes and each `FocusNow` entry from 8 to 24 bytes; validity makes at most two integer comparisons per entry where it made one; the report gains one byte of evidence; no call, message or wake-up is added.

### MSAA object equality

Designed 2026-10-30, comparing location now by Dickson's decision of that day; amended by the combined review (Fable, 2026-10-30, pending Dickson's acceptance). It replaces Core's role-and-name fallback (`same_by_role_and_name`, `same_entry`, `quiet_entered`, `FocusLine::quiet`), which undoes that part of 9fd28a66: what is below the meeting point is entered, as in NVDA.

The outpost compares each MSAA object a focus walk reads, the focus included, that COM identity or its address does not already place, with the candidates: the kept nodes of Core's focus line, as this outpost knows it (`HeldFocus::for_outpost`), and of the newest focus it reported that Core has not judged, in the sighting's window. Each line is scanned from its innermost entry outward, NVDA's meeting order, and the first candidate that is equal gives its id. A candidate's facts come from the registry's entry for its node. NVDA's order (`IAccessible._isEqual`) with its role and location steps swapped, for each candidate; NVDA compares the location before the role, and since each step only rejects, the swap changes no verdict and saves the location reads of a candidate of another role:

1. different child ids: different;
2. both IAccessible2: NVDA's unique-ID step, from M7, when Verbatim reads `IAccessible2`;
3. the event window, event object id and event child id, each where both are known: one differs, different;
4. different windows: different;
5. different identity strings, absence included where both asked: different (the focus, acquired at its event's address, and a parent placed in no window do not ask);
6. different raw roles: different, compared before the location, which needs reads;
7. the location: the sighting's `accLocation`, read once per sighting when a candidate has passed the steps above, or the one its own read made, as for the focus, against the candidate's, read live once per candidate per walk; a failed read is no location, and a kept object that no longer answers fails it; different locations: different, so a destroyed container is never taken for a new one in its place;
8. the name: the sighting's against the candidate's kept name, and when they differ the candidate's read live once per walk and normalised as a sighting's is; still different: different;
9. otherwise equal.

A reissue gives the sighting the candidate's node id and nothing else: the node keeps its old object, address and event parameters, as NVDA keeps its old objects through the meeting point, and at most its kept name and location are refreshed. No COM object changes hands, so a reissue needs no commit. After a reissue from a line's entry only the entries outward of it are scanned, in every line that has it, so within one walk a candidate gives its id at most once. Reissuing is not stopping the walk: the walk stops only at a node of the followed focus's chain, and walks on past any other reissued node, so every chain stays complete to the desktop for Core's meeting. A focus-now answer has no chain to splice from and walks to the desktop, reissuing against Core's line.

Costs, MSAA calls only: one `accLocation` per sighting that passes a candidate's first steps and whose read made none, and per candidate per walk one live `accLocation`, plus one `accName` when the names differ. An `accLocation` on an object reached through `accParent` has oleacc walk the object's parents in the provider, 3 `accParent` hits per read for a group two levels below its window's root, growing with depth; at the walk's 64-hop bound about 65 hits a read and about 12,600 for the 194 location reads of the worst case, an inference not measured at depth. The steady state, a move within one container, is the measured count in `docs/performance.md`; the cold path, with no candidate passing, is today's; with the candidates capped to Core's line and the newest unjudged focus, the worst case is 64 hops plus two calls per candidate of two lines of at most 65 nodes, 324 calls, all within the walk's time bound. Memory: the facts kept per MSAA node and the walk's cache of live reads, freed when the walk ends. Nothing changes for UI Automation.

Limitations: the role is compared by reading, not live; the event parameters and identity string are fixed when a node is first read; overlay classes and application-specific equalities (MSHTML's node number, Acrobat's `accID`) are not compared; IAccessible2 objects are compared without their unique IDs until M7. NVDA's speech for the edge cases (a scrolled, renamed, destroyed and recreated or unlocatable container; two identical unnamed lists; a container focused at an event child id) is read from its source and waits for one capture session against mockapp's `object_equality.json` fixture.

### Mechanisms removed and added, with their costs

Removed: `CoreFocus` and its builder in the app; the listener's `HeldElements`; the outpost's `Held`; the "no repeat while unjudged" rule; `Reading::abandoned`'s keeping for Core and the follow-up on abandoned reads; the outpost's old gap with its matching across two clocks, `MarkerNews`, `DropEvidence` and the drop classes; the listener's arbitrators and raw probe; the hand-over's multisets, `TWIN_BOUND`, `ListenerBeforeHook` and run ordering by raise time; `record_probe`, the renewal of a 'no' at an entry's end; the listener's judgement of a menu opening before its turn; the resend of kept facts; the millisecond field on the wire; Core's second acceptance test, `report_foreground`'s second look and the intake's second hung test; `ancestors_unknown` with Core's kept chain and the windowless-focus adoption; the app's in-ignored-window judgement (to Core, with the top-level window's process in the window facts); Core's four notions of the foreground.

Added: the unknown verdict, kept verdicts compared with each event's observation, and the outpost's drop of a listener's read in a window it judges not UIA; the unread fact for every repeat with Core's read request to the reader that sent it, each reader keeping its newest unread item; the continuation from a parent window's object, plain windows at the bound, the watchdogs publishing at the bound through one function, and Core's meeting of chains; the window handle in node details; the listener's currency stamp; refusal notices; Core's spoken record; the menu opening read in order and judged in Core; the outpost's gap record with its timer, its restamp and the answers each drop carries; the hand-over's hold; the desktop switch and the desktop's name; the delayed pump, if its capture agrees.

Per focus on the steady path: two or three fewer messages, the `CoreFocus` notices, and as many fewer wake-ups of the listener's and outposts' pipe threads; no call added; one local `GetAncestor` and one atomic store per focus and foreground event in the listener; memory down by the unjudged queues and held elements. A repeat Core holds costs NVDA's test (two calls for UIA, none for MSAA and the foreground) and one small message. Since a reader sends a repeat unread only while its kept read is current, a repeat Core does not hold is rare: for UIA and MSAA it needs Core's focus to have moved without the reader seeing it, an MSAA focus elsewhere in the same top-level window or a refusal, which the first step counts; it then costs a read request's round trip before the read, expected under a millisecond and measured before it is trusted, and the application's events raised meanwhile are handled before the requested read's report. The first focus after a return to an application reads its whole chain, as NVDA. Fixed costs: one more global `WinEvent` kind and possibly one process-scoped hook in the listener; the delayed pump's up to 10 ms on a batch beginning with a popup start or foreground change, and one timer, which NVDA has too, only if its capture agrees.

### Edge cases, limitations and what must be measured

- Verdicts. The outpost's verdict alone decides, so the listener's reads in a window the outpost judges not UIA are wasted and change nothing that is spoken, as NVDA drops a focus that is not native; the listener's class-list skip drops only a focus the outpost's verdict gives to MSAA by the same table, except while an M5 reload of the tables, or a backend override forcing UIA, has reached one process and not the other, a limit of a change in flight (the override is sent only by tests today). A focus is spoken twice where its copy's window is judged not UIA and its UIA focus's window UIA, as in NVDA, and where one window's verdict changes between a focus's two observations, past its keep. A UIA focus whose window's probe times out at 3 seconds is not spoken, where NVDA's UIA handler, whose probe is not cancelled, speaks it if the window answers before `UiaHasServerSideProvider` gives up, about 5 seconds (difference 15). The listener's exposure to a busy application: in an MSAA window off the class lists it reads each focus through UIA's MSAA proxy, whose calls go into the application's `IAccessible`, and walks its ancestors through the proxies too, one call per ancestor, each read bounded by `READ_DEADLINE` and each application by `ABANDONED_LIMIT`; UIA's transaction timeout bounds the proxy's calls into `IAccessible`: with mockapp's MSAA window stalled for 12 seconds, the listener's read returned after 5,008 and 5,016 ms (measured at the build with mockapp's `--backend msaa` under `stall`, `docs/performance.md`). `READ_DEADLINE` loses its probe term with the listener's probe: the read's budget of 2 seconds, UIA's transaction timeout of 5 and a second to spare, 8 seconds where it was 11. Until step 11 removes `HeldElements`, a listener read the outpost drops reaches no judgement of Core's, so it stays unjudged in the listener, whose duplicate test is off while such a read waits, and in an MSAA window off the class lists a repeat then costs a full read, which Core's duplicate rule drops: nothing more is heard, and this interim cost is accepted with no new message (Dickson, 2026-10-30). Measured: probes per focus in `call_counts` against mockapp; and, in `conhost_two_windows`, `windows_terminal_two_windows` and `explorer_folder_window` ten times each, no focus lost or doubled, with the listener reads the outpost dropped counted from its log.
- Hooks in. An outpost stuck without exiting is covered by `HookRemoved` at the failure decision; its stuck worker's output is refused by the live-generation gate. Events raised in the relay hop after a crash, and those raised before it and not handled, are lost, as now. Checked by `outpost_crash_recovery` with a context menu opened during the replacement.
- The fallback. A popup owned by another window is placed under the desktop by plain windows, as NVDA's fallback places it, where UIA's raw view may put it under its owner. A thread whose call returns after the watchdog published goes on to the application's next event, so a second slow window can still reach the abandoned limit only through the deadline, as now. Measured: a mockapp provider stalling only its parent navigation for 15 seconds, with the focus arriving from another application and from within, giving "title window" at 500 ms, compared with NVDA's capture; a mockapp MSAA dialog whose child's `accParent` fails, giving its dialog; `windows_terminal_two_windows` and `measure_focus_windows_terminal` ten times each.
- Core's meeting of chains. A plain window matched by handle to a UIA or MSAA window may differ in name; Core keeps the previous chain's entry, as NVDA keeps its old focus line's. Unit tests in Core per case.
- Repeats and currency. A focus in another top-level window raised with no event at all is not observed, so a kept read can be judged current across it. A read request that finds a newer unread item in its reader is answered gone and nothing is spoken for it, a newer event having come. Measured: the repeats per scenario, the kept reads found not current, and a Windows Terminal return after its title changed.
- A read request's read. The reader makes the read as asked, without NVDA's acceptance and keyboard-focus tests again, which it made on the event; an element gone answers gone, and nothing is spoken. Between Core's request and the read the focus may move on; for MSAA the focused-state test then drops a focus no longer focused, which NVDA, reading at once, would have spoken and cut. A foreground read answered after a focus the outpost had queued changes nothing, the window having been spoken as the focus's entered container. Measured: the round trip and the words of a return to Notepad by Alt+Tab in `second_application_and_verbatim_menu` and `return_after_retirement`.
- The refusal notice lags by one message, during which the outpost follows the refused node. With no departure notice, a background application's topmost window can have its focus's changes read and dropped by Core. An outpost unit test checks an older report's refusal is ignored.
- The menu opening. A focus observed before it but arriving after it, from another outpost or as a requested read's report, cannot count as pending, a limit: the menu is spoken and then the focus, where NVDA drops the menu; current-only repeats make the requested case rare. Unit tests in Core for the duplicate-then-menu case and the redirect.
- The plain window above the process boundary removes the outpost's wait on the host, but not the listener's: UIA's client side still sends the parent window its connection messages as the listener's lookup returns the child window's element (decision 13 of the listener design), so a busy host delays the listener's read, and past the connection timeout drops the focus as having no window (40 of 40 at 5 seconds, 12 of 12 at 2, `experiments/the-listeners-read-below-another-processs-window/`). Each check of a result after the system picked its target leaves the one call that returned it: a walk's parent step reparented after its check connects into the other process (10 of 10 with the order forced, waiting 1,007 ms on a process stalled for a second, `experiments/a-walk-up-whose-window-changes-after-its-check/`), and an `accFocus` read after the focus moved into another process's child window names nothing, so the parent's client is reported as the focus, followed by the focus event of where the focus went (5 of 5, `experiments/accfocus-names-no-other-processs-child-window/`).
- The second form inside the listener's read, a stated limit. Under a parent window whose application is busy with a third process's call pending, the listener's implicit connection and the parent's outpost's lookup can meet the second form of `docs/uia-bugs.md`'s first fault: the listener's read came one connection timeout late, about 2.3 seconds with the right chain, in 5 of 42 trials, and the outpost's lookup never failed (0 of 42); two outposts' lookups alone returned UIA's proxies in 3 of 42, and none without the pending call (0 of 12) (`experiments/two-new-connections-beside-a-pending-call/`). It needs a busy host, a third process's pending call and the focus below it at once, and is heard as one focus 2 seconds late.
- A replacement outpost beside its predecessor, a stated limit until `docs/roadmap.md`'s M6.2 ends the old outpost before its replacement starts. An outpost that failed by its abandoned workers still has calls queued in its application while its replacement starts; the replacement's lookup was refused after the busy time and one connection timeout in 6 of 6 trials, and with nothing queued returned its own element in 6 of 6 (`experiments/a-replacement-outposts-lookup-beside-its-predecessors-operation/`). Heard as the replacement's focus-now answer empty, so the application's focus is spoken at its next event.
- The gap's read is made once (Dickson, 2026-10-29). If it fails or its checks refuse it, the focus is not read again and nothing is spoken for it, because a read that has just failed in a busy application would very likely fail again, and NVDA has no recovery read at all. Its checks judge the foreground thread's focus window before the read and again after it, with `GetGUIThreadInfo`; whether, in Windows Terminal or a XAML island, a focus window can be judged not UIA while its focus is native UIA is not known, and the step's runs log the window of every gap read.
- The gap and the hold: measured as the order of work says. A next focus whose copy comes within 100 ms after the previous focus's UIA outcome in the same top-level window, and whose own UIA focus never comes, is not recovered, a limit. A gap read reduced after another application's focus in the same top-level window can take Core's focus while the stale rule's same-top-level exception stands, a race of one read's length.
- The delayed pump, the speech cut and the desktop's name are built only after their captures and probes.

### The foreground wait

Studied and replaced (Fable, 2026-10-30, pending Dickson's acceptance): no foreground wait is built, and the Settings frame's entry in a focus chain is described as NVDA's object for it instead. It restores NVDA's speech on entering the Settings app, which `settings_system_page` has lacked in 10 of 10 runs since the readers' walks (c7c7c6dc): expected "Settings", "Settings window", "Search box, Find a setting edit", "blank"; heard "Settings window" twice, then the edit and "blank".

The cause. Windows raised `EVENT_SYSTEM_FOREGROUND` for ApplicationFrameHost's frame and, within 4 ms, the frame's MSAA focus on its client. When the frame's outpost handled them, about 4.6 ms after the event, `GetForegroundWindow` still named Program Manager, so the worker dropped the foreground change as not in front, the MSAA focus as unrelated to the foreground window (`accepts`), and the frame's UIA focus with its window, which it judges MSAA. The content's focus, 550 ms later, then entered the frame as a plain window and its own window element, both role window and named "Settings". Before the readers' walks the content's chain read the frame's client through MSAA, a call into ApplicationFrameHost, and spoke it as a pane by its title, which hid the drop; the plain window above the process boundary rightly replaced that call, but with the role of NVDA's plain `Window`, not of the object NVDA makes there.

The study. The review asked how NVDA says "Settings" in 5 of 5 launches when the frame is in front within NVDA's defer in only 8 of 20 (`experiments/the-settings-frame-comes-in-front-after-its-foreground-event/`). The pinned source and a capture of five launches with NVDA's MSAA debug log (that folder's `nvda-capture.txt`) settle it.

- NVDA never speaks a foreground object, which refutes the hypothesis that the bare "Settings" is `objectInForeground()` spoken from `doPreGainFocus`. `doPreGainFocus` takes `objectInForeground()` only when the new focus line shares no more than the desktop with the old one, stores it and runs its `foreground` event (`eventHandler.py` 402 to 413), and `NVDAObject.event_foreground` only cancels speech and tells vision (`NVDAObjects/__init__.py` 1382 to 1388); NVDA has no foreground output reason.
- The bare "Settings" is always the frame's client object, role client mapped to pane, which NVDA presents by its title because it is a client, child id 0, of a window with a system menu (`NVDAObjects/IAccessible/__init__.py` 2095 to 2106), spoken by name alone since an entered container is spoken as a focus (`speech/speech.py` 892 to 894) and a pane's role is silent then (`controlTypes/role.py` 536 to 549).
- It is spoken as the focus in 4 of 5 launches. NVDA's defer holds both of the frame's events (`IAccessibleHandler/internalWinEventHandler.py` 231 to 262); the pump after the second deferral, 27 to 38 ms after the event, found the frame in front, `shouldAcceptEvent` passed, and `pumpAll`, trying the newest focus event first, took the frame's focus winEvent, so the foreground event itself was never processed. "Settings" came 0.4 to 0.6 s before the content's focus.
- It is spoken as an entered container in 1 of 5. The defer ran out 27 ms after the event with Program Manager in front, and both events were dropped. The content's UIA focus came 0.5 s later; its walk's parent step from the content's window element into the frame's window changes API for that window, which NVDA judges not UIA, with the relation "parent", which for MSAA gives the window's client object (`NVDAObjects/UIA/__init__.py` 2130 to 2154; `NVDAObjects/window/__init__.py` 319 to 330; `NVDAObjects/IAccessible/__init__.py` 564 to 565). The focus line holds the frame's client pane, entered and spoken 5 ms before "Settings window".
- "Settings window" is the content's window element, UIA, role window and named, entered below the frame's client: the new focus line meets the old one at the client when the frame's focus was taken, and enters the client with it otherwise. NVDA's `WindowRoot` for the frame is what object navigation reaches, not an entry of the focus line.

So NVDA's words on entering Settings do not depend on its defer; only when "Settings" is said does. The expected words for a window above the process boundary, which the decision of 2026-10-29 left to a capture of the Settings frame, are therefore those of a top-level window's MSAA client.

The fix, one change in the outpost's reader. A window above the process boundary, other than the desktop, a ghost window or a window of a hung application, is described as NVDA's MSAA client object for it, from Windows' own state: role pane, named by `InternalGetWindowText`, with `top_level_client` set when its style has `WS_SYSMENU`, the rule and the one local style read that `verbatim-ia2`'s `is_top_level_client` makes for an MSAA client; it carries the window's handle, as plain windows do, and their states: unavailable from the style, and never focused or invisible. The outpost's reader decides it per window where it builds windows from local state (`read.rs`, `plain_windows_from`, which `window_onward` calls at the first window another process serves and past a walk's bound, and which completes a walk cut at its bound or failed): each window not a ghost's or a hung application's is the pane, above the boundary and, (Fable, 2026-10-30, pending Dickson's acceptance), below it too, since a plain window published at a bound said File Explorer's folder window as "… - File Explorer window", "… window" and "ShellView window" where the walk read in full says its title once (`explorer_folder_window`, run 8 of the bound fix); the listener sets nothing, since its walk ends below the boundary or at its bound and the outpost continues it. A walk cut at its bound whose outermost element is its window's own has read that window, and the plain windows start from its parent window, so the window is not in the chain twice. A ghost's or a hung application's window above the boundary stays a plain window, role window, as NVDA makes its plain `Window` there (`NVDAObjects/window/__init__.py` 85 to 97). Core needs no change: `is_presentable_container` presents a pane with `top_level_client` by its title and takes any other pane as structure (`docs/parity/focus-context.md`, "Top-level windows in the ancestry"), as NVDA treats a client that is not a top-level window's, and the meeting of chains still matches the entry by its handle.

What is not built, and why.

- The foreground wait. It would change no word: with the frame's focus dropped, the content's chain says "Settings" from the frame's pane, as NVDA's run whose defer ran out did. It would not even move "Settings" earlier, since its bound was 20 ms and the frame came in front within 20 ms of its event in none of 20 launches (23.7 ms at the least); a bound of NVDA's real 27 to 38 ms would catch about half, at up to 38 ms of held events behind every lagging foreground change in every outpost. NVDA's defer stays a deliberate difference, recorded in `docs/parity/focus-events.md`, "Event acceptance", when the step is built.
- The `above_process_boundary` mark. It would silence every window above the boundary, among them WPF and WinUI hosts that NVDA speaks, as the review found; the pane presents what NVDA presents and leaves structure silent, by Core's existing rule.
- Core's foreground object as speech. It stays the record step 9 plans and is never spoken, as NVDA's is not.

Expected speech for `settings_system_page`, entering from the desktop: "Settings", "Settings window", "Search box, Find a setting edit", "blank", in every run. The frame comes in front 24 to 78 ms after its event, so its outpost, handling them about 5 ms after the event, drops its foreground change and its focus, and the content's chain enters the frame's pane, spoken by its title, and the content's window element; an outpost that handles them only once the frame is in front reports the frame's focus instead, which the content's chain meets by handle, with the same words. Brought forward from another window, or switched to with Alt+Tab, the frame is in front before its event (measured), its outpost reports its MSAA focus, its client named "Settings", and the content's focus meets that entry at the pane by its handle and enters only the content's window: "Settings", "Settings window", the groupings and the focus, as NVDA's capture from a Windows Forms window. Moving within the page says nothing for either window.

The invariants the change claims, each with the sequences that attack it:

- It changes one node's role and one flag, nothing on any event path: no event is held, dropped, reordered or added, so the outposts' order, Core's stale rule and the idle barrier are as today. Tried: the frame's focus reaching Core before the content's, met at the pane by handle and not entered again; the content's focus first, from the desktop, entering the pane once; a frame focus older than the content's focus arriving after it, which is the stale rule's same-top-level exception, a stated limit under "Open items" that this change does not touch.
- Two outposts describing one window agree on what Core keeps. Tried: ApplicationFrameHost's outpost reports the frame's own client, an MSAA top-level client named "Settings", and SystemSettings' outpost the plain pane for the same handle; Core keeps the previous chain's entry, which in either order is a pane spoken "Settings".
- No call reaches another process. The style is read with `GetWindowLongPtrW`, the name with `InternalGetWindowText`, both Windows' own state. Tried: `a_window_above_the_process_boundary_is_a_plain_window_with_no_call`'s setup, the parent stalled for 4 seconds, with no provider call counted in the parent's process.
- A dropped foreground change is handled as today. Tried: the launch above, where attention follows the content's focus in the system's foreground window with no foreground report ("Who owns each duplicated fact", the foreground).
- The desktop is never a pane: it is the chain's top, named by NVDA's rule for the desktop. A hung ApplicationFrameHost leaves the frame a plain window, so entry says "Settings window" twice, as NVDA's plain `Window` for a hung window and the content's window element would. A ghost frame likewise.
- Verbatim's hidden frame is in no chain, being never visible and so never a visible owner. A console window is above the boundary only as the visible owner or parent of another process's window, `served_by` reading its provider process, the console host; it is then a pane said by its title, where NVDA, reading it through UIA, says "<title> window", the limit stated below for WPF and WinUI hosts.
- A window above the boundary below the top level, such as a hosting application's child window, usually has no system menu and is then a pane taken as structure, unspoken, as NVDA's client object for it; today it is spoken as "<name> window" when named. The rule reads the style alone, as NVDA's does, so a top-level window re-parented as a child keeps its system menu and is spoken by its title, as NVDA speaks its client.

Limitations.

- "Settings" is said with the content's focus, 0.4 to 0.6 s after NVDA says it in the launches where NVDA's defer finds the frame in front (4 of 5 captured), the same words; a launch whose content raised no focus would say nothing for the frame where NVDA, in those launches, says "Settings".
- A window above the boundary that NVDA reads through UIA, as it reads a WPF or WinUI host its probe finds UIA, is said by its title alone where NVDA says "<title> window", since the probe is a call into the other process; an application whose own MSAA client reports another role, such as a dialog, is described as a pane, said by its title when it has a system menu. Neither changes the meaning, which proceeds by Dickson's rule of 2026-10-30; the Settings frame is the one boundary captured.
- Object navigation up from the content reaches NVDA's `WindowRoot`, "Settings window", where Verbatim's chain holds the pane; the step checks what Verbatim's object navigation says there.

Costs: for each window above the boundary that a walk describes, the ghost and hung checks, three local calls (`IsWindow`, `GhostWindowFromHungWindow`, `IsHungAppWindow`), beside the local calls it already makes; the style is read once, as the plain window already read it; no wake-up, no latency, no memory, and no new field on the wire, `top_level_client` existing.

This reverses part of the plain window above the process boundary (10c9e83a): the window keeps being read with no call into its process, and only its role changes, from NVDA's plain `Window` to the client NVDA reads there. It also replaces the foreground wait and the mark designed earlier on 2026-10-30, which were never built.

### Room for extensions

Nothing in this section is built by this rework: each is a constraint its steps keep. Extensions arrive in M5 ([extensions](extensions.md)), whose host runs in the outposts and Core and never in the listener, and whose window and event rules compile into tables the listener and the outposts both consult. This rework builds none of it, but keeps these things M5 needs, so that a module's veto or redirect of a focus never costs a read NVDA would not make:

- The listener's checks before a read stay in NVDA's order, as code today, which M5 turns into data-driven tables with a place for M5's window, event and node rules judged from the event's cache alone. NVDA's base cache request, which the listener's registration copies, already carries the framework, automation id, class name, control type, name and process id (`UIAHandler/__init__.py` 61 to 74), so a veto declared as a rule drops the focus before any read.
- Hooks run in the outpost after the listener's read, as NVDA runs a module's `event_gainFocus` after `doPreGainFocus` has walked the ancestors (`eventHandler.py` 349 to 357, 378 to 387), so a code veto or redirect makes no read NVDA would not; NVDA's own UIA vetoes before the walk are UIA's default `shouldAllowUIAFocusEvent`, the keyboard-focus read the listener already makes, and in its application modules a class constant and a container override on the Alt key's state (`appModules/explorer.py` 35 to 48), expressible as rules from the cache. No general read request is kept for M5; a later module whose pre-walk veto is code adds it with its use.
- Kept verdicts can be dropped: M5 makes each outpost drop its verdicts when it installs a new version of the rule tables, since a reloaded module can change a window's backend, and the listener, which keeps none, reloads the class tables it skips reads by.
- Repeats judged in Core change what the Windows Terminal second-identity hook ([design](windows-terminal-twin-focus.md)) assumes, the listener's comparison before the read and `focus()` as Core's focus; M5 restates it against the unread repeat and the followed focus, and says which element `focus()` means when a hook drops a focus the listener's kept read is on.
- M5's node and event rules apply at one point, the outpost's emission, to every kind of report: listener reads, the requested read's report of an unread repeat (the unread fact itself carries only an identity), plain windows published at a bound, menu openings, and the outpost's own recovery reads (the gap's read, focus-now), which find a focus with no judgement of the listener's and so must meet the same vetoes, or a vetoed focus would come back through the gap.

### Why no smaller design

The study's first recommendation, on 2026-10-25, removed four reconciliations and left the copies of the focus, the two verdicts, the hooks-in window, the foreground copies and the audit's fallbacks; this design replaces it. A one-process host and outposts holding the focus registration stay ruled out for the reasons in the constraints section. The two routes are forced; given them, an application's events need an order (the marker), a new outpost needs its first events (the hold), a missed UIA focus needs a recovery (the gap, beside the verdict that dropped its copy), several outposts need a common order at Core (one time unit), a window's backend needs one verdict (the outpost's, the listener skipping only by NVDA's MSAA class list), and the outpost needs to know when it follows the wrong node (the refusal notice). Each is kept once, in the process that holds its evidence.

The alternative in which the listener makes no call and each outpost finds and reads its application's UIA focus itself, with `GetFocusedElementBuildCache` and a comparison of runtime ids, was written and reviewed on 2026-10-29 and rejected by Dickson the same day; it is the outpost redesign of 2026-10-01 that option (b) replaced, and git keeps its record (e2c883a8). It would return to dropping a focus whose read of a starting application fails, which made the Notepad scenario fail in 1 of 5 to 25 runs (13dcd2db); `GetFocusedElement` does not always name the sender, as Start's search results raise focus while it stays on the search box (018e62d5), so the comparison would take such a focus for a repeat and say nothing; the reads that fail report nothing, so they would close the gap that should recover them; NVDA's refusals would be judged on the event rather than on the element found, which can be the console window; every new window's first focus would need a connection by handle, which held the worker 2.0 to 2.5 seconds in 1 to 6 of 10 new File Explorer windows (decision 11 of `docs/design/listener-without-connections.md`); and the window's name would wait on that read, against the decision of 2026-10-29. Its gain, a listener that makes no call, does not outweigh silences that NVDA does not have.
