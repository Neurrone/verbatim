# A registration that times out reports success

## Fact

`AddEventHandlerGroup` on a window whose provider does not answer returns success after the connection timeout, whatever the transaction timeout, though the provider never answered (`books/dev/src/reference/uia-bugs.md`, the fault of that name). With the client's connection recovery on, as Verbatim's and NVDA's clients have it, such a registration then delivers nothing until about 2 seconds after its call returned (1,981 to 2,001 ms), or until the stall ends if that is later, and every event raised in that gap is lost: none was delivered by the end of the watch, 0.5 seconds after the last event raised, about 0.7 seconds after the gap; with recovery off there is no gap. The gap's end follows neither timeout, the next event does not bring it forward, and a registration from a second client process behaves alike. With no stall the registration returns at once and hears every event. The events raised in the gap reach the provider, which makes calls after each one; that they are lost in the registering client is inferred. The registration's own request runs as the stall ends. Events raised between a focus change and a registration made for the new focus are not heard (`books/dev/src/explanation/windows/uia.md`, "Events into the client"). The gap's reach over the process's other registrations, a removal during it and the silence after the stall are [the gap's own program](../the-gap-after-a-timed-out-registration-silences-the-processs-registrations-on-that-window/readme.md)'s.

## What Verbatim relies on

`timed_out` in `crates/verbatim-uia/src/subscribe.rs`, which treats a registration slower than `CONNECTION_TIMEOUT` as timed out and logs it at warn, the only sign; `Follower::close`'s second removal is [the gap's own program](../the-gap-after-a-timed-out-registration-silences-the-processs-registrations-on-that-window/readme.md)'s. If registrations began to fail honestly, the follower's error path would run instead, which is not wrong; if the wait stopped following the connection timeout, the warning would misfire. The gap's figures are used only by an unbuilt proposal, which `books/dev/src/reference/uia-bugs.md` describes.

## Method

The kept CI tests, in `crates/mockapp/tests/uia_bugs/a_registration_that_times_out_reports_success.rs`, check that the registration returns success while its provider is held and that a read on a held provider fails: `a_group_registered_while_its_provider_is_held_reports_success` and `a_read_while_its_provider_is_held_fails`. The program covers how long the registration waits and what follows it, which needs timing and pauses a test may not make.

Every trial runs in a client process of its own (the program's `trial` role), as e57 ran each trial in a test process of its own, so no other trial's registration is in the process. Its client object takes the run's `--recovery` and `--coalescing`; Verbatim's client (`create_client` in `crates/verbatim-uia/src/client.rs`) and e57's had both on. Every registration is an event handler group holding UIA's focus event, subtree scope, the sender's name cached, on the provider program's window, as e57 made it. Each focus event is raised as e57 raised it through mockapp's `set-name` and `focus`: the next of nodes 5 to 8 in turn is renamed `ev<i>` with a name change raised, then the tree's focus moves to it and its focus event is raised, so the handler names the event it heard. A stall is the window's UI thread busy until released. The stall's release 200 ms after the registration returns, the event offsets and the listening after the last event, 0.5 s, are deliberate waits, since the fact is about time. The gap's claims are judged at offsets clear of its end, about 300 ms either side, so a tick of jitter cannot move an event across it: every event lost up to 1,500 ms after the stall's end and heard from 2,300 ms, or, released 1,000 ms after the return, lost up to 700 ms and heard from 1,300 ms. The stall's backlog is the registration's own request, which ends with the provider's advise of the focus event, so the advise's end marks it.

- `ett`: E-TT's registration, both timeouts 1,000 ms, the provider holding `FragmentRoot`.
- `connection`: a registration during a stall at connection 1,000 ms and transaction 500 ms, and the reverse.
- `e57`: connection 2,000 ms, transaction 5,000 ms; a focus event every 50 ms from the stall's end to 2,500 ms after it, and every 10 ms from 1,700 to 1,850 ms, about the gap's end; the provider's log read after. Judged: the outcome and wait; the gap clear of its end; lost, not late; none lost after the first heard; the last lost and the first heard spanning 1,981 to 2,001 ms after the return; provider work after each event raised in the gap 50 ms apart; the backlog the registration's request alone. Then, with no stall, ten events 50 ms apart from the return.
- `release-later`, `next-event` and `second-process`, at both timeouts 500 ms, so that the gap's end is shown at other timeouts than `e57`'s 2,000 and 5,000 ms: the stall released 1,000 and 3,000 ms after the return; events at 1,500 and 2,500 ms after the stall's end alone, the first lost and the second heard, so the next event brings the gap's end no nearer; `e57` registered by a second client process (the `registrar` role), given the run's flags.
- `between`: a focus event and, at once, a name change on the focused node; the client, on hearing the focus, registers for the node's name changes; then a second name change.

## Client settings

Event coalescing changes no outcome; connection recovery causes the gap (checked on 2026-11-06 by running every case by default and once each with `--recovery`, `--coalescing` and both). The documents state the gap, so the gap's cases are judged with `--recovery`; the program judges each setting against its own record. The registration's success and its wait on the connection timeout (`ett`, `connection`, and `e57`'s first two claims), the control with no stall, the backlog, and `between` do not change.

- Connection recovery on, as Verbatim's and NVDA's clients have it: the gap, as the Fact says and the records below show. `target\debug\a-registration-that-times-out-reports-success.exe --recovery`, or one case, such as `--recovery --case e57`.
- Connection recovery off, the default: no gap. In `e57` every event raised from the stall's end is heard; released at any time, at both timeouts 500 ms, and from a second client process, the first event heard is the one at the stall's end and none is lost; both of `next-event`'s events are heard. `target\debug\a-registration-that-times-out-reports-success.exe`, or `--case e57`.

## How to rerun

`uia-facts/build.ps1`, then `target\debug\a-registration-that-times-out-reports-success.exe --recovery` for the gap the documents state, or without `--recovery` for the outcome with connection recovery off; one case with `--case`. Under `--pause`, each trial's process prints its provider's and its own process ids once its provider is up, and waits for Enter here. No desktop, application or debugger; it starts a copy of itself for each trial, and another for `second-process`'s registrar. The tests: `cargo test -p mockapp --test uia_bugs -- --exact a_group_registered_while_its_provider_is_held_reports_success`, and the same for `a_read_while_its_provider_is_held_fails`.
