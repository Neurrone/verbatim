# A process that used UIA can hang or crash as it exits

## Fact

A process that has connected to UIA providers as a client sometimes hangs at full processor use, or crashes with an access violation, as it exits normally and runs its DLLs' process-detach code. It happened in about 1 to 3 percent of runs of the arbitration tests. One crash was captured under cdb with full page heap (`recorded/providers-alive-fault.txt`, one of 152 children of the `providers-alive` setup): `UIAutomationCore.dll`'s detach code destroys its telemetry object (`TelemetryUtility::m_instance`), whose `std::unordered_map` from a provider's process id to a per-provider connection record had a corrupt list, and it faulted destroying the list's head as if it were a record, before anything was freed: the stack runs from the list's `_Freenode` into `AppContainerInfo::~AppContainerInfo` and a `std::basic_string` destructor; the faulting read is at `[rcx-8]`, `rcx` holding the page heap's `0xc0` fill; and the page heap places `rbx` inside the head node that `TelemetryUtility`'s constructor allocated, still allocated.

What the capture shows of how the list got that way: each new connection inserts a record on the thread that connects (`TelemetryUtility::StartConnection`, from `ChannelBasedClientConnectionManager::CreatePipeToProvider`), and in the faulting child three client threads were inside it at once. After their three inserts the forward chain held two records and the size count was 2. The next insert, made alone, left the head's back link naming a node that is not on the forward chain, with a size count of 3 against two nodes, and the list was the same at the destructor's entry. Three other children of the 152 ended with fewer nodes than the size count and the head's back link on the chain's last node, and none of them faulted.

What is inferred, not captured: that nothing locks the map, from `StartConnection`'s disassembly; that the inserts made at once corrupt it, since the corruption followed three of them and connections made one at a time prevented every fault; how an insert made alone then took the back link off the chain (the race is inferred to have left the map's bucket table naming the lost record's node); and, from the destructor's code, that it sets the next link of the node the head's back link names to null and frees nodes forward until it meets a null, so that when that node is not on the forward chain the walk passes the head, frees it, and goes on through freed memory. Under page heap that faults at the head, as captured; without it the walk is inferred to loop without end, the hang, or read a freed page, the crash; no hang was captured under cdb. UIA's I/O thread erases a record when the provider's end of the pipe closes (`TelemetryUtility::CloseConnection`, from `ChannelBasedClientConnection::OnRemoteCloseOrError`); in the earlier cdb run of `exit`'s setup, whose providers end before the exit, 6 of 150 children had an inconsistent list after the inserts and a consistent one at exit, from which it is inferred that erasing records as providers go can repair the links, so whether a corrupted list faults depends on what is left at exit.

The records show the fault only with three or more client threads each connecting to provider processes of its own: with connections from one thread, from two threads to two provider processes, or from six threads to one provider process, none of 230 children faulted; with three, four, six or twelve such threads, 1 to 5 percent of children faulted, even when each only read a window's name. Making the connections one at a time, behind a lock of the process's own, prevented it: none of 1,050 children faulted. A proper teardown before the exit (every handler removed, every UIA object released and COM left on every thread) does not prevent it, and neither does leaving the providers running through the exit (`books/dev/src/reference/uia-bugs.md`, the fault of that name).

## What Verbatim relies on

Its processes end themselves with `TerminateProcess` once shut down, so the detach code never runs: outposts and the listener (`books/dev/src/explanation/architecture.md`, section 1), and the mockapp test runner (`crates/mockapp/tests/common/harness.rs`). A proper teardown does not prevent the fault. Making connections one at a time does, but would put one lock across every thread that connects, so ending without the detach code is what avoids it.

## Method

Each child process (the `exiter` role) makes UIA's first-time setup on its main thread, starts provider programs of its own and, from threads of its own, connects as a UIA client to their windows and reads each window's name, as its setup says. It then ends its providers, as the mockapp tests' providers ended before their test process, and ends normally with `std::process::exit`, running every DLL's detach code. Children run one after another. Only children that report their work done count. A child that has not ended 3 s after its work is a hang, since the detach code spins without end once it hangs, and one that ends with an access violation a crash; a child that fails in any other way makes the claim inconclusive, with its outcome among the evidence.

- `exit`: 150 children (Dickson, 2026-11-06) of the setup with the most faults a minute among the candidates: four provider programs with a UIA window each, a thread each, connecting and reading the name, the clients, elements and cache requests left in place. Judged as a rate against that setup's 8 of 230; 90 children would already make none differ.
- `candidates`, run only when named, 230 children of each setup: the first build's, six provider programs of three windows each (a UIA window, one with its own `IAccessible`, a plain one), a thread each, every connection reading, searching its subtree and registering a name-change handler; then that setup with one step removed or changed at a time, and smaller and larger ones. 230 children show a setup with none below the first build's rate of about 1 in 75. The rate and the faults a minute are printed as new.
- `clean`, run only when named: `exit`'s setup torn down before the exit: each thread removes its handlers, releases every element, cache request, handler and client and leaves COM, and the main thread does the same, the providers ended after the teardown in one run and before it in another, 120 children each, at which none separates the clean rate from 8 of 230 with a margin (0.965 to the 120th power is about 0.014). Judged against the dirty rate: differ when apart from it, pass when no difference shows.
- `serial`, run only when named: `exit`'s setup, each thread holding a lock of the child's own while it connects and reads, so no two connections are made at once; 150 children. Its record is a rate apart from 8 of 230, which none in 150 shows (0.965 to the 150th power is below 0.005): pass when apart, differ when no difference shows.
- `providers-alive`, run only when named: `exit`'s setup with the providers left running through the exit, so UIA erases no record before it; their jobs end them once the child has ended. 150 children, judged as `clean` is.

## How to rerun

`uia-facts/build.ps1`, then `target\debug\a-process-that-used-uia-can-hang-or-crash-as-it-exits.exe`, which runs `exit`; `--case candidates` takes about 20 minutes, `--case clean` about 35 seconds, and `--case serial` and `--case providers-alive` under a minute each. No desktop or application.

## Debugger guide

What it shows: the client threads that insert into the telemetry map at once, the list each insert and erase leaves, and the list the destructor walks. A child runs alone with `--role exiter` and the setup's words: providers, windows, every window a UIA window, threads, every thread to every window, search, register, serial, and the end (`dirty`, `clean-after`, `clean-before` or `providers-alive`). `exit`'s setup is `4 1 0 4 0 0 0 0 dirty`.

1. Fill the symbol cache first (`books/dev/src/how-to/debugging.md`, "Filling the cache without freezing anything"). The offsets below are for `UIAutomationCore.dll` 7.2.26100.9549; after an update, find them again with `uf UIAutomationCore!TelemetryUtility::StartConnection` and `uf UIAutomationCore!TelemetryUtility::CloseConnection` in a `-z` session, which give each function's address and its `ret`; the destructor is `TelemetryUtility::'scalar deleting destructor'`, found with `x UIAutomationCore!*Telemetry*`.
2. From this folder, start the child under cdb, breaking at process creation: `cdb -xe cpr -y <symbol path> -cf cdb-commands.txt ..\..\target\debug\a-process-that-used-uia-can-hang-or-crash-as-it-exits.exe --role exiter 4 1 0 4 0 0 0 0 dirty`.
3. `cdb-commands.txt` runs in this order. Full page heap without `gflags`, which needs an elevated shell: at the creation break, `ed @$peb+bc 02000000` (the heap's page-allocation flag in the PEB's global flags), `sxe ld:verifier.dll`, `g`; at that load, `.reload /f verifier.dll`, `ed verifier!AVrfDphGlobalFlags 3`, `sxd ld:verifier.dll`; cdb prints `page heap enabled with flags 0x3` before the initial breakpoint. Then `sxe ld:UIAutomationCore.dll` and `g`; at that load, `$$><cdb-breakpoints.txt`, which runs the file as one block, so that each breakpoint's quoted command string reaches cdb whole; `bl`, which must list five breakpoints, each with its command; `sxd ld:UIAutomationCore.dll`; an access violation handler, `sxe -c "k 30; r; !ext.heap -p -a @rbx" av`; and `g`. With an elevated shell, `gflags /p /enable a-process-that-used-uia-can-hang-or-crash-as-it-exits.exe /full` gives page heap to every run instead, and `gflags /p /disable` with the same name undoes it.
4. The breakpoints of `cdb-breakpoints.txt`, each printing a line and going on with `gc`; the object is `poi(UIAutomationCore!TelemetryUtility::m_instance)`, whose list size count is at offset 0x20 (the list head's address is at 0x18; a node is its next link, then its back link, then the key):
   - `UIAutomationCore+dfdd8`, `StartConnection`'s entry: `start tid <thread> key <the provider's process id>`, the key at `poi(@rsp+28)`.
   - `UIAutomationCore+dff82`, its `ret`: `start ret tid <thread> size <count>`.
   - `UIAutomationCore+237858`, `CloseConnection`'s entry: `close key <key>`, the key in `@r8`.
   - `UIAutomationCore+2379a1`, its `ret`: `close ret size <count>`.
   - `UIAutomationCore+237828`, the telemetry object's destructor: `destructor size <count>`.

   To see the list itself, break at a `ret` or at the destructor and walk it by hand: `dq poi(poi(UIAutomationCore!TelemetryUtility::m_instance)+18) L3` gives the head's next link, back link and key, and the same on each next link walks the chain back to the head.
5. Run each child with a limit (25 s did) and kill cdb past it: a hung child spins in the destructor.

The stacks to expect, innermost first:

- An insert, on a client thread: `TelemetryUtility::StartConnection`, `ChannelBasedClientConnectionManager::CreatePipeToProvider`, `ChannelBasedClientConnectionManager::CreateLowBoxChannelBasedClientConnection`, `ChannelBasedClientConnectionManager::CreateChannelBasedConnection`, `ProviderEntryPoint::GetUiaNode`, `ProviderEntryPoint::GetNativeUiaNode`, `UiaNodeFactory::CompleteNodeInfo`, `UiaNodeFactory::FromPartialNodeInfo`, `UiaNodeFactory::FromProviderEntryPoint`, `CUIAutomation::GetNodeFromFactory`, `CUIAutomation::ElementFromHandle`, then the program's `use_uia`.
- An erase, on UIA's I/O thread: `TelemetryUtility::CloseConnection`, `ChannelBasedClientConnection::OnRemoteCloseOrError`, `ReadWriteChannelInfo::Service`, `OverlappedIOManager::IoThreadProc`, `OverlappedIOManager::StaticIoThreadProc`.
- The walk, on the exiting thread: `TelemetryUtility::scalar deleting destructor`, `std::_Ref_count<TelemetryUtility>::_Destroy`, `std::_Ref_count_base::_Decref`, the dynamic atexit destructor for `TelemetryUtility::m_instance`, `ucrtbase!execute_onexit_table`, `dllmain_crt_process_detach`, `dllmain_dispatch`, `ntdll!LdrShutdownProcess`, `ntdll!RtlExitUserProcess`, `KERNEL32!ExitProcessImplementation`, then the program's `std::process::exit`.

The result matches when a second `start tid` line comes on another thread before the first one's `start ret`, and a count the files print differs from the inserts less the erases: a `start ret` size below the inserts made, or a `destructor size` that is not 0, such as `ffffffff` after four inserts and four erases. In the records above, read with the earlier, longer breakpoint commands whose output is `recorded/providers-alive-fault.txt`, inconsistent lists came in 6 of 150 children of `exit`'s setup and in 4 of 152 of `providers-alive`'s, one of which faulted. With `serial` set (`4 1 0 4 0 0 0 1 dirty`), in 20 children under cdb on 2026-11-06, each of the 80 inserts began after the previous one's `ret`. The files were checked under cdb on 2026-11-06 with one child of `exit`'s setup, bounded at 2 minutes: `bl` listed the five breakpoints with their commands, and the run printed two inserts overlapping, the four erases, and `destructor size ffffffff`.
