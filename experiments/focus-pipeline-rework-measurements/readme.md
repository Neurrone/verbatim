# Experiments: the focus pipeline rework's measurements

These are the measurements and captures of step 1 of [the focus pipeline rework](../../docs/design/focus-pipeline-rework.md) ("Order of work", step 1, and "The rules the results feed"). They ran on 2026-10-29 on Dickson's x64 machine (Windows 11 25H2, build 26200). The end-to-end runs used a scratch build of main with `debug.patch` applied, through the local agent, with NVDA not running. The NVDA captures used NVDA alpha-58022,732d1ce2, which is the `nvda` submodule's pin (`git -C nvda rev-parse HEAD` gives 732d1ce2f0f051da3ef1e6a6247c64cf04596337), so the expected speech and the pinned source describe the same NVDA. On 2026-10-29 Dickson capped every measurement at 20 runs of a scenario (bb3dde9c), so the design's thirty runs each for the gap became twenty.

Terms used here are the design's ("Terms"): the gap, a kept read and its currency, a repeat, the hold. In addition:

- A firing is the outpost's gap reaching its one-second bound with no UIA focus, after which the outpost reads its application's focus itself (the log line "no UIA focus followed a WinEvent focus in a UIA window").
- A copy is a `WinEvent` focus in a window whose backend is UIA, which the outpost drops for the listener's read ("MSAA focus dropped: UIA owns the window"); a copy that opened a gap started the one-second wait.
- A repeat is a focus that NVDA's duplicate test calls one: for UIA, `CompareElements` equal to the reading thread's kept element; for MSAA, at the event address of the outpost's kept read, which was not a simple child.

## The rules applied

Each rule of "The rules the results feed", with its inputs and outcome. The figures are in the sections below.

- The gap: built as designed, decided by the rule. In the gap runs (41 runs with artifacts) there were 427 copies, 139 of which opened a gap, and 2 firings. One of them came after its application's first delivered UIA focus: in `measure_focus_explorer` run 1 of the gap batch, File Explorer process 12192's first UIA focus reached its outpost at 10:22:53.814 (the "Working on it..." placeholder), and six more did before the copy at 10:23:01.380. The UIA focus for that copy ("Inner") reached the listener at 10:23:02.490, 1,110 ms after the copy and 96 ms after the firing. So this is late delivery to a running application, not a new process's first focus. The `focused-read` batch shows the same in 4 more firings, and 4 further firings whose sender window was gone. The other firing in the gap runs came before its process's first UIA focus.
- Repeats Core did not hold: goes to Dickson. In the step's seven scenarios, 5 of the 24 repeats a reader would send unread (one in about five) found Core holding another node when they reached it. All five were MSAA, one in every run of `second_application_and_verbatim_menu`. All 18 UIA repeats were held. The threshold is one in twenty. The tally script's own "Core did not hold" count (18 UIA, 5 MSAA) overstates UIA. It takes Core's focus at the listener's judgement, before Core has reduced the kept read's own report, which is still in flight. Taken when the repeat would reach Core, which is after that report, no UIA repeat in any batch went unheld (0 of 178).
- Menu openings with a focus of their application observed before and applied after: decided by the rule. There were 0 of 20 in the repeats batch and 0 of 15 in the `focused-read` batch, against a threshold of one in ten. All 35 were Verbatim's own context menu, and 4 of the 20 were dropped behind a waiting focus by today's listener.
- Refusals while Core held another node of that application: the refusal notice stays, decided by the rule. The rule sends only "none in any run" to Dickson. There were 3, one each in `return_after_retirement` runs 1, 3 and 5. All three were foreground reports of the window holding the focus ("a foreground report of the window holding the focus"). There were none in any other run of any batch: 0 of the 1,735 focus reports Core reduced in the repeats, gap and `focused-read` batches.
- The probe's bound: decided by the rule. `UiaHasServerSideProvider` waited 5,001 to 5,014 ms and answered false under a 10 s and a 30 s stall. Under a 3 s stall it waited until the stall ended (3,001 ms) and answered true. Its wait is about 5 seconds, longer than 3, so `RAW_PROBE_TIMEOUT_MS` stays 3 seconds and difference 15 is recorded as deliberate with the figure: `UiaHasServerSideProvider` waits about 5 seconds on a window that does not pump, where Verbatim's probe waits 3. NVDA makes that call through its watchdog's cancellable execution (`UIAHandler/__init__.py` 1278 to 1286); how soon its watchdog cancels it was not measured.
- The desktop name's raising process: decided by the rule. Every desktop switch, rename and closing raised exactly one `EVENT_OBJECT_NAMECHANGE` on `GetDesktopWindow()`, always from csrss.exe (process 980, thread 1152), 25 to 140 ms after the switch's foreground event. So difference 23's process-scoped hook would be scoped to csrss. The window's text (`InternalGetWindowText`) was empty every time. The name is the window object's MSAA `accName` ("Desktop 1", "Rework Desk"), which NVDA reads (`eventHandler.py` 338 to 346). The design's "speaks the window's text" therefore has to read `accName`, and so does its fallback, which reads the text with each foreground change and would read nothing. Whether an out-of-context hook scoped to csrss installs was not tried.
- The combo box's chain: decided by the rule. The drop-down is a top-level `ComboLBox` window with no owner, whose parent is the desktop. Its MSAA `accParent` chain reaches the desktop through the combo box and the form's top-level window. UIA's raw view puts it directly under the desktop root, so the raw view does not confirm the combo box as its parent. The speech cut's expectation for step 8 is no cut, from the MSAA chain, which is the backend of a `ComboLBox` window. NVDA said "expanded" for Alt+Down, then "Letters list" and "Beta" for Down Arrow, with no cancel but the key's own. The design's clause "once the raw-view probe confirms its parent" cannot be met and does not need to be.
- The File Explorer checks: decided by the rule. On 25H2, Control+E focuses a WinUI island (an `InputSiteWindowClass` window under a `Microsoft.UI.Content.DesktopChildSiteBridge`), and no window of class "Search Box" or "UniversalSearchBand" exists in the folder window. Its MSAA focus came 16 ms before the search box's UIA focus and was dropped as a copy. The Windows key, Windows+X and Tab from the desktop raised no MSAA focus in a window of class `Start`. So M5 does not add NVDA's `StartButton` rule. The `SearchBoxClient` rule matches nothing on 25H2 and rests on the audit's 24H2 finding alone.
- NVDA's captures against the design's expected speech. Two go to Dickson: focus-now onto a hung window, and a stuck walk, where NVDA never speaks a focus that the design publishes at its bound. The others agree with the design or set an expectation where it states none: the gone UIA element, the nameless foreground window, the keyboard-opened context menu, the MSAA simple child and the virtual desktop's speech. Each is under "The captures".

There is no rule for the hold. It is measured below: across 126 outpost starts, the median was 20.6 ms, the 90th percentile 68.2 ms and the maximum 591.3 ms. This is longer than the design's per-start cost sentence ("0.05 to 0.15 ms at the median, 36 ms at the 90th percentile and 146 ms at worst"), which gives the relay's latency rather than the time from hook-in to forwarding's end.

## Questions for Dickson

- The unread MSAA repeat on a return to an application. Evidence: in each of the five runs of `second_application_and_verbatim_menu`, closing Verbatim's context menu returns the focus to the second application's edit. The outpost's kept read of that edit is current by the Terms' rule, because the outpost's process-scoped hook never sees the menu, which is another application, and its own foreground change in between was in the same top-level window. So the repeat goes unread, while Core holds the menu item or the application's foreground node. That is 5 of 24 unread repeats in the step's scenarios, against the rule's one in twenty. The design sentence it contradicts: "a repeat Core does not hold is rare: for UIA and MSAA it needs Core's focus to have moved without the reader seeing it". It also contradicts "So a return to an application onto the element it left is read at once". In all five runs, the outpost's hook delivered a foreground change of that window between the kept read and the repeat. Recommendation: end the outpost's kept read's currency at any foreground change its hook delivers. The design already uses this rule for the outpost's kept chain ("current while its hook has delivered no foreground change since it was read"). It matches NVDA, whose foreground event queues a new focus and so replaces `lastQueuedFocusObject`. With it, the five become full reads, and no repeat in these runs goes unheld. The alternative is to accept the read request's round trip on every such return: its stand-in, a text request, took a median of 0.8 ms, 2.6 ms at the 90th percentile and 62.4 ms at most.
- Focus-now onto a hung window. Evidence: `captures/focus-now-onto-hung-window.txt`. NVDA said nothing when Alt+Tab brought back a window whose thread was stalled for 30 seconds: there was only the key's cancel, and nothing when the stall ended. The design sentence it contradicts: "the focus-now answer onto a hung window is the plain window, as NVDA's (pending its capture)", and step 6's plain window for the focus-now answer. Recommendation: match NVDA and say nothing for a focus-now answer onto a hung window. The design's only reason for the plain window was NVDA, and the capture shows NVDA does not do it.
- A focus whose walk is stuck, which NVDA delays past its stall or never speaks. Evidence: `captures/uia-parent-read-cancelled.txt` and `captures/msaa-stall-after-focus-judged.txt`. With a UIA parent step 4 seconds late, NVDA said the window and "Items list" after about 8 seconds and never said the item. With a 15 second wait, it said the item at 32 to 45 seconds. With an MSAA `accParent` past the hung judgement (8 and 30 seconds per call), NVDA said nothing for the focus at all, and its focus stayed on the previous control. NVDA never substitutes a window for a slow parent. The design publishes the focus with plain windows 500 ms after the event ("Meeting the bound when a call is stuck"). The 2026-10-29 decision makes naming the window by its text a deliberate difference. It does not say that the focus is also spoken where NVDA loses it entirely. Recommendation: keep the design's publication at the bound and record these NVDA timings with the deliberate difference. A focus the user moved to is then heard within 500 ms instead of late or never.
- `GetFocusedElementBuildCache` in the outpost (`docs/design/outpost-focused-element-study.md` recommends replacing it with the read below the checked window). What is proved: when the system focus moves to another application between the outpost's check and the call, the call connects into that application and returns its element. With the order forced, this happened in 20 of 20 rounds, with about 70 provider calls into the other window and its `WM_GETOBJECT` connection request, and in 0 of 20 control rounds ([GetFocusedElement after the focus moves](../getfocusedelement-after-the-focus-moves/readme.md)). What is not proved: that it happens unforced. The scratch build's `focused_read` line counted 12 reads in 30 runs of six scenarios, all of them gap firings in File Explorer and Settings. There were 0 mismatches and 0 failures, with 1.8 to 20.9 ms from the check to the call's return. Twelve reads with no mismatch bound the unforced rate only loosely: at 95 percent confidence, below about one in four. No focus-now answer came in these runs. Recommendation: replace the call, as the study recommends. The mechanism and its cost, a connection into another application, are now proved. The unforced sample is too small to show the race is rare, and the reads happen at gap firings and focus-now answers, which are the moments another outpost is likely to be connecting. If you want an unforced instance first, the probe can run longer, but at 12 reads in 30 runs, finding a rare one would take many batches.

### Repeats at arrival

`tally.py` judges whether Core held a repeat's kept node at the moment the listener or outpost judged the repeat. In a burst, that is before Core has reduced the kept read's own report: in `notepad_editing` run 1, repeat 24567212933130 was judged at ...219490746, and its kept read 24567212933128 was applied at ...219533457, 43 ms later. The design's reader sends the unread repeat after the kept read's report, on the same ordered route. So the recount takes Core's focus at the later of the repeat's judgement and the kept read's application. Where the kept read was itself a repeat that never reached Core, today's outpost having replaced its report, it follows the kept traces back to the first one Core reduced. Every repeat compares equal to its kept element, so the chain names one element. The script was scratch work and is not kept; its rule is the one stated here.

Current repeats Core did not hold, at arrival:

- The repeats batch: UIA 0 of 18; MSAA 5 of 6, all in `second_application_and_verbatim_menu`, one per run.
- The hold batch: MSAA 0 of 5 (the tally's rule gives 2).
- The gap batches: UIA 0 of 26.
- The `focused-read` batch: UIA 0 of 134; MSAA 5 of 6, again one per run of `second_application_and_verbatim_menu`.

Counting all repeats rather than the current ones, which are the only ones a reader would send unread, the repeats batch has 5 of 69. That is still above one in twenty.

In the five returns, Core's focus when the repeat arrived was Verbatim's menu item (runs 3 to 5) or the application's foreground node reported a moment before (runs 1 and 2). The outpost's patch judges currency by the Terms' rule, a focus or foreground event in another top-level window, and the foreground change it handled in between was in the edit's own top-level window.

## The three programs

`programs.rs`, run at ee618c31 on a desktop of its own; outputs beside it.

- `server-side-provider-under-stall.txt`. Unstalled, `UiaHasServerSideProvider` answered true. Under `stall 3000`, it answered true after 3,001 ms in all five runs, returning only when the stall ended; the raw probe answered "Provider" at 3,000 ms four times and timed out once. Under `stall 10000` and `stall 30000`, it answered false after 5,001 to 5,014 ms, before the stall ended, while the raw probe timed out at 3,000 to 3,015 ms. So `UiaHasServerSideProvider` gives up after about 5 seconds and answers no provider.
- `probes-per-new-window.txt`, today's baseline that step 3 changes, with a no-provider verdict kept for 500 ms. In a UIA window, the first focus costs the listener 1 probe and the outpost 0, and the second focus costs 0 and 0. In an MSAA window, the first focus costs the outpost 1 probe and the listener 1, for UIA's proxy focus, which it drops as not UIA; the second costs 0 and 0.
- `provider-description.txt`. `UIA_ProviderDescriptionPropertyId`, cached with the focus event, names "Unidentified Provider (unmanaged:mockapp.exe)" for mockapp's UIA backend and "Microsoft: MSAA Proxy (unmanaged:uiautomationcore.dll)" for its MSAA backend, in three focus events out of three. So the description tells UIA's MSAA proxy apart. The `win32` backend raised no focus that reached the desktop-wide registration on the isolated desktop, by `SetFocus` or by a raised `EVENT_OBJECT_FOCUS`, so its description was not read. Nothing in the design depends on this.

## The captures

All are under `captures/`, taken through `cargo xtask nvda capture` with NVDA alpha-58022,732d1ce2 (the pin), eSpeak NG and default symbol and verbosity levels. The exception is the File Explorer check, which ran Verbatim instead of NVDA. `driver.py` held mockapp's stdin, and the raw logs are under `captures/run/`.

- `uia-parent-read-cancelled.txt`. A focus on `item1` makes six parent navigations on it: two inside mockapp's own raise and four from the client. So the slow step was `slow-parent item1 <ms> 3`. As a baseline, NVDA says "Items list" and "First 1 of 2", and "Mockapp Tree Fixture window" first by Alt+Tab. With 4 seconds, by Alt+Tab, it says "Mockapp Tree Fixture window" and "Items list" at 8,061 ms, and the item never. From within, it says "Items list" at 10,488 ms in two runs, and the item never. With 15 seconds, by Alt+Tab, it says the window and list at 30,070 ms and "First 1 of 2" at 45,078 ms. From within, it says "Items list" and "First 1 of 2" at 32,491 ms. NVDA neither speaks the focus before its ancestors are read nor names a window in place of the slow parent. With mockapp's own raise stalled 15 seconds, NVDA said "unknown" at 5,062 ms, then the window and list at 60 s and the item at 75 s. ## Rerunning

Apply `debug.patch` to a worktree of main at 63b62397 and build it into its own target directory. Then run `batch.ps1` with the scenarios and run counts above, with the desktop free and NVDA not running, and run `python -I tally.py` over the batch folders. The repeats at arrival need the recount described above. `programs.rs` says how to run the programs. Each capture text gives its setup.
