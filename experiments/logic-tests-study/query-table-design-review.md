# Review of the design "Core's query table"

Reviewed on 2026-11-01: the section "Core's query table (a build of its own)" of `docs/design/logic-foundations.md` (working-tree text), against main 39dce0b2. Nothing was built or run; every counterexample below is a sequence read from the code at 39dce0b2.

**Important findings: 4.**

## Verdict on the goal

The goal can be reached. One table owned by Core's reducer, with the number as the wire's request id, removes the second numbering, the shell's judgement of the operation, the cross-numbering lookup for the withdrawal, and the order-of-tries routing. The answer, the wrong outpost's answer, a late answer after supersession, a duplicate, a send that fails, the withdrawal racing the read's answer, and the id ranges all hold as the design states them (see "What holds", below). The design misses one kind of query, the one Core answers from its own memory. It also turns two identity checks into rules nothing enforces: the per-slot `== query_id` match, and the single `awaiting_watch`. As written, step one hangs the idle barrier in a console, and step two changes what is heard in three sequences. All of these can be fixed inside the design without changing its shape.

## Important

### 1. Queries Core answers itself have an entry that never ends, so the idle barrier hangs in a console

- Where: design, "One way to ask" and "One ending" (the invariant names three endings). Code: `crates/verbatim-core/src/reduce.rs` 69 (`terminal_model::intercept` applied to every input's effects); `crates/verbatim-core/src/terminal_model.rs` 1062 to 1103 (`intercept`, `queue`), 1296 to 1315 (`queue_only`), and 1020, 1232 and 1254 (Core answers through `reduce::text_completed` directly); `crates/verbatim-core/src/review_text.rs` 145 to 160 (`command_mark` allocates a number and makes no request at all).
- Defect: a review command's text request on a terminal Core models is taken out of the effects after it was made, kept in `TerminalModel::reviews` and answered by Core from its memory, never by an outpost. Under the design, `ask` has already inserted its entry as `Read`. No `Answered`, outpost end or withdrawal ever ends that entry while the outpost lives, so `outstanding()` never returns to 0.
- Counterexample (step one already): the focus is a console Core reads. The user presses "read current line". `review_text::request` calls `ask`, which inserts Q8 as `Read` on outpost B and returns `Effect::Text` Q8. `intercept` replaces that effect with a fresh read F9 (`review::answers` is true for `TextOp::Read` at the caret, `review.rs` 61 to 76). F9 is answered, and `answer_reviews` calls `text_completed(.., Q8, ..)`, which speaks the line through the slot tries (step one) or the entry's `then` (step two) without removing Q8 from `open`. The shell's idle check then never clears while outpost B lives, and every end-to-end idle round after a console review command waits until it times out. `command_mark` has the same problem in another form: its number has no request effect, so it cannot use `ask -> Effect` at all, and the generated test's invariant "every entry's number came from a request effect" is false for it.
- Also missed: `TerminalModel::reviews` (`Review::Text { query_id }`, `Review::Mark { query_id }`) and `TerminalModel::history` (`HistoryRead::review`) hold query numbers. The "14 fields in 8 holders" leave them out, though both sit in the cited range 192 to 207. A model dropped while a review waits (`forget_text_of`, `reduce.rs` 469; eviction past `MODELS`, `terminal_model.rs` 449) leaves those entries open for good under the design. Today they were never in the shell's table.
- Needed: name a fourth ending, Core's own answer, which removes the entry and runs its `then`. Either `intercept` turns the entry into a kind the idle count does not see, or the queued review keeps no entry until it is sent. A model's drop then ends its waiting reviews' entries. Add a sequence for each, and count `reviews` and `history` among the holders.

### 2. Removing the per-slot identity check makes every unlisted clear a speech change

- Where: design, "Supersession by kind" (the clears named are only `ToFocus`, a focus change's `end_text_activity` and say-all's stop, besides the outpost's end) and "Answers routed by the entry". Code: `crates/verbatim-core/src/reduce.rs` 458 to 470 (`forget_text_of`), `crates/verbatim-core/src/terminal_model.rs` 445 to 450 (`model_or_new` evicts), `crates/verbatim-core/src/terminal.rs` 1188 to 1191 (`focus_moved` resets `request`, `cancel` and `overtaken`).
- Defect: today a cleared slot drops a late answer because the slot no longer holds that number (`take_if(.. == query_id)`, `is_pending`, `TerminalModel::asked`). Under the design, the entry keeps its continuation unless the clear site turns it to `Nothing`. `Then::Terminal`, `Then::SayAll` and `Then::Caret` name no identity that the continuation checks against the current state. So any clear the build misses runs a stale continuation against new state, and the design lists only some of the clears.
- Counterexample A, a caret key: Right Arrow on edit N opens caret entry Q4 (`Then::Caret`). A focus event for N arrives with `earlier: NotFocused` (the user tabbed away and back). `focus_dropped_first` calls `forget_text_of(N)`, which clears `pending_caret` today, and the refocus announces N's line afresh. Q4's answer then comes. Today `take_if` finds no slot and drops it. Under the design Q4 is still `Caret`, and `caret_reply` speaks the key's movement after the focus announcement.
- Counterexample B, a terminal model: Core models terminals T1 to T16 (`MODELS` is 16). A review on T1 asks Fresh Q5 (`Then::TerminalModel { node: T1, ask: Fresh }`). T1's model is evicted, and a new model for T1 is made from its next snapshot. Q5's answer runs `terminal_model::reply`'s fresh branch on the new model, taking an old snapshot as fresh. Today `TerminalModel::asked` refuses it.
- Counterexample C: whether `terminal::focus_moved`'s reset counts as part of `end_text_activity` has to be stated, because `Then::Terminal(Read)` carries no node. If it is left out, console C1's read answered after the focus moved to console C2 is taken into C2's output (`terminal::answered`, `terminal.rs` 470 to 494).
- Needed: list every site that clears or replaces a slot today: `editing.rs` 190, 320 and 433; `reduce.rs` 467, 1216, 1385, 1392, 1412, 1771 and 2593 to 2609; `say_all.rs` 102 and 124; `terminal.rs` 485 to 491, 1168 and 1190; `terminal_model.rs` 449, 922 and 943, and the model drops at `reduce.rs` 469 and in `outpost_ended`. Give each one its table operation, or keep an identity in the continuation (the node for `Terminal`, and the model's own record of what it asked) so that a missed clear drops the answer instead of running it.

### 3. `awaiting_watch` as a per-entry `held_behind` flag can mark two watches, and a stray resume silences a key

- Where: design, `Asked::Watch { held_behind }`, "Answers routed by the entry" (the resume "whatever its then"), and step two's "`awaiting_watch` as `held_behind`". Code: `crates/verbatim-core/src/editing.rs` 200 to 202 (a newer caret key takes over `awaiting_watch`), 320 and 433 (the wait ends by its bound, so `awaiting_watch` becomes `None` while the watch stays open), 382 (`pass_held` sets it to the key's own watch), and 1648 to 1655 (`later_key`).
- Defect: today `awaiting_watch` is one `Option<QueryId>`, so at most one watch resumes the held keys. The design copies it into a flag on each entry, which `asked` describes as what the outpost was asked, a fact fixed at the request. It never says that the flag moves to a newer caret key's watch, or that it is cleared when the held key's bound ends the wait. A watch that keeps a stale flag resumes the keys a second time.
- Counterexample: K1 is passed on at t, and its watch W1 has `held_behind`. K2 is pressed at t+20 and waits. At t+100 `HeldKeyDue` reads K2's caret, and today `awaiting_watch` becomes `None` (433). The read answers, K2 is passed on, and its watch W2 becomes `awaiting_watch` (382). K3 is pressed and waits behind W2 with its bound set. W1's late evidence arrives at t+150. Today W1 is not `awaiting_watch`, so nothing resumes. Under the design W1 still has `held_behind`, so `next_held(state, true)` runs, and `later_key` marks W2's pending caret as having a later key. W2's answer then only keeps the caret current, and K2's movement is never spoken.
- Needed: keep the one designation, either as `HeldKeys::awaiting_watch: Option<QueryId>` checked against the ended entry's number, or as a table rule "at most one entry is held behind", moved at `editing.rs` 200 and cleared at 320 and 433. Give the generated test an invariant for it, and keep the order in which the continuation runs before the resume (`reduce.rs` 560 to 567).

### 4. The generated test cannot reach the design's risks, and its stated start state cannot exist

- Where: design, "Tests", the generated test.
- Defects:
  - The start state "a focused text box with a caret on outpost A and a focused console's terminal on outpost B" is impossible. `SrState` has one `focus`, and the terminal's speech state follows it (`terminal.rs` 1188 to 1191). So with the text box focused, "a terminal key" asks nothing, and the terminal's and terminal model's asks, `intercept`, and Core's own answers are never driven.
  - No input changes the focus, refocuses with `earlier`, or evicts a model, and those are the clears of findings 1 and 2.
  - The reference model, a map from each number to open, watch and outpost, copies the table's own bookkeeping. `BTreeMap::remove` already guarantees what it checks. Nothing checks routing or the rule "an answer with no entry, or with `Nothing`, runs nothing", which is where findings 2 and 3 live.
  - The invariant "`held_nodes` holds no node of an ended entry" fails on the first answered caret key, because the focus holds the same node.
  - `reduce` refuses no input, so nothing is pruned. With about 20 non-answer inputs plus, for each number asked, 2 outposts by 5 outcome kinds, five steps make on the order of 10^8 sequences, not seconds in a debug build.
- Needed: drive the test from two start states, a text box and a console Core reads, and add a focus change, a refocus with `earlier: NotFocused`, and a model eviction. Check after every step that an `Answered` for an absent or `Nothing` entry leaves `SrState` unchanged apart from `queries` (and the one resume), and that at most one entry is held behind. Because step one keeps both the slots and the table, it is the right point for a differential check: step two's routing by `then` must give the same effects as step one's tries on every enumerated sequence. That check proves "nothing heard changes" directly. Group outcomes into Done, Gone and other, and set the depth to fit the measured time.

## Nits

- "Today's order kept exactly": the order of the query numbers is the order the reducer allocated them, not the order they were sent. `intercept` allocates the fresh read (`terminal_model.rs` 1098 to 1103) after effects that follow it in the list, so it sends before those effects but numbers after them. This is harmless at an outpost's end, since those entries are cleared first, but "request order" should read "number order".
- The trace of a continuation at the endings with no reply on the wire (the outpost's end, the withdrawal): today each "gone" is its own input under the asker's trace, which is the trace the shell executed the request under, a key's trace inside `Effect::ForKey`. `ask` is called at sites that have no trace (`caret_key`, `start_held`, `say_all::read_next`, `terminal::request`, `terminal_model::request`). State where `Open::trace_id` comes from, and that `keys::stamp` sees it.
- `held_nodes` from the table: say whether `SupersededCaret`'s node is held. Today it is not (`state.rs` 927 to 965).
- The list says "Eight holders" and gives seven.
- The shell items' one deadline:
  - An entry `Wanted::ready` hands out after its deadline has passed but before `expire` has run is sent and expired at once. Today it gets a fresh 6 s.
  - Expiry sends no `Cancel`, so the walk runs in explorer for up to 5 s for nothing.
  - Drop the send when less than `WALK_DEADLINE` remains, or withdraw the walk at expiry.
  - The change is unrelated to Core's queries and could be its own step.
- Ids from 2^63 exceed `i64::MAX`. Any non-Rust reader of the JSON frames or logs (PowerShell's `ConvertFrom-Json`, .NET `long`) loses them. 2^62 serves the same range test.
- `say_all::read_next` allocates before it checks that say-all exists (`say_all.rs` 133 to 136). With `ask`, that order would insert an entry and send no request.

## What holds

- An answer, the wrong outpost's answer (dropped, the entry kept), a duplicate, and a late answer after supersession, as the design's sequences give them. The idle count of superseded reads matches today's.
- The withdrawal racing the read's answer: both are inputs on the reducer thread. An answer first clears Core's deadline slot. The due time first removes the entry, and the late answer or `NotStarted` is dropped.
- A send that fails: answered `Failed` in the same nested way `send` and `finish` work today (`main.rs` 1850 to 1855). A query asked after its outpost ended fails at `send_to_outpost` (`QueueError::Closed`) and gets its one answer.
- An outpost's end with an activation, a held read and held keys: the clearing then "gone" order keeps today's speech. The activation says "No action". The held read passes its key on, and with the focus dead the next keys pass on at once. The watch with the held keys behind it resumes them. This holds apart from finding 3's flag and the nit on number order.
- The id ranges: Core's numbers start at 0 in the one `SrState` the process has (`main.rs` 2089), and nothing on the outpost side compares or reserves request ids. The standalone outpost's 0 has no Core. There is no collision or wrap.
- The 6 s claim: the bound is now `WALK_DEADLINE` plus 1 s from the dialog's request, against up to 12 s today (`main.rs` 1898 and 1917). The loss after a slow start is stated correctly.
- The counts: `SrState` goes from 27 fields to 23, and the shell's table from 8 askers to 5 and from 4 entry fields to 3, both as stated. The 3 `is_pending` functions are covered.
- The two steps: step one keeps the slots and routes by today's tries, so main stays sound between the steps, apart from finding 1, which breaks step one already.
